## Points to raise with the advisor

1. **The narrow claim needs one more narrowing.** "No item-level strictly proper score on ordinal scales" is not true of the published record (Chen et al., Guerdan et al., Lyu et al.). The abstract says "44 of 49 report none". The intro says order-aware scores are rarer still: 1 of 49.
2. **The skill view makes the linear-head experiment look weaker as evidence about judges.** The CE-trained heads have little skill: at most +0.071 raw, and at most +0.099 calibrated. The large raw differences come from Pearson-trained heads being worse than the base rate. That is a calibration failure, which correlation cannot see, and not a gap in discrimination.
3. **S9's real-judge test is only partly in.** Experiment 46's frontier panel is complete, with 2 significant reversals out of 50 cells. Its local-judge panel is partial, and its API panel has not run. The two reversals use Brier skill on nominal NLI labels, not RPS on ordinal labels; on ordinal DICES the finding is negative raw skill, not a reversal.
