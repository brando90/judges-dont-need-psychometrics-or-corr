## Points to raise with the advisor

1. **The narrow claim needs one more narrowing.** "No item-level strictly proper score on ordinal scales" is not true of the published record (Chen et al., Guerdan et al., Lyu et al.). The abstract says "44 of 49 report none". The intro says order-aware scores are rarer still: 1 of 49.
2. **The skill view makes the linear-head experiment look weaker as evidence about judges.** The CE-trained heads have little skill: at most +0.071 raw, and at most +0.099 calibrated. The large raw differences come from Pearson-trained heads being worse than the base rate. That is a calibration failure, which correlation cannot see, and not a gap in discrimination.
3. **S9's real-judge test is only partly in.** Experiment 46's frontier panel is complete, with 2 significant reversals out of 50 cells. Its local-judge panel is partial, and its API panel has not run. The two reversals use Brier skill on nominal NLI labels, not RPS on ordinal labels; on ordinal DICES the finding is negative raw skill, not a reversal.

## The two warnings of the 7:41 PM Slack message (added 27 Nov 2026, late evening)

The advisor wrote that the abstract repeats phrases from the pushback but contradicts the two warnings that mattered most. Both checks below were run against the emailed abstract and against the 217-word committed version (commit b93cd56 and earlier).

**Warning (1): read Elangovan et al. and Li et al.; binned JSD and MSE to the annotator mean already evaluate a judge against the rating distribution.**

- *Was the abstract wrong?* In substance, yes. The emailed text is not in the repo; the committed abstracts before it framed the gap as "43 report no item-level proper score" and never credited the distributional evaluations, which reads as the claim the advisor objected to. The 217-word version had narrowed this to "44 of 49 sampled judge-validation papers report no item-level strictly proper score", but still introduced binned JSD only as a concession ("Despite distributional comparisons such as ...") and did not credit Li et al. at all.
- *What we did.* Both papers were read in full from the arXiv PDFs. The paragraph for the advisor, with every claim pinned to section, equation and page, is [elangovan_li_summary_27Nov2026.md](elangovan_li_summary_27Nov2026.md). Findings that changed our wording: binned JSD pools all human ratings and all 20 sampled judge labels per bin of items sharing a human median or majority (Elangovan Sec. 3.2, Eqs. 1–2), so it is a distributional comparison at bin level; Li et al. evaluate by MSE between the stated P(B ≻ A) and each item's ten-vote mean (Sec. 5.1), an item-level, Brier-type comparison; their votes are Gemini-2.5-Flash persona annotations, not people (Sec. 4); their Table 1 Brier and ECE are against the binarized majority (App. C.1); Elangovan et al. already show that judge rankings move with the metric (Tables 2–3 captions).
- *New abstract.* It now opens the contribution by crediting both ("Prior work scores judges against it: binned Jensen–Shannon divergence ... and squared error to per-item vote means ...") and claims only what they leave open: item-level strictly proper scores for ordinal or nominal *human* ratings estimable from one rating per item, the sampled-score result, significance-tested reversals on real judges, and recalibration costs. The intro's prior-work sentence and the related-work paragraphs on both papers were rewritten to the same effect; the 44/49 audit stays in the intro and supplement but left the abstract.

**Warning (2): the headline experiment is the CE-vs-RPS comparison called circular, and the 0.0024 calibration number argues against the conclusion.**

- *Was the abstract wrong?* Earlier committed versions headlined it. The 217-word version had already moved it out of the abstract text but still attributed the label-budget number to linear heads ("for linear heads, needed 50–200 held-out ratings per dataset"), and the intro's Evidence item still led with the linear-head skill comparison.
- *Concession, stated plainly.* The advisor is right. Scoring a cross-entropy fit and a correlation fit with a proper rule favors the cross-entropy fit by construction, and after recalibration the largest five-seed mean gaps are 0.0024 normalized RPS (Experiment 41) and about 0.01 skill (Experiment 53), consistent with the heads differing mainly in calibration ($r^2 = 1 - \mathrm{MSE}_{\text{aff}}/\mathrm{Var}$). The abstract concedes it (28 Nov wording, after the Codex QA): "Per-judge recalibration on held-out ratings left no significant reversal, so with in-domain labels correlation-based selection was not contradicted." (The 27 Nov wording "correlation suffices" was stronger than the evidence: point reversals remain.)
- *What the paper now claims instead.* The claim is about validation without in-domain labels, under shift, or with sampled scores. The headline evidence is on real frontier judges, not trained heads: no significant reversal under the preregistered Pearson test, but two Holm-significant Spearman-vs-Brier-skill reversals with NLI coded as ordered (GPT-6 Astra vs GLM 5.3 on SNLI and MNLI), four judges with 80–82% top-1 agreement below the base rate on DICES-350, and a top-1 tie hiding a 0.175 skill gap (Experiment 46). The cost of the recalibration remedy is measured on the same judges: every judge's mean calibrated skill first exceeds the base rate at 100 held-out single ratings per dataset (the smallest tested budget at which it does), and with 25 ratings 21 of 25 judge-set cases end below the uncalibrated judge (new Experiment 56); a map moved to another dataset, rubric or pooled source is significantly worse than an in-domain map in 78 of 82 cases and significantly worse than none in 33, 21 of them between NLI and DICES (Experiment 48).
- *Why a new experiment.* Brando asked for the real-judge study's own 100–200 held-out-ratings figure. Checking the READMEs showed that Experiment 46's "100–200 labels per task" was borrowed from Experiment 47, which measured Experiment 41's linear heads. Rather than quote a linear-head number as a frontier-judge number, Experiment 56 (protocol frozen at commit b487c4b before running, CPU, \$0) measured it on the frozen frontier forecasts. The answer is about 100 for "every judge above the base rate"; the preregistered "within 0.01 of full calibration by 200" failed on four of five sets, which is reported.
- *Where the linear heads went.* Intro Evidence item: one sentence calling the comparison circular and a check only. Supplementary related work: the sentence "This supports the evaluation claim ..." was replaced by an explicit circularity statement.
