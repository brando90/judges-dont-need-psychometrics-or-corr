# Support files for the evidence-led blog post (11-27-2026)

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/judges-dont-need-psychometrics-or-corr/blob/main/docs/notes/blog_support_27Nov2026/README.md>

**TLDR:** Every number in the preview post is recomputed from committed result files by `blog_numbers_check.py` (64 claims, 0 FAIL, two byte-identical runs), and `scan_post_numbers.py` fails if the post contains a number that no passed claim registers.

**What this is.** Support for the preview post `_posts/2026-11-27-bitter-judges-lesson-evidence.md` in the website repository ([brando90/brandomiranda](https://github.com/brando90/brandomiranda)), an evidence-led rewrite of "The Bitter Judges Lesson". Nothing here is a new experiment. The inputs are committed results of experiments 41, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 53, 54 and 55 (and experiment 31 through experiment 41's `verify_blog_numbers.py`), plus exact-fraction arithmetic for the toy example and the square-root identity. The one number that is not in a committed result file, that always answering No agrees with the human majority on 269 of 350 DICES-350 conversations, is recomputed from the public DICES-350 file by `check_always_no_dices350.py` and matches experiment 28's constant forecast.

## Files

1. [`blog_numbers_check.py`](blog_numbers_check.py): the claim checker. Run `python3 -I blog_numbers_check.py` from this folder. It re-derives each quoted number from a committed CSV, JSON or log, or by exact fractions (seed 20261127 for the random-law checks), prints PASS or FAIL per claim, then prints the registry of numeric tokens the post may use, and exits 1 on any FAIL.
2. [`blog_numbers_check_output_run1_27Nov2026.txt`](blog_numbers_check_output_run1_27Nov2026.txt) and [`blog_numbers_check_output_run2_27Nov2026.txt`](blog_numbers_check_output_run2_27Nov2026.txt): two runs of the checker on `main` at the time of writing. `cmp` reports no difference.
3. [`check_always_no_dices350.py`](check_always_no_dices350.py) and [`check_always_no_dices350_output_27Nov2026.txt`](check_always_no_dices350_output_27Nov2026.txt): `python3 -I check_always_no_dices350.py PATH/diverse_safety_adversarial_dialog_350.csv` reads the public DICES-350 file you point it at (nothing is downloaded), counts the unique human modal label per conversation (No on 269, Yes on 79, a tie on 2 of 350) and checks that always answering No therefore agrees on 269/350 = 0.7686, ties counted as wrong. The saved output is from the file `diverse_safety_adversarial_dialog_350.csv` and was identical on a second run.
4. [`scan_post_numbers.py`](scan_post_numbers.py): run `python3 -I scan_post_numbers.py POST.md [CHECK_OUTPUT.txt]`. It extracts every number in the post's prose and requires an exact match with a registered token (rounded, year, 95% and single-digit tokens are listed for review). It also scans for banned patterns (provenance notes, internal-folder links, appendix or sources lists, advisor names, share links) and estimates the read time the website layout shows. It exits 1 on any unregistered token or banned pattern.
5. [`make_blog_figure.py`](make_blog_figure.py) and [`dices350_agreement_vs_skill.png`](dices350_agreement_vs_skill.png): the figure in section 4 of the post. It reads only `experiments/46_*/results/judge_skill_ci.json`: for five frozen frontier judges on DICES-350, top-1 agreement with the human majority rating (higher is better) and normalized ranked probability score (RPS) skill over the cross-fitted human base rate (higher is better; zero is the base rate), with 95% item-clustered bootstrap intervals, B = 4,000, and a dashed line at 0.7686, the top-1 agreement of always answering No. The only computation is the 269/350 constant; every axis has `ax.grid(True)`.

## What is not machine-checked

1. That the per-sample proper risk is unbiased is Lean-checked for finite rating scales (`PlanAudit.plan_estimator_unbiased`, `plan_estimator_unbiased_product`, `instance_unbiased` and, for a number of ratings that depends on the ratings, `adaptive_stopping_biased`, in experiment 35's `PlanClaims.lean`; Lean 4.36.0, Mathlib `fc4f59e5`, standard axioms only). The statement is for a fixed loss table, forecasts fixed before the test ratings and a number of ratings per item fixed in advance; continuous rating scales are not covered and the paper text does not yet cite the Lean file (see [`docs/LEAN_TODO.md`](../../LEAN_TODO.md), row 4). The skill ratio is consistent, not unbiased, and that is not Lean-checked.
2. The squared-correlation identity (one minus the squared error of the best affine refit, over the variance of the rating): exact-fraction numerics on about two thousand random finite laws, not Lean-checked. Claims T6 to T10 build the joint table of judge and human for each law (a judge that samples from the item's rating distribution, a judge that reports the item mean, and an arbitrary per-item judge) and compute every variance, covariance, correlation and refit error from that table, with the refit error summed over the table and the refit line confirmed to be a strict minimum; they do not use the closed forms they are testing. The comparison of the square root of rho with rho for finite joint laws is Lean-checked (`PlanAudit.Model.anti_proper`, experiment 35).
3. What the post says about other papers (Pavlick and Kwiatkowski 2019, Plank 2022, Elangovan et al., Guerdan et al., Wang, Zhang and Choi, Lyu, Do and Pergola, Murphy 1988, Kumar and Hoerling 2000, ChaosNLI, DICES): read from each paper's abstract page or search snippet on 11-27-2026, not by script. The census numbers (experiment 45) describe 40 sources chosen on purpose, not the field.
4. Two groupings in the Experiment 45 report look alike and are not the same rows. By lineage (what the post uses, and what claim P5 of the checker re-derives), graded-score papers lead with a correlation in 12 of 14 and leaderboard-validation papers in 4 of 6. By label type (the report's finding 8 and its closing recommendation, from `tally_output.txt`), the figures are 7 of 8 for graded scores and 4 of 5 for system rankings. Both come from `census.csv`; do not compare them. The report is a frozen record, so this note is the reconciliation.
5. The Experiment 46 README says "H1 is supported". The frozen H1 is about Pearson and is not supported at the frozen B = 4,000; see [`ERRATUM_11-27-2026.md`](../../../experiments/46_real_llm_judge_rank_reversal_corr_vs_proper_scores_start_27Nov2026/ERRATUM_11-27-2026.md).
6. The 77% line in the figure. The script counts the two DICES-350 conversations whose modal label is tied as wrong for always answering No, which gives 269/350 = 76.9%. Half credit for the ties gives 270/350 = 77.1% and counting a tie as correct gives 271/350 = 77.4%, so "about 77%" holds under all three rules.
7. "A count too small to lean on" in section 2 of the post, for the 4 of 25 selecting sources that maximized correlation alone, rests on line 59 of experiment 45's `tally_output.txt`: dropping any one of the nine strict-selection rows moves the correlation-only share between 12.5% and 16.7%, below the pre-declared 15% line in 4 of the 9 cases.
8. Git order. The census protocol was committed alone (`03f93a2`, 11-27-2026 18:07:57 PST, one file) before any census row (`ebd11a4`, 18:23:23). For experiments 44, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50, 53, 55 and 56 the protocol or predictions commit precedes the first results commit; for experiment 41 the predictions were committed (`b811fd9`, 09:49:54) before its results, which first appear in `0dade09` (11:26:18) after two renumberings. Experiment 31, the scaling-law look in section 7 of the post, has no protocol file and the post calls it a first look.
