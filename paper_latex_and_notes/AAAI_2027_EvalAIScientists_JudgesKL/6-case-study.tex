\section{Human-Rating Evidence: Retrospective Theorem Judges and Public Benchmarks}
\label{sec:case}
On 75 retrospective theorem-proving artifacts (25 tasks, 19 families, 195 expert ratings~\citep{companion2026}), cached 3-call discrete histograms were inconclusive across 25 family folds (CE vs.\ Spearman $8.868$ vs.\ $8.098$, $+0.770$ [$-0.895$, $1.317$]; weighted fits~\citep{companionweighted2026} converged at $-0.010409$ [$-0.061911$, $+0.005933$]), and pooled 3-call scalar mixtures lost to constants on CE because empty bins hit $10^{-6}$ ($+0.0405$ [$+0.0237$, $+0.0575$] mixture RPS vs.\ $-0.0317$ [$-0.0638$, $-0.0047$] single-judge across 10,000 splits).

\paragraph{Native-probability open-weight and frontier theorem judges.}
Across the same 75 artifacts (60/75 reference hashes differ from annotation metadata), native six-category probabilities improve mixtures over Pearson across 10 open-weight models (200 15/4 splits: CE $1.63$ vs.\ $3.48$, RPS $+0.0417$ [$+0.0194$, $+0.0666$]; 59 harnesses $+0.0511$ [$+0.0222$, $+0.0833$]) and 15 frontier configurations (7 models, $5\times 5$ splits: $+0.0775$ [$+0.0437$, $+0.1135$], $0.114$ vs.\ $0.191$ RPS). Five-fold LoRA CE beats Pearson LoRA on 4B ($+0.1344$ [$+0.0495$, $+0.2220$]) and 7B ($+0.2088$ [$+0.0957$, $+0.3037$]) judges (a 12-param CE head on Pearson 4B features hits $0.141$ RPS), and 48-weight heads favor CE from 5 families ($\approx 52$ ratings; $+0.0426$ and $+0.0250$), while coarse single-prompt search stays noisy ($-0.0261$ open-weight; $-0.0060$ [$-0.0493$, $+0.0613$] joint frontier, 7/30 uniform fallbacks; obligation auditing lowers single-model RPS from $0.141$--$0.183$ to $0.114$--$0.160$).

\paragraph{Public benchmarks and open-weight LoRA scaling.}
In prompt- and source-disjoint public tests (Exp.~23), discrete CE cleared raw Pearson and uniform from 12 crowd grades on KonIQ ($2{,}015$ test images, $215{,}415$ votes; $24$ after draw sensitivity; $0.1068$ vs.\ $0.3072$ and $0.1332$ RPS at $6{,}144$ ratings despite higher raw Pearson $r=0.685$ vs.\ $0.645$ and $+1.385$ bias), whereas on SummEval ($40$ test articles, $1{,}920$ grades) unseen bins favored raw Pearson until Jeffreys pseudocounts ($\ge 0.01$) reversed the margin; 4-bin train-only proper-loss Pearson calibration matched or beat direct CE on both ($0.0543$ vs.\ $0.2837$ primary [$0.0552$ vs.\ $0.0900$ with $0.5$ pseudocounts]; $0.0922$ vs.\ $0.1068$ on KonIQ).
Top-4-block LoRA fine-tuning ($\text{rank}=8$, matched \texttt{AdamW} grids, $S_{\text{val}}$-RPS selection, disjoint $S_{\text{cal}}$ 7-param CE calibration) on \texttt{nvidia/HelpSteer2}~\citep{wang2024helpsteer2} ($1{,}038$ test responses, $519$ prompts, $3{,}610$ votes; human leave-one-vote-out RPS $0.14567$) yields two patterns across \texttt{Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507} (Exp.~24: $10$ draws, $B_{\text{total}}\le 2{,}048$; Exp.~25: $20$ draws, $B_{\text{total}}\le 8{,}192$) and \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct} ($10$ draws, $B_{\text{total}}\le 2{,}048$). First, \textbf{uncalibrated raw Pearson LoRA loses on mean RPS at every budget} ($20/20$ Qwen3-4B draws for $B_{\text{total}}\ge 512$ and $10/10$ Llama-3.1-8B draws for $B_{\text{total}}\ge 64$, crossed $p=0.0002$: at max budget, $0.15119$ vs.\ $0.17090$ RPS on Qwen3-4B, $+0.01971$ [$+0.01509$, $+0.02432$]; $0.16031$ vs.\ $0.19409$ on Llama-3.1-8B, $+0.03379$ [$+0.01477$, $+0.05280$]). Second, with $S_{\text{cal}}$ CE calibration, Qwen3-4B CE LoRA stays lower on RPS at every budget ($0.15200$ vs.\ $0.15309$, $9/10$ draws, cond.\ sim.\ $p=0.0177$ at $B=2{,}048$ in Exp.~24; $0.15080$ at $B=8{,}192$ in Exp.~25 vs.\ all-dev calibrated base $0.15481$, crossed $p=0.0002$, $20/20$ draws), yet growing $N_{\text{cal}}\to 2{,}048$ closes the calibrated Pearson gap ($+0.00009$ [$-0.00117$, $+0.00136$], $11/20$ RPS and $16/20$ CE/TVD wins); on weaker Llama-3.1-8B (all-dev calibrated base $0.16186$), calibrated RPS differences stay indistinguishable across $B_{\text{total}}=16$--$2{,}048$ ($0.16005$ vs.\ $0.15893$ at $2{,}048$, crossed $p=0.8533$).
