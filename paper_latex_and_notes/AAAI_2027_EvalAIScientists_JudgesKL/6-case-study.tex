\section{Historical Theorem-Judge Evidence}
\label{sec:case}
The companion repository reports a retrospective comparison on 75 artifacts, 25 tasks, 19 families and 195 expert ratings~\citep{companion2026}.
We import only public aggregates and preserve their limitations.
% Provenance: experiment02/results/public_ttj_evidence.json; TTJ 5aef572d30048e6ce574b39d91877c14ab9b3ad7, experiment06/RESULTS.md.
Across five seeded five-fold family partitions (25 outer folds reusing those 75 artifacts), cached-histogram CE selection had held-out CE 8.868 versus 8.098 for Spearman selection.
The difference was $+0.770$ [$-0.895$, $1.317$], using a 95\% family-bootstrap interval that repeats selection.
On the original historical split (15 validation artifacts selecting over 30 test artifacts, 10 tasks and 78 ratings), CE selection had test CE 9.133 versus 8.710 ($+0.423$).
Those historical ratings, test outcomes and candidate prompts had prior exposure, and neither denominator establishes a CE advantage.

A separate fitted-head comparison favored CE fitting by $-0.370$ [$-0.690$, $-0.080$] in test CE, conditional on fitted heads.
However, 215/225 Pearson fits failed to converge, including 24/25 final refits.
This limits any objective-level interpretation of the comparison.
Neither the Wasserstein nor the kernel-distance follow-up resolved an advantage over Spearman selection.
All intervals are descriptive; no confirmatory hypothesis test was specified.

% Provenance: TTJ ea5d84238f6f27bf24ddc0359409921a2e55c459, training_followup_11-08-2026/RESULTS_training.md.
The separately completed weighted-probability and stable-Pearson follow-up resolved the earlier numerical comparator issue~\citep{companionweighted2026}.
All 50 primary fits passed checks and all 900 secondary fits converged.
Its weighted-loss difference was $-0.010409$ [$-0.061911$, $+0.005933$], with both mixtures refitted in 998/1,000 valid family-bootstrap draws.
Two numerical certificate failures were retained without replacement; the interval conditions on successful draws.
It still does not establish an advantage.
Our synthetic experiment neither reruns this condition nor replaces its conclusions.
Fresh human returns were not independently checked from cloud for this report; we do not infer that they are absent.

\paragraph{Matched comparisons on the same ratings.}
A certified matched-budget comparison fitted CE-trained and Pearson-trained mixtures of the same five cached judges across five five-fold task-family partitions.
Its 95\% percentile interval refits both mixtures in 1,000 family-bootstrap draws, conditioning on the 998 draws that passed numerical certificates.
Held-out CE of the CE-trained minus the Pearson-trained mixture was $-0.260$ [$-1.458$, $+0.198$], and both had higher held-out CE than a constant training-histogram forecast (5.49 and 5.75 versus 1.93 nats).
Over 1,000 random splits of 20 training and 5 test tasks, held-out RPS of the Pearson-trained minus the CE-trained mixture was $+0.0056$ [$-0.0059$, $+0.0181$].
In the same order, held-out CE differed by $+0.374$ [$+0.101$, $+0.693$], favoring CE training, and held-out Spearman by $+0.063$ [$+0.052$, $+0.073$], favoring Pearson training.
With 39 weaker open-weight judges added, the CE-trained mixture had lower held-out RPS ($+0.0405$ [$+0.0237$, $+0.0575$]), whereas the single judge chosen by Pearson beat the one chosen by CE ($-0.0317$ [$-0.0638$, $-0.0047$]).
The random-split comparisons use 95\% percentile intervals from 10,000 task-bootstrap draws over 25 tasks, conditional on saved fits; overlapping splits are not independent replications.
These comparisons reuse the same 195 ratings with prior exposure and add no independent human evidence.
With about 156 training ratings per fit, they lie in the range where the synthetic crossover of our learning study depends on where the target's information lies.

Natural-output generalization still requires matched probability forecasts, an explicit rater population, stable comparison procedures and appropriately held-out evidence.
