The companion repository reports retrospective comparisons on 75 artifacts, 25 tasks, 19 families and 195 previously exposed expert ratings~\citep{companion2026}.

Across five five-fold family partitions (25 outer folds) with cached three-call histograms, CE selection had held-out CE 8.868 versus 8.098 for Spearman ($+0.770$ [$-0.895$, $1.317$], 95\% family bootstrap); the original 15/30 split (10 tasks, 78 ratings) gave 9.133 versus 8.710 ($+0.423$).
An earlier fitted-head comparison gave $-0.370$ [$-0.690$, $-0.080$] test CE, but 215/225 Pearson fits (24/25 final refits) failed to converge, and Wasserstein and kernel follow-ups were unresolved.
The weighted follow-up~\citep{companionweighted2026} resolved numerical convergence (50 primary and 900 secondary fits converged), yet its weighted-loss difference remained inconclusive ($-0.010409$ [$-0.061911$, $+0.005933$], 998/1,000 certified draws).

\paragraph{Three-call scalar comparisons.}
A certified five-judge matched-budget comparison over those five five-fold family partitions gave CE-minus-Pearson mixture held-out CE $-0.260$ [$-1.458$, $+0.198$] (998/1,000 certified draws), with both exceeding the training-histogram constant (5.49 and 5.75 versus 1.93 nats) because three discrete votes leave empty bins at the $10^{-6}$ probability floor.
Across 1,000 overlapping 20/5 random task splits (10,000 task-bootstrap draws, conditional on saved fits), Pearson-minus-CE mixture held-out differences were $+0.0056$ [$-0.0059$, $+0.0181$] RPS, $+0.374$ [$+0.101$, $+0.693$] CE, and $+0.063$ [$+0.052$, $+0.073$] Spearman; adding 39 weaker open-weight scalar judges yielded $+0.0405$ [$+0.0237$, $+0.0575$] mixture RPS versus $-0.0317$ [$-0.0638$, $-0.0047$] for single-judge selection.

\paragraph{Native-probability open-weight and frontier judges.}
We evaluated native six-category probability elicitation, prompts, harnesses, and weight updates on the same 75 retrospective artifacts (60/75 on-disk reference hashes differ from annotation metadata, and one rubric template uses historical training summaries).
Across ten open-weight models (200 grouped 15/4 family splits, 95\% family-bootstrap intervals), verbal probability mixtures lower held-out CE from 3.48 to 1.63 nats (constant 1.89) and beat Pearson mixtures by $+0.0417$ [$+0.0194$, $+0.0666$] RPS; across 59 harnesses, CE mixtures reach 1.61 nats (0.133 RPS; RPS mixture 0.131) and beat Pearson mixtures (0.184) by $+0.0511$ [$+0.0222$, $+0.0833$] RPS.
In five-fold family cross-validation, end-to-end LoRA training by CE beats Pearson LoRA on held-out RPS for a 4B model ($+0.1344$ [$+0.0495$, $+0.2220$]) and a 7B coder model ($+0.2088$ [$+0.0957$, $+0.3037$]), while fitting a 12-parameter CE calibration head on Pearson-trained 4B features reaches 0.141 RPS (1.59 nats).
Across 15 native-probability configurations of seven frontier models from two commercial providers ($5\times 5$ family partitions), CE mixtures reach 1.42 nats (constant 1.93) and lower held-out RPS over Pearson mixtures by $+0.0775$ [$+0.0437$, $+0.1135$] (0.114 vs 0.191), while single-judge train selection favors CE (0.125) and RPS (0.136) over Pearson (0.143, or 0.185 pooled with scalar judges).
Softmax 48-weight heads favor CE from five training families ($\approx 52$ ratings) in both studies ($+0.0426$ [$+0.0105$, $+0.0822$] across 120 open-weight splits at 15 families; $+0.0250$ [$+0.0238$, $+0.0262$] conditional split-Monte-Carlo interval across 250 frontier splits).
By contrast, uncalibrated single-prompt and single-harness selection across three-to-five validation families stays noisy ($-0.0261$ [$-0.0643$, $+0.0138$] open-weight meta-prompt search; $+0.0418$ [$-0.0200$, $+0.1813$] validation frontier prompts, $-0.0744$ train-selected, and $-0.0060$ [$-0.0493$, $+0.0613$] joint prompt-by-harness, retaining 7/30 uniform fallbacks), whereas two-step obligation auditing lowers matched single-model RPS from 0.141--0.183 to 0.114--0.160.
Pools differ across models and prompts, leaving zero-exposure human validation open.
