\section{Controlled Conditional Identification}
\label{sec:toy}
Our new experiment uses $p(r\mid t)=\operatorname{Binomial}(5,t)$ for a fixed symmetric grid inside $[0.1,0.9]$.
Define
\begin{equation}
q_\lambda(r\mid t)=(1-\lambda)p(r\mid t)
 +\lambda[(1-t)\delta_0+t\delta_5].
\end{equation}
For $\lambda\in\{0,.25,.5,.75,.95\}$, every forecast has mean $5t$.
Consequently all five receive identical scalar correlations and squared errors on every realized rating dataset.
Their conditional distributions differ whenever $\lambda>0$.
The excess proper losses are strictly positive; Brier and RPS excess losses scale as $\lambda^2$.

% Provenance: experiments/02_conditional_binomial_distribution_identification/input_manifest.json and protocol.json.
We use twelve symmetric parameter-band families, holding out six/three/three families for train/validation/test, with 96/48/48 items respectively.
The nine-candidate pool adds $p(r\mid1-t)$, constant binomial and uniform forecasts, and a Laplace-smoothed train-rating constant.
Only the final constant is fitted; every criterion selects from the same nine candidates using validation ratings.
The reversed conditional preserves each split's aggregate histogram exactly by symmetry.

% Provenance: experiments/02_conditional_binomial_distribution_identification/results/summary.json; full selected metric rows in selection_rows.csv.
We generate 256 independent rating replications, each providing three ratings per item, and compare their one-, two- and three-rating prefixes.
The procedure and source hashes were committed before measurement.
All 768 conditions and 5,376 selections completed without missing selections.
With one rating, CE selects the oracle in 249/256 replications, and Brier and RPS each select it in 245/256.
Their mean exact test CE values are 1.331 [1.329, 1.334] and 1.333 [1.330, 1.336], respectively.
Intervals are 95\% Student-$t$ Monte Carlo intervals across independent rating replications on fixed items; no hypothesis test was specified.
All three proper losses select the oracle in 256/256 two-rating and three-rating replications, which does not prove zero selection error beyond these replications.

Pearson, Spearman, Kendall and nMSE retain all five mixture candidates in every replication.
We enumerate the full tie set with tolerance $10^{-12}$ rather than select by array order.
Their exact test CE spans 1.328--3.348; uniform tie-breaking has expected CE 1.995.
A tie policy that always picks the oracle attains CE 1.328, so superiority is not independent of tie policy.
These are losses of individual selected forecasts, not of an averaged distribution.

% Provenance: experiments/02_conditional_binomial_distribution_identification/results/analytic_controls.json and population_metrics.csv.
The reversed conditional has identical marginal probabilities but conditional KL 2.887 nats.
The five mixtures have zero normalized mean bias, mean 0.5, and normalized individual-rating MAE 0.157.
Their correlations with the population conditional mean are all one, but Pearson correlation with a future individual rating is 0.786.
Population-mean agreement is therefore distinct from agreement with noisy individual ratings.
