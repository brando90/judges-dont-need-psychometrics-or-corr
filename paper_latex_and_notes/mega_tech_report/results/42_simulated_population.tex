\subsection*{Bottom line}

This is a simulated-population testbed, not a certification on human raters. The base measures are frozen marginal counts from VeriBench \texttt{native\_main}, DICES-990, and ChaosNLI. On the polarized subpopulation the oracle ranked probability score (RPS) is \(0.221429\), cross-entropy is \(1.416382\) nats, and total variation distance (TVD) is \(0\). A Pearson binomial forecast has Pearson correlation \(1\), TVD \(0.614594\), smoothed cross-entropy \(2.742210\) nats, and RPS \(0.281693\). At 256 training ratings per item, binomial RPS minus persona cross-entropy RPS is \(0.059517\) \([0.059452, 0.059583]\) (400 replications, Student-\(t\) 95\% interval; positive means persona cross-entropy is better). A lenient point mass has generalizability coefficient \(1\) and RPS \(0.396067\) \([0.395810, 0.396324]\). Maximum \(E\rho^{2}\) selects it in \(400/400\) replications. Lower is better for RPS (0 to 1), cross-entropy (nats), and TVD (0 to 1).

\subsection*{Caveats}
\begin{itemize}
\item The Monte Carlo draws are simulated. They do not certify a judge on human raters.
\item At 4 and at 16 ratings per item the pooled forecast has lower expected RPS than the persona split. The persona forecast is ahead at 64 and at 256.
\item The chaos template places a three-class marginal on bins 0, 2, and 5. The strict persona is a reflection of a lenient histogram.
\item A first moment-panel cut asked for an average RPS gap above 0.01. The exact gap is \(167/23040\). The cut was corrected and the Monte Carlo was re-run. Lean checks the center gap \(9/512\).
\end{itemize}
