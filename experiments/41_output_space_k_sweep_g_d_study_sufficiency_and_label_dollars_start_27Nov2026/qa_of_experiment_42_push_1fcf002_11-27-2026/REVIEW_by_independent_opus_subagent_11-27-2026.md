## 1. Verdict

The arithmetic is right and the attribution is wrong. Every exact number I recomputed on the constructed laws matches, and the Monte Carlo means agree with my own closed-form expectations within Monte Carlo error. But the headline sentence, that the Pearson binomial is about six hundredths of RPS worse than persona cross-entropy, is a comparison between a one-parameter binomial family and a six-class histogram. It is not a comparison between training by correlation and training by a proper loss. No arm in this experiment is trained or selected by correlation. The "Pearson binomial" is the mean-matched binomial, which is also exactly what cross-entropy training of a binomial gives. And a Pearson-scored scalar followed by a cross-entropy calibration map, the matched design of Experiment 41, would reproduce the persona or pooled cross-entropy forecast exactly. Under that matched design the gap is zero or about five thousandths, not six hundredths.

The second headline, that a lenient point mass is selected by the generalizability coefficient in 400 of 400 replications, is true by construction. The pool holds exactly one point mass, and any point mass has coefficient one. That is an illustration of a definition, not a finding, and its replication count, interval and p-value carry no information.

Safe to quote: the exact risks on the constructed polarized, strict and shifted laws; the equal-moment identities (9/512, 9/16, 167/23040, panel TVD 43/144); equal coefficients for the two panels; the crossover where pooled cross-entropy beats the persona split at 4 and 16 ratings and loses from 64; and the honest form of the main point: among forecasts whose Pearson correlation with the bin means is exactly 1, the expected RPS on this constructed law ranges from 0.221429 (the oracle) to 0.281693 (the mean-matched binomial), so correlation cannot tell them apart.

Not safe to quote: any sentence that reads as "a Pearson-trained judge is 0.0595 worse"; "selected by that coefficient in 400/400 replications" as evidence; "Lean checks these identities" for the three-item panel numbers; "four subpopulations are tilts of VeriBench, DICES-990 and ChaosNLI" (DICES-990 is not used in any of the four); the two shifted-mixture TVD values in `results.md:112`; and the p-values (p < 1e-300) as evidence strength.

## 3. Findings (most severe first)

**1. Blocking for any "correlation versus proper loss" reading. The headline gap measures model family, not training objective (design; overstated).**
Evidence: `HOW_TO_READ_RESULTS.md:51` and `benchmark.py:131-139`: the "Pearson binomial" is Binomial(5, mean/5) at the pooled training mean; `run_experiment.py:109-113` and `:78`: the only selection rules are maximum Eρ², validation RPS and maximum Φ; nothing in the code optimizes or selects by Pearson. `results.md:48` nevertheless says "except the correlation and coefficient rules", and no correlation rule exists.
Why it is wrong to attribute: (a) the cross-entropy (maximum-likelihood) fit of a Binomial(5, θ) family is θ = mean/5, so a CE-trained binomial is this same forecast with the same 0.281830; (b) Pearson's argmax set contains the oracle itself (`results.md:56`, Pearson 1, RPS 0.221429), so "the Pearson forecast" is the experimenter's choice of one member of a tied set; (c) a Pearson-scored scalar followed by a per-score cross-entropy calibration map (Experiment 41's matched design) maps six distinct scores to six add-one histograms, which is exactly pooled CE (if fit on all ratings) or persona CE (if fit on the polarized quota); from the reported numbers the matched gaps at 256 would be +0.004823 [0.004774, 0.004873] or 0, against the headline 0.059517. Experiment 41's README:5 already found that raw CE-over-Pearson gaps are mostly calibration; Experiment 42 does not cite Experiment 41 (`results.md:160` cites 34 and 39 only).
Smallest fix: rename the arm "mean-matched binomial"; add two arms, "Pearson scalar plus CE calibration map" (pooled and persona-conditional) and "CE-trained binomial"; rewrite the headline as a non-identifiability statement: "forecasts with Pearson 1 against the bin means span RPS 0.221429 to 0.281693 on this law".

**2. Important. "Selected by that coefficient in 400/400 replications" is true by construction (overstated as a finding).**
Evidence: `run_experiment.py:78` pool has exactly one point mass; `benchmark.py:172-189` gives Eρ² = v_u/(v_u + v_e) with v_e = mean forecast variance, so any point mass with non-constant locations has Eρ² = 1, while the add-one forecasts (full support) and the binomial (positive variance) are always below 1. `summary.json` `selection.max_erho2` is 400/400 at every budget, 4, 16, 64 and 256, not only the 64 and 256 that `results.md:18,124` report ("still selects" implies it could have changed). The Spearman point mass, also Eρ² = 1 and with better RPS (0.359936), was removed from the pool (`HOW_TO_READ_RESULTS.md:57`), which is what makes the pick "the lenient point"; `benchmark.py:53-61` keeps a dead `SELECT_POOL` that still lists it, with the stale comment "tie breaks toward ... a proper-score forecast". `PROTOCOL.md:14` states the mechanism, but `README.md:5`, the `experiments/RESULTS.md` section, the `experiments/README.md` row and `42_simulated_population.tex:9` present it as a replicated result, with an interval [1, 1] and a selection count. The scoping in `results.md:18` ("judge-only Eρ²") is right; the implication holds only for Eρ² computed with v_e equal to the forecast's own variance, that is, a judge whose rating is one draw from its forecast, and `HOW_TO_READ_RESULTS.md:33` never defines v_e.
Smallest fix: say "every point-mass forecast has Eρ² = 1 by definition (v_e = 0), so maximum Eρ² picks whichever point mass the pool contains"; drop the counts, intervals and p-values for this rule; define v_e.
