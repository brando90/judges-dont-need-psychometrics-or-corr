Generalizability coefficients versus cross-entropy on known ordinal distributions (11-25-2026)

Result 1 of 2 · experiment 34 · result date 11-25-2026 20:15
folder 34_g_d_theory_vs_ce_where_reliability_agrees_and_fails_start_25Nov2026

Bottom line
On known synthetic ratings in {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5}, with no held-out items, a binomial-projection learner
and an add-one cross-entropy learner agree when the conditional distribution is binomial. At
256 training ratings per item, expected ranked probability score (RPS) of the projection minus
expected RPS of add-one cross-entropy is −0.000187 [−0.000194, −0.000181] (400 replications,
Student-t 95% interval). Both beat the pooled Laplace forecast by about 0.075 RPS. A lenient
point mass has judge-only generalizability coefficient 1 and, at 16 ratings, expected RPS 0.049183
[0.047743, 0.050622] worse than that constant forecast, with Pearson 0.960708 [0.959250, 0.962167].
A same-mean, same-variance shape change leaves the coefficients unchanged. At 256 ratings the projection remains 0.020508 [0.020476, 0.020541] RPS, 0.689846 [0.689685, 0.690007] smoothed crossentropy nats, and 0.486076 [0.485578, 0.486575] total variation distance (TVD) worse than add-one.
Lower is better for RPS (0 to 1), cross-entropy (nats), and TVD (0 to 1).

At 64 ratings per rater in the six-rater mixture, add-one on all raters has expected RPS 0.139818
[0.139813, 0.139824] against the true marginal 0.139585. The lenient point mass has 0.345844

Caveats
• Items are fixed. Intervals cover new ratings of those items, not a new task or a language-model
judge.
• Judge-only reliability of the point mass is 1 by construction. The empirical result is that this
forecast loses to the constant forecast on RPS, smoothed cross-entropy, and TVD.
• Absolute dependability detects a constant shift only when the human source is in the same
panel. Experiment 04 remains the result on allocating raters versus occasions.
• Smoothed cross-entropy uses the floor 10−6 . Unsmoothed cross-entropy of the point mass is
infinite.

Simulated multi-modal subpopulations versus correlation and
G-theory (11-27-2026)

Result 2 of 2 · experiment 42 · result date 11-27-2026 19:40
folder 42_multimodal_simulated_population_ce_rps_vs_corr_gtheory_start_27Nov2026

Bottom line
This is a simulated-population testbed, not a certification on human raters. The base measures are
frozen marginal counts from VeriBench native_main, DICES-990, and ChaosNLI. On the polarized
subpopulation the oracle ranked probability score (RPS) is 0.221429, cross-entropy is 1.416382 nats,
and total variation distance (TVD) is 0. A Pearson binomial forecast has Pearson correlation 1,
TVD 0.614594, smoothed cross-entropy 2.742210 nats, and RPS 0.281693. At 256 training ratings
per item, binomial RPS minus persona cross-entropy RPS is 0.059517 [0.059452, 0.059583] (400
replications, Student-t 95% interval; positive means persona cross-entropy is better). A lenient
point mass has generalizability coefficient 1 and RPS 0.396067 [0.395810, 0.396324]. Maximum Eρ2
selects it in 400/400 replications. Lower is better for RPS (0 to 1), cross-entropy (nats), and TVD
(0 to 1).

Against the strict persona at 256 ratings, the Pearson binomial has bias +0.801169 [0.798735, 0.803604],
Pearson correlation 0.997604 [0.997440, 0.997768], and TVD 0.493241 [0.493069, 0.493414]. The

Caveats
• The Monte Carlo draws are simulated. They do not certify a judge on human raters.
• At 4 and at 16 ratings per item the pooled forecast has lower expected RPS than the persona
split. The persona forecast is ahead at 64 and at 256.
• The chaos template places a three-class marginal on bins 0, 2, and 5. The strict persona is a
reflection of a lenient histogram.
• A first moment-panel cut asked for an average RPS gap above 0.01. The exact gap is
167/23040. The cut was corrected and the Monte Carlo was re-run. Lean checks the center
gap 9/512.
