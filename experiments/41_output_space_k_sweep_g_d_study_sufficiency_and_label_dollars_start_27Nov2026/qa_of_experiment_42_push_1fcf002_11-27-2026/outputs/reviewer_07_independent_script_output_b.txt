## Bottom line

A Simile-style population is a mixture of named subpopulations, not one average rater. Here the subpopulation a deployment asks for can be named, and the training ratings carry that label.

- **Where the mean is not the law.** On the polarized subpopulation every compared forecast that matches the six bin means has Pearson correlation 1. The Pearson binomial still has TVD 0.614594 and smoothed cross-entropy (CE) 2.742 nats, against the oracle's 1.416 nats. At 256 ratings per item the binomial's expected RPS minus the persona cross-entropy forecast's expected RPS is 0.059517 [0.059452, 0.059583], p < 1e-300, on 400 replications.
- **Where reliability selects the wrong forecast.** The lenient point mass has judge-only Eρ² = 1. Maximum Eρ² selects it in 400/400 replications at 64 ratings and again at 256. Validation RPS, on ratings from the polarized law that the fit never sees, selects it in 0/400 replications at 256 ratings (persona cross-entropy 202, persona RPS 198). The selected forecast's expected RPS minus the lenient point's is −0.173771 [−0.174030, −0.173513].
- **Where a constant shift hides from correlation and not from calibration.** The strict persona's bin means are the polarized means minus 0.8. At 256 ratings the Pearson binomial's bias is +0.801 [0.799, 0.804], its Pearson correlation is 0.9976 [0.9974, 0.9978], its Spearman correlation is 1, and its TVD is 0.4932 [0.4931, 0.4934]. The strict persona's own cross-entropy forecast has bias +0.0156 [0.0119, 0.0193] and TVD 0.043945 [0.043270, 0.044620]. The oracle bias is 0 and the oracle TVD is 0.
- **Where matched moments hide from G-theory.** Two three-item panels share every item mean and every item variance, so both have Eρ² = 1/5. Their average RPS differs by 167/23040 and their TVD is 0.298611. The center pair alone has RPS gap 9/512 and TVD 9/16. Lean 4.36.0 checks these identities with no `sorry`.
- **What this does not say.** It does not certify a judge on held-out human raters. It does not say a persona model wins at every sample size: at 4 and at 16 ratings per item the pooled forecast, which ignores the persona, has lower expected RPS than the persona split, because the split keeps only a quarter of the ratings. By 64 ratings the persona forecast is ahead.

## Which data

Six bins, means 1.6, 2.0, 2.4, 2.8, 3.2, 3.6. Eight items in a bin share the bin's law. Items 0–3 train, 4–5 validation, 6–7 test. No item is in two splits. Simulated rater ids do not cross splits.

Four personas. The strict and lenient personas are exponential tilts of the reflected and the raw VeriBench `native_main` histogram (250 ratings, counts `[0, 7, 1, 15, 42, 185]`, mean 4.588). The polarized persona mixes a tilt of the reflection to mean 0.5 with a tilt of the raw histogram to mean 4.5; the bin mean sets the weight. The chaos persona places the ChaosNLI three-class counts `[109508, 138882, 62910]` on bins 0, 2, and 5, in the order entailment < neutral < contradiction, and tilts that template to the bin mean. Bins 1, 3, and 4 of that template stay empty. That placement is a transport onto the 0–5 rubric, not a claim that those annotators used a 0–5 score. The native three-class side table does not use the transport.

DICES-990 contributes its own three-class marginal, counts `[45678, 3231, 15977]` on No < Unsure < Yes, 64,886 ratings after dropping 7,217 quality-filter rows. It is not embedded into 0–5.

VeriBench `R_v1` at `veribench@d1c47355` returned HTTP 404. The experiment 36 report of an `R_v1` mean 2.97 on 345 ratings is cited only. It is not a histogram in this fit.

A training budget of n ratings per item is a quota of n/4 from each persona. The fit sees train items only. Exact risks use the known law. They are not in-sample scores.

## How each forecast was chosen

Persona cross-entropy and persona RPS are closed forms on the polarized quota: add-one (count + 1) / (n + 6), and the empirical frequency. Add-one is the unique minimizer of cross-entropy plus a Dirichlet(1) penalty. The empirical frequency minimizes empirical normalized RPS. Pooled cross-entropy is add-one on all four quotas. The Pearson binomial matches the pooled mean. The Spearman point and the lenient point are point masses, as defined in the reading guide. Maximum Eρ² and maximum Φ read only the forecast's own means and variances. Validation RPS uses a fresh draw from the polarized law on the validation items. No iterative optimizer is used. Every optimum above is the closed form of a convex empirical risk, except the correlation and coefficient rules, which are not proper scores.

## Limits

The items are a fixed simulated design. The intervals cover new draws from these laws, not a new human population and not a language-model judge. The chaos persona's empty bins are an order-preserving transport of a three-class marginal, not a 0–5 annotation. The strict persona is a reflection of a lenient histogram. Own-source Φ cannot see that a dependable persona is the wrong subpopulation; joint Φ with the polarized law would charge the +0.8 source shift and would still not be a proper score. Experiment 34 remains the result on a lenient point mass inside a binomial panel. Experiment 39 remains the result on spending a rating budget across items and raters. This result is about named subpopulations whose means match and whose shapes do not.
