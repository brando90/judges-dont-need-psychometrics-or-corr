# QA of the sister repository's Experiment 28 (commits df7f6af and efdc8c3), run 11-27-2026

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/judges-dont-need-psychometrics-or-corr/blob/main/experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/qa_of_sister_experiment_28_efdc8c3_11-27-2026/README.md>

**TLDR:** The sister repository's Experiment 28 reproduces. I recomputed its metrics from the raw ratings, re-ran its final evaluation (7,131 numbers compared, no structural mismatch, differences only in bootstrap and rank-correlation fields), and checked the frontier-judge forecast files. The headline that the CE-trained head beats the Pearson-trained head holds raw, but my matched-calibration controls show it is not purely calibration there the way it is in Experiment 41: on the 20-task test set an edge of about 0.01 normalized RPS survives a calibration map for one head (C7), while on 50-task cross-validation it shrinks to 0.003 with an interval that covers zero, and for the other head (G9) the sign flips. For the open-weight LoRA judges the CE-trained advantage falls from 0.0597 to 0.0160 after a calibration map (interval covers zero), and no LoRA judge is distinguishably better than the set-aware constant forecast (the CE-trained one is indistinguishable from it; the RPS- and Pearson-trained ones are worse). These are small, previously opened test tasks, so they support a direction, not a claim.

**As of:** 11-27-2026 (Friday, Pacific time). I audited the pushes myself; this is not an independent-reviewer report. Local CPU, no model calls.

## What was audited

The sister repository `trustworthy-theorem-judges`, Experiment 28 (multi-family distributional judge and open-weight LoRA on the new ratings), at two commits: df7f6af (11-26-2026 17:26 PST, "land held-out and 50-task nested OOF results") and efdc8c3 (11-26-2026 18:20 PST, "reconcile Mega QA Stages 1-3 (Amendment 2 ...)"). The numbers below are those of efdc8c3.

**Data.** The frozen 11-26-2026 bundle: 175 artifacts in 50 tasks and 445 ratings on a 0 to 5 scale (K = 6), two to four ratings per artifact (82 artifacts with two, 91 with three, 2 with four). The "old" set has 75 artifacts and 195 ratings (11.3% fives); the "new" set has 100 artifacts and 250 ratings (74.0% fives). Two test regimes: the `exp27_split` held-out test (20 tasks, 70 artifacts; 30 train and validation tasks; no task overlap with the test) and a five-fold task-grouped cross-validation over all 50 tasks (10 tasks and 35 artifacts per fold, no task in two folds). **Prior exposure:** the 20 test tasks were scored by earlier judges in the sister repository's Experiments 22 and 27, so the test split is opened, not sealed.

**Metric.** Normalized ranked probability score (RPS): the RPS of a forecast against one individual rating, divided by K minus 1, averaged over the ratings of an artifact and then equally over artifacts; range 0 to 1, lower is better. Reference: the set-aware constant forecast scores 0.1088 on the 20-task test and 0.1186 on the 50-task cross-validation; the global constant scores 0.1720 on the test.

**Difference, in words.** "Test normalized RPS of the Pearson-trained head minus test normalized RPS of the CE-trained head on the same artifacts; positive means the CE-trained head is better" (the opposite sign to the lines printed in the saved outputs, which print CE-trained minus Pearson-trained; the saved files are untouched).

**Uncertainty.** Paired task-cluster percentile bootstrap, 10,000 draws, seed 20261126, 95% (resampling whole tasks, not artifacts).

## Checks and results

1. **Metrics recomputed from the raw ratings** (`scripts/indep_metrics.py`, `outputs/indep_metrics.txt`). For the committed prediction files (three frontier-judge lanes, Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B logits, the LoRA predictions and the constants) my RPS, cross-entropy, Brier and total-variation numbers equal the committed ones to the printed digits in every row shown, for example the set-aware constant (0.10881 on the test, 0.11864 on cross-validation), the three frontier lanes (0.10189, 0.0765, 0.07854 raw) and the LoRA CE-trained judge (0.11826 on the test).
2. **Full re-run of the final evaluation** (`outputs/rerun_final_eval_stdout.txt`, `scripts/compare_json.py`, `scripts/compare_by_field.py`, `outputs/rerun_compare.txt`). 7,131 numeric values compared against the committed JSON: no missing or mismatched fields; the largest differences are a bootstrap count of 10 out of 10,000 draws, 0.008 in a Spearman quantity of the constant forecast, 0.007 in a Kendall quantity, 0.002 in one p-value, and at most 0.0006 in every other field (largest: Pearson 0.00053; intraclass correlation 0.00014; cross-entropy 0.00005). So the rerun agrees up to bootstrap and rank-tie noise.
3. **Provenance of the frontier-judge lane files** (`scripts/h4_audit.py`, `outputs/h4_audit.txt`). Three lanes, each with 175 rows, all status ok, one rating-forecast per artifact, probabilities summing to one within 2.2e-16. In the lane labelled `agy_opus`, 10 of the 175 rows carry the model name `claude_opus_cli` instead of the lane's own name. I did not find out why; the committed results do not depend on a per-row model field that I could see, but the lane label and model field disagree on those rows and the results table should say which model produced them.
4. **Matched-calibration control for the frontier-judge heads** (`scripts/matched_calibration_control.py`, `outputs/matched_calibration_ce_map.txt` and `matched_calibration_rps_map.txt`). For each training objective (CE, RPS, Pearson) I fitted the same calibration-map family on cross-fitted out-of-fold predictions of the training tasks only (four inner task-grouped folds), fitted the head on the whole training subset, and applied that head's own map to the held-out tasks. The map family is a one-distribution logit pool plus a linear or quadratic term in the expected rating, fitted by cross-entropy (or, in the second file, RPS), ridge 0.01. Results, Pearson-trained minus CE-trained, positive meaning the CE-trained head is better:
   - **C7 head, 20-task test (70 artifacts):** raw +0.0144 [+0.0056, +0.0258]; after a CE-fitted map +0.0104 [+0.0034, +0.0192]; after an RPS-fitted map +0.0097 [+0.0023, +0.0189]. The interval excludes zero both before and after: an edge of about 0.01 survives calibration.
   - **C7 head, 5-fold cross-validation (50 tasks, 175 artifacts):** raw +0.0051 [-0.0006, +0.0111]; matched +0.0030 [-0.0017, +0.0077] (CE-fitted map) and +0.0029 [-0.0015, +0.0077] (RPS-fitted map). Intervals cover zero.
   - **G9 head, 5-fold cross-validation:** raw +0.0068 [-0.0009, +0.0152]; matched with a CE-fitted map -0.0050 [-0.0117, +0.0000] (the Pearson-trained head is slightly better after calibration); with an RPS-fitted map -0.0016 [-0.0068, +0.0030].
   So in this setting the calibration-only story fits cross-validation but not cleanly the 20-task test, and the sign for G9 depends on the map's objective. The test set is 20 tasks, so the interval resamples 20 clusters.
5. **LoRA judges (Qwen3-4B), symmetric recalibration** (`scripts/lora_symmetric_recal.py`, `outputs/lora_symmetric_recal.txt`). No LoRA validation predictions were committed, so a validation-fitted map is impossible; two diagnostic controls applied identically to all three heads. (a) Cross-validation, 175 artifacts: the map for fold f is fitted on the out-of-fold predictions of the other four folds. Pearson-trained minus CE-trained: raw +0.0597 [+0.0307, +0.0920]; recalibrated +0.0160 [-0.0009, +0.0331]. (b) The 20-task test with a leave-one-test-task-out map (uses test labels, diagnostic only): raw +0.0662 [+0.0257, +0.1152]; recalibrated +0.0068 [-0.0059, +0.0218]. For the RPS-trained LoRA judge against the Pearson-trained one the recalibrated differences are -0.0108 [-0.0340, +0.0100] (cross-validation) and +0.0046 [-0.0064, +0.0182] (test), covering zero. Most of the raw LoRA gap is calibration, and the remainder has an interval that includes zero.
6. **LoRA judges against the set-aware constant forecast** (`outputs/lora_vs_constant.txt`). LoRA judge minus the set-aware constant, normalized RPS, negative meaning the LoRA judge is better: on the 20-task test the CE-trained LoRA judge is +0.0094 [-0.024, +0.046], the RPS-trained +0.0826 [+0.0184, +0.1573] and the Pearson-trained +0.0757 [+0.0133, +0.1496]; on cross-validation, -0.0055 [-0.0306, +0.0158], +0.0203 [-0.0114, +0.0510] and +0.0541 [+0.0142, +0.0964]. **No LoRA judge is distinguishably better than the constant forecast** (the CE-trained one is indistinguishable from it, with point estimates -0.0055 on cross-validation and +0.0094 on the test set, both intervals covering zero); the RPS-trained and Pearson-trained ones are significantly worse on the test, and the Pearson-trained one also on cross-validation. The frontier-judge heads score lower than the constant on the 20-task test, but the paired interval just covers zero (the sister's registered G9: 0.0729 against 0.1088, paired -0.0360 [-0.0746, +0.0011], p = 0.056), which the sister repository's own results state.
7. **Quoted numbers** (`scripts/quote_check.py`, `outputs/quote_check.txt`) print the JSON values behind the numbers quoted in the sister's TLDR and results file; the two headline lines I compared (the registered G9 and the set-aware C7 against the set-aware constant, 0.0729 and 0.0728 against 0.1088, paired -0.0360 each) match the JSON.

## What this means for Experiment 41's conclusion

Experiment 41 finds, for linear heads on hashed text, that almost all of the CE-trained head's raw advantage is calibration. The sister's frontier-judge setting agrees on 5-fold cross-validation and for G9, and disagrees on the 20-task test for C7, where about 0.01 remains. The LoRA result agrees (most of the gap is calibration) but with a final interval that includes zero. These sets have 20 to 50 tasks, so the honest reading is "the calibration explanation is not refuted and not established for stronger judges", which is the limitation already stated in Experiment 41.
