# Audit of Gemini's last suggestion (G/D framing and master prompt)

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/judges-dont-need-psychometrics-or-corr/blob/main/experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/GEMINI_LAST_SUGGESTION_AUDIT_11-27-2026.md>

**TLDR:** The framing is mostly right and mostly already in this repository. Four things need correcting before anyone executes the master prompt: "D-study" means decision study, not decision theory; the prompt states its conclusion (G/D theory "unnecessary", proper losses "dominate") before the experiments, which this repository's rules forbid and which the evidence does not yet support; several data and price anchors are unverified or do not match the repo; and "exact ERM convergence" is a statement about one plug-in estimate, not about a learned judge. Treated as material, not as instructions.

Source: the attachment "last suggestion by gemini" (framing section, coding-agent recommendation, and the five-stage master prompt, 26,638 characters), read in full. Each item is tagged **true**, **true with a caveat**, **wrong**, **unverified**, or **already done**.

## A. The framing

1. **G-study is a scalar random-effects ANOVA that outputs reliability ratios, and so inherits correlation's blind spots (it sees only means and variances, assumes additive effects, and cannot see multimodality or miscalibration).** True. A generalizability coefficient is a function of two moments of the scores, so two judges with different distributions but the same two moments get the same coefficient. Lean: `same_two_moments_different_law`, `covPooled_eq_covF`, `ceiling_pooled_rho` (experiment 41), and Cursor's `g_theory_blindness_witness` (under independent review). The caveat is scope: this is about the standard variance-component analysis of a scalar rating, not about every psychometric method.
2. **"D-study (Decision Theory)".** Wrong name. A D-study is a decision study in generalizability theory (Cronbach, Gleser, Nanda and Rajaratnam, 1972): it projects the variance components to a new design. It is not statistical decision theory. The distinction matters because a reader from decision theory would take the paper's claims about it differently.
3. **The projection formula for dependability, with the rater and residual variances divided by k.** True for the absolute-decision dependability coefficient of a crossed prompt-by-rater design with k raters per prompt; the relative coefficient leaves out the rater main effect. The algebra of "reaches a target reliability exactly when k exceeds a threshold" is Lean `reliability_iff` (`experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/lean/AllocationAndClosedForms.lean`).
4. **Any strictly proper scoring rule gives a distribution-free decomposition: expected score equals expected divergence from the truth plus expected entropy of the truth.** True, with no normality or additivity assumption. Lean for Brier and RPS: `brier_risk_decomposition`, `rps_risk_decomposition` (Cursor's) and `brier_decomposition`. Two caveats: the entropy term is the unobservable entropy of the true conditional, and estimating the divergence from a handful of sampled ratings per prompt is biased upward by a known amount (Lean `plugin_DB_bias`), which is why the test estimator averages the loss over individual ratings.
5. **Replace the D-study with iso-budget curves, power curves and a dollar axis.** Already attempted in experiments 36, 39 and 40 (Cursor's; independent reviews found problems in the headline claims of 39 and 40, see the two `qa_of_experiment_..._11-27-2026` folders) and in 41, with one correction to the story. The raw cross-entropy-versus-Pearson gap is large enough to detect with about 100 test prompts on DICES-990 (power 0.96), but that gap is mostly the level and scale a correlation-trained head cannot set; after per-head calibration the residual is at most 0.0022 normalized RPS and needs many more test items to resolve (`experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/results/sufficiency_and_dollars_tables_l2_validation_chosen.md`, table F).
6. **Compare the D-study's recommended split of items and ratings against the empirical proper-loss optimum.** Already done three times (`experiments/39_proper_score_vs_d_study_budget_allocation_start_26Nov2026/`, Cursor's `experiments/40_g_d_theory_dollar_pareto_crps_start_27Nov2026/tables/allocation.md`, and experiment 41). The common finding is that spreading a fixed budget over more items with fewer ratings each tends to give lower held-out proper-score risk, which is the same algebra as test-side precision; the experiment 39 headline was partly a design artifact (an undeclared tie rule, two constant-rule selectors, and a selector that used far more rating data than the pilot it is described as using), and the experiment 40 allocation paragraph contradicts its own results file, so quote only the experiment 41 version and the items the two reviews verified.
7. **Price the budget at crowd $0.05 to $0.20 per rating and expert $5 to $20, and set it against 1 to 3 researcher-months at $15,000 to $75,000 or more.** Unverified except for one anchor. About $0.05 per crowd rating is derived from POPQUORN's reported $12 per hour and a 13-minute median for 50 annotations; the other prices and the researcher-month band are assumptions, and the sister repository measured 4.0 minutes per rating where a length-scaled assumption gave 37.34.

## B. The master prompt

8. **"Render G-theory and D-theory unnecessary" and "prove that proper CE/RPS evaluation and training dominate correlation" are written as conclusions.** Not acceptable as instructions. This repository's rules say the title and the hypothesis do not establish the outcome, and the evidence is mixed: after each head gets its own calibration map, the correlation-trained and cross-entropy-trained heads are within 0.0022 normalized RPS on all 16 views, and an MSE-trained head ties the cross-entropy one. Reword as hypotheses with decision rules fixed before the run.
9. **"Verify exact ERM convergence E‖q̂_N − p*‖² = (1/N)(1 − ‖p*‖²)."** True for the empirical-frequency estimate of one distribution from N draws (Lean `empirical_l2_rate`); it is not the excess risk of a learned judge, and for chi-squared the corresponding excess is (K − 1)/N, which grows with K (numerics claims C9a to C9f in `experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/scripts/numerics_theorems.py`).
10. **Stage 0 and Stage 1 ask the agent to audit the repository and build Lean and numerical proofs.** Largely already done: experiment 36 has `SanmiPitch.lean`, `proofs_numeric.py` and `PLAN_STATUS.md`, experiment 35 has `BrierCorr.lean`, and experiment 41 adds 23 and 17 theorems. An agent following the prompt literally would redo them.
11. **Dataset facts in Stage 2.** Partly checked. SummEval with three expert and five crowd ratings per item matches the loader (`experiments/36_sanmi_distributional_pitch_anti_proper_corr_start_26Nov2026/data_io.py`), and ChaosNLI's 100 annotations per item match. **Wrong or mismatched:** the prompt says VeriBench FSC `R_v1` has 343 items with about five experts each; the loader records 75 items from 25 tasks and 345 ratings in the five-rater pool, so 343 looks like a mix-up with the rating count. **Unverified:** POPQUORN's rater counts (6.7 and 8.7 per item), DICES's 60 to 112 raters per item, and WMT direct assessment as multi-rater microdata (Cursor checked the Hugging Face copy and it stores a mean and an annotator count only).
12. **The metric list (Krippendorff's alpha, weighted kappa, expected calibration error, spread ratio, entropy, argmax accuracy).** Reasonable and cheap on CPU. Alpha and kappa are not in the code at all today; the others exist in part. Optional.
13. **Experiment 1(b): compare temperature-0 decoding with a calibrated distributional readout of the same judge to show the square-root-of-rho inflation in the wild.** A good test, not run (it needs a model). The synthetic proof and the real heads show the same direction.
14. **Experiment 5: direct single-pass logits against M-sample chain-of-thought rollouts.** The caveat is correct (final-token logits give the rating distribution given one rationale, not the marginal over rationales). Run on a linear head only so far; GPU scripts are ready.
15. **Experiment 3: a two-dimensional scaling law in data budget and model size.** Not run (GPU). Note that where a head has little learnable signal the learning curves are flat, so a smooth law may not be there to fit.
16. **Model and effort recommendation (top model at high effort for Stages 0 to 2, mid-tier at medium for Stages 3 and 4).** Disagree in one respect. In this project the bugs that mattered were statistical-design bugs (an undeclared tie rule, selection on test data, a fixed regularization weight that distorted the comparison), and they sit in the experiment stages. Keep high effort for any step that picks a hyperparameter, a tie rule or a selection criterion, fix the decision rules before the run, and put a max-effort independent review on every push; sweeps and figures can run at medium.

## C. What to take and what to drop

1. **Take:** the three-pillar rules (Lean and numerics for every theorem, unbiased per-sample risk with prompt-level splits and item-clustered intervals, strict provenance) which are already in this repository's `CLAUDE.md`; the entropy strata; the system-level versus instance-level check; the two-moment blindness witness; the reframing of the D-study as an annotation-planning tool.
2. **Drop or fix:** the pre-stated conclusions, "D-study (Decision Theory)", the 343-item figure, and any dollar figure without a source.
