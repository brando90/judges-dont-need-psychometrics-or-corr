# Coverage matrix: Sanmi's 11-25 call, Gemini's two audits, and the 11-27 questions

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/judges-dont-need-psychometrics-or-corr/blob/main/experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/COVERAGE_MATRIX_11-27-2026.md>

**TLDR:** The finite output space (binary, categorical, continuous) was not the only open item. Of the 16 points in the call, 9 are done with a machine check or a real-data run, 6 are partial and 1 is stated in prose only. Of Gemini's six gaps, one is done, two are partial, two are stated in prose only (the symmetric-versus-asymmetric framing and the Bayes-consistency framing, neither yet in the paper or the code) and one needs a GPU (a real chain-of-thought judge and a model-size axis). The evidence is mixed in two places that matter: after each head gets its own calibration map the correlation-trained and cross-entropy-trained heads are within 0.0024 normalized RPS on all 16 views (0.0022 when the penalty is chosen on validation), and a squared-error-trained head ties the cross-entropy one within 0.0004.

Status counts: done 12, partial 9, stated-only 3, not run 3, conceded 0

## How this was built

1. **Sources read in full:** the text of the 11-25 call (Google Doc "SK BMG, we should be doing scaling laws for judges, it's not special in any way", 37,970 characters, plus Brando's two margin comments inside it), Gemini's six-gap audit with its "100% closure" checklist, Gemini's G/D framing with its master prompt, and Brando's 11-27 message.
2. **Status vocabulary (coverage, not verdict):** **done** = tested or proved by a machine-checked or real-data experiment in this repository; **partial** = some of the point is tested; **stated-only** = written in prose here but not tested; **not run** = nothing in the repository tests it.
3. **Verdict** appears only where the evidence can pull against Brando's position: supports, mixed, against, or n/a.
4. `scripts/verify_coverage_paths.py` checks that every backticked repository path below exists and that every cited Lean theorem name occurs in a `.lean` file of the repository. It checks existence, not that the file supports the sentence. Its output is `logs/verify_coverage_paths_11-27-2026.txt`.
5. Items from Cursor's experiments 39 and 40 (`experiments/39_proper_score_vs_d_study_budget_allocation_start_26Nov2026/`, `experiments/40_g_d_theory_dollar_pareto_crps_start_27Nov2026/`) are marked "Cursor's". Both were reviewed independently (`experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/qa_of_experiment_39_push_1cd4fc6_11-27-2026/README.md`, `experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/qa_of_experiment_40_push_a4ccb30_11-27-2026/README.md`); where an item cites something the review did not uphold, the item says so.

## A. The sixteen points of the 11-25 call

1. **Two changes at once (train and evaluation) are hard to sell.** Status: partial. Verdict: n/a. The roadmap separates them (`experiments/36_sanmi_distributional_pitch_anti_proper_corr_start_26Nov2026/SANMI_PITCH.md`, "Two-paper roadmap"), and the evidence is separated the same way: the raw head-to-head score is the evaluation claim, the per-head recalibrated comparison is the training claim (`experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/results/k_sweep_l2_sensitivity_tables.md`). Open: the AAAI-27 paper in `paper_latex_and_notes/AAAI_2027_EvalAIScientists_JudgesKL/` was not re-scoped to this split.

2. **Information versus estimation: can the higher-order moments be estimated at realistic sample sizes?** Status: done. Verdict: mixed. Learning curves in training prompts and in ratings per prompt on seven real sets (`experiments/36_sanmi_distributional_pitch_anti_proper_corr_start_26Nov2026/SANMI_PITCH.md`, "Finite-sample curves and iso-budget"; `experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/results/sufficiency_and_dollars_tables_l2_validation_chosen.md`, tables B and C), test-set power curves (`experiments/36_sanmi_distributional_pitch_anti_proper_corr_start_26Nov2026/tables/eval_power_mdd_dices990.md`) and checked estimation-rate formulas with their scope limits (`experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/scripts/numerics_theorems.py`, claims C9a to C9f). What they say: proper scores separate a cross-entropy-trained head from a Pearson-trained head on raw output with tens to a few hundred test prompts, but most of that raw gap is level and scale, which Pearson cannot identify. After each head gets its own validation-fitted calibration map the gap is at most 0.0022 normalized RPS on all 16 views, so resolving it takes far more test items: 8 to 1,135 depending on the view for a difference of 0.0022 at 80% power (table F of the same sufficiency file). Training-side sufficiency has no single answer: with the penalty chosen on validation the learning curve is still rising at the largest training set on two of the four sets where the head beats the constant forecast.

3. **Why correlation and not accuracy at evaluation?** (Sanmi: "in most cases it should be accuracy", and agreement is for settings with no ground truth.) Status: partial. Verdict: n/a. Lean in `experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/lean/E40_theorems.lean`: `sampled_accuracy_le_max`, `sampled_accuracy_eq_max_iff`, `truthful_sampled_accuracy_optimal_iff`, `accuracy_prefers_overconfidence`, `decision_accuracy_proper`, `decision_accuracy_not_strict`. The hierarchy table is in `experiments/36_sanmi_distributional_pitch_anti_proper_corr_start_26Nov2026/SANMI_PITCH.md` ("Elicitation hierarchy"). Open: no real-data accuracy column (modal or exact-grade) is reported in experiment 36 or 41; adding one needs a decision rule fixed before the run.

4. **Discrete outputs (binary, 1 to 5) are statistically fine; the break is continuous or open-ended output.** Status: done (for finite K; the continuous case is conceded in experiment 36 and untested). Verdict: supports. Lean: `binary_mean_identifies`, `mean_not_identifying`, `brier_binary_local`, `brier_not_local_three`, `same_two_moments_different_law`. Real data: a K-sweep at K = 2, 3 and the native K on seven datasets, 16 views (`experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/scripts/k_sweep_matched_calibration.py`). The existing five-row taxonomy is in `experiments/36_sanmi_distributional_pitch_anti_proper_corr_start_26Nov2026/SANMI_PITCH.md` ("Complete Sanmi Transcript Coverage"). Cursor's experiment 40 adds a labeled synthetic finite-grid sweep to K = 101 (`experiments/40_g_d_theory_dollar_pareto_crps_start_27Nov2026/REPORT.md`), but its K = 2 crossover comes from a bug in the competitor's spread (review finding 6), so it is not cited as evidence. Open: no real continuous-output data, no CRPS-trained head, and K = 10 on real data was not run.

5. **Ordinal distance: "a notion of wrong that depends on distance from correct."** Status: done. Verdict: supports. Normalized RPS is the primary metric in every run here. Lean: `ordinal_same_mean_different_law` (experiment 36); Cursor's `rps_strictly_proper` and `rps_risk_decomposition` in `experiments/40_g_d_theory_dollar_pareto_crps_start_27Nov2026/theory/GTheory.lean` (the review confirmed these two, and two more general scoring-rule theorems, as real proofs with only the standard axioms). The paper's RPS appendix defines the score as a distance between cumulative staircases (`paper_latex_and_notes/AAAI_2027_EvalAIScientists_JudgesKL/8-appendix-rps.tex`). A search of the repository's `.md`, `.tex` and `.txt` files for phrases like "captures ranking" found none.

6. **The ground truth is a distribution over experts or personas; disagreement is a feature.** Status: done (adopted as the protocol's target). Verdict: n/a. Every score is against the held-out multi-annotator ratings with a prompt-level split (`experiments/36_sanmi_distributional_pitch_anti_proper_corr_start_26Nov2026/PROTOCOL.md`). Which expert population defines the target is a modelling choice, not something a run can check.

7. **Multiple samples on both sides; practitioners run a judge once.** Status: partial. Verdict: mixed. The test estimator is the unbiased per-sample proper risk, valid at one rating per prompt (`experiments/36_sanmi_distributional_pitch_anti_proper_corr_start_26Nov2026/metrics.py`, `unbiased_risk`; Lean `plugin_DB_bias` and `one_coord_plugin` in `experiments/36_sanmi_distributional_pitch_anti_proper_corr_start_26Nov2026/SanmiPitch.lean`). A single-pass readout against Monte Carlo rollouts was run on a linear head only (same file, "Single pass versus Monte Carlo rollouts"): few-sample floored histograms score far worse than the single-pass softmax. Open: a real chain-of-thought judge, whose final-token logits give the distribution given one rationale and not the marginal over rationales.

8. **Fix the judge, or sharpen the ground truth?** Status: done. Verdict: n/a. Entropy strata (consensus versus disputed items) are reported for every view (`experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/results/k_sweep_l2_sensitivity_shard0of2.json`, key `strata_at_chosen`), and experiment 36 argues the two are complementary (coverage table, point 5).

9. **"Correlation is really a proxy for squared error, with L2": the MSE plus L2 comment.** Status: done. Verdict: mixed. Refined, not disproved. Identities: Lean `standardized_mse_corr`, `mse_decomposition`, `pearson_affine_invariant` (`experiments/35_brier_vs_corr_math_l2_pearson_lean_start_26Nov2026/BrierCorr.lean`). Experiment: an MSE-trained head beside cross-entropy, Brier, RPS and Pearson heads, with a validation-chosen L2 penalty per head (`experiments/41_output_space_k_sweep_g_d_study_sufficiency_and_label_dollars_start_27Nov2026/scripts/k_sweep_l2_sensitivity.py`). After the same per-head calibration the MSE-trained head is within 0.0004 normalized RPS of the cross-entropy-trained head on all 16 views, which supports Sanmi's point that a regularized squared-error objective is a reasonable training loss. What it does not do is give the level and spread of the distribution by itself, and correlation as an evaluation metric cannot see them at all. An earlier "MSE beats cross-entropy" signal was an artifact of a fixed 0.01 penalty over-shrinking the cross-entropy heads.

10. **"Squared error is effectively Brier, and Brier is proper"; Brando: "Brier and correlation are not the same."** Status: done. Verdict: supports. Lean `binary_brier_eq_two_l2` (two-class Brier is twice the scalar squared error), `brier_strictly_proper`, `binary_witness_corr_tie` (same Pearson correlation, worse Brier) in `experiments/36_sanmi_distributional_pitch_anti_proper_corr_start_26Nov2026/SanmiPitch.lean`; the derivation is `experiments/35_brier_vs_corr_math_l2_pearson_lean_start_26Nov2026/DERIVATION.md`.

11. **"In discrete settings the mean of the indicator equals the distribution."** Status: done. Verdict: supports (Brando's margin comment: "this is false obviously"). True for K = 2 (`binary_mean_identifies`) and for the full one-hot vector; false for a scalar ordinal score with K of 3 or more (`mean_not_identifying`; `ordinal_same_mean_different_law`: consensus on 2 and a 50/50 split on 0 and 4 share the mean 2). Explainer and check: `experiments/35_brier_vs_corr_math_l2_pearson_lean_start_26Nov2026/extended_checks_11-26-2026/discrete_means_EXPLAINER.md` and `experiments/35_brier_vs_corr_math_l2_pearson_lean_start_26Nov2026/extended_checks_11-26-2026/discrete_means_check.py`.

12. **Paired versus unpaired: "unpaired is the one advantage correlation might have."** Status: partial. Verdict: mixed, and the one data point favors Sanmi's hunch. On SummEval at 768 expert grades the raw Pearson-trained fit had a higher mean system-level Spearman correlation across 16 systems than the distribution-calibrated fit, 0.877 against 0.835 (`experiments/23_public_ordinal_judge_ratings_and_label_cost_start_21Nov2026/RESULTS_11-21-2026.md`, "What to evaluate in practice"). ## D. Brando's four questions of 11-27

28. **Is the finite output space the only thing missing?** No. Beyond the output space the open items are: items 22 and 27 (GPU runs), the VeriBench rebuild (item 24; the native_main set is loadable but tiny and already used), a real-data accuracy column with a rule fixed beforehand (item 3), a system-level ranking check on every set (item 12), the paper text itself (items 1 and 20), kappa and alpha baselines (item 19), and measured expert price and time (item 14).

29. **Are G-theory and D-theory needed if a proper loss and enough data are used?** Not to choose or train the judge, and the Lean witness shows their two-moment inputs cannot see the difference a proper score sees. They remain useful for planning annotation, because the item-mean precision they compute is the same algebra as the held-out risk's test-side noise. This is a reframing, not "unnecessary".

30. **Did we address Sanmi's MSE plus L2 comment?** Yes, and the verdict is refined, not disproved (item 9).

31. **Labels against researchers.** Item 14: cheaper under the stated assumptions (training labels at the point where the curves flatten cost \$6 to \$192 at the derived crowd rate, against an assumed \$30,000 for a researcher-month; at the assumed \$5 expert rate they cost up to 0.64 of a researcher-month), but only the crowd rate is derived from a source and the rest are assumptions. Nothing here measured whether researcher-designed judges are mediocre.
