## Question

When the human ratings are real and public, how many of them does it take before fitting a frontier judge's forecast by cross-entropy (CE) beats fitting it by Pearson correlation and beats uniform, on held-out ranked probability score (RPS)? Lower is better for RPS, CE and TVD.

Subsamples: `numpy.random.default_rng(SeedSequence([20261123, k]))`, sampling without replacement (`runners/prepare.py`; k = 0 SNLI, 1 MNLI, 2 alphaNLI, 4 DICES-990). The request bundle (system and user text only, no label, no count) has SHA-256 `6004ef74d41bfd99415900a4da7661361c2278c2da22db49217d2960d138ee64`, 2,350 requests. The label table is a separate file that no judge call reads.

Ordinal coding is an assumption, stated here, not a finding: NLI is read as an agreement scale (contradiction < neutral < entailment), DICES as a harm scale (No < Unsure < Yes). Pearson needs a scalar and RPS needs an order, so both depend on it; **CE and TVD do not**, and are reported next to RPS for that reason. For alphaNLI (two categories) the mean fully identifies the human distribution, so it is a control: Pearson loses no shape information there.

Selection bias, carried into every conclusion: ChaosNLI SNLI and MNLI-m contain only examples whose original five labels were not unanimous (agree_3 subsets), so they over-represent disagreement; alphaNLI is the full dev set. DICES items are adversarial conversations, raters are a demographically balanced pool, and "Unsure" is a rater option, not a category with a known position on the scale.

## Judges (frozen)

Five, as in experiment 26, same system prompt per set for every judge, one call per item, temperature 0, 2,048 output tokens, provider default thinking, no examples, no tools, no retries on content: GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 (closed weights), Kimi K3, GLM 5.3 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (open weights). Route: Stanford AI API Gateway chat completions from a lab host (the plan routes were tried first in experiment 26 and were unavailable then; no extra charge is possible on any plan). Output is `{"probabilities":[...]}` with the category order above. Parsing: finite, within [0,1], length K, sum within 0.01 of one then renormalized; anything else, or a failed call, becomes the uniform forecast and is counted per judge and set. Forecast tables are committed before any label is joined.

## Fitting (identical for both objectives)

Each judge forecast p (K categories) is passed through the same 27-transform family as experiment 21, generalized to K categories: q ∝ p^t · exp(b·u_k + c·u_k²), u_k = (k − (K−1)/2)/((K−1)/2), t in {0.5, 1, 2}, b and c in {−1, 0, 1}, p floored by (1 − K·1e-6)p + 1e-6. Exhaustive search, ties to the transform closest to identity (same ordering as experiment 21). CE fit minimizes mean negative log probability of the individual training ratings. Pearson fit maximizes Pearson correlation between the expected ordinal score of q and the mean training rating of each training item (an all-undefined fit keeps the identity). A third objective, RPS fit, is reported as an extra.

Comparators fitted from the same training ratings: uniform; training-rating histogram (0.5 pseudocount); Pearson fit followed by train-only distribution calibration (training items split into four quantile bins of the Pearson-selected expected score, category frequencies per bin with 0.5 pseudocount; histogram if a bin is empty); the judge's raw forecast (identity, no fitting).

## Design

- Held-out unit: five folds over groups (SNLI: premise image id; every other set: item), group order `SeedSequence([20261123, 100+k])`, fold = position mod 5. Training pool = the other four folds.
- Budget B = total human ratings spent on training. Condition d=1: B items with one rating each, B in {4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256}. Condition d=5: B/5 items with five ratings each, B in {10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320}.
- 200 training draws per fold and condition from `SeedSequence([20261123, 200+k, fold, d])`: one random item order and, per item, one random order of its raters' labels (sampling without replacement from that item's full count vector); budget B takes the first B/d items and first d ratings (nested budgets).
- Test: each held-out item is scored against its full empirical distribution (all 100, 123 or 69 to 76 labels), so test RPS and CE are exact means over individual human ratings. Metrics: RPS (normalized by K−1 thresholds, primary), CE (nats), TVD = ½Σ|h−q|. Not optimized by either objective: top-1 agreement (argmax of q equals the modal human category; ties count as wrong). Secondary, descriptive: Pearson and Spearman between expected score and human mean score, computed within each held-out fold and averaged.
- Intervals: per-item scores averaged over the 200 draws, then 4,000 bootstrap resamples of held-out items per set and judge; simultaneous max-t lower bound across all budgets of a condition at 97.5% one-sided for each of the two primary contrasts (CE-fit vs Pearson-fit, CE-fit vs uniform). The bands condition on the 200 draws and on the subsample of items.
- Break-even N*: the smallest B at which both lower bounds are above zero at B and at every larger budget in that condition (sustained). Also reported, descriptive: first budget with a positive point estimate, and N* against the histogram and against calibrated Pearson.
- Every judge, set and budget is reported, including every cell where the Pearson fit is better or ties.
