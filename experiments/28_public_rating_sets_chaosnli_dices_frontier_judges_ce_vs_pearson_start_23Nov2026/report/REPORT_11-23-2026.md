# Break-even number of human ratings: CE-fit vs Pearson-fit on real public rating sets | QA update 11-26-2026

**As of 11-26-2026:** read the [dated QA corrections and evidence limits](../QA_CORRECTIONS_11-26-2026.md). Historical values and scored tables below are preserved.

As of 11-23-2026. Experiment 28. [Code, forecasts and tables](https://github.com/brando90/judges-dont-need-psychometrics-or-corr/tree/main/experiments/28_public_rating_sets_chaosnli_dices_frontier_judges_ce_vs_pearson_start_23Nov2026)

**Bottom line.** On five public sets of real human ratings (ChaosNLI SNLI and MNLI-m, an alphaNLI control, DICES-350 and DICES-990) and five frontier judges, fitting the judge by cross-entropy (CE) beat fitting it by Pearson correlation and beat the uniform forecast on held-out ranked probability score (RPS) after only 4 to 8 human ratings at one rating per item, or 10 to 20 ratings at five per item, in all 25 set-by-judge cells. At $5 a rating that is under $100 (11-23 text; corrected 11-26-2026: **at most $100**, because 20 ratings × $5 = $100). Scored on RPS, CE and total variation, never on correlation, the Pearson-fit judge was significantly better in 19 of 325 comparison cells, mostly at two training items, where the tie rule keeps the raw judge. Two findings limit the claim. (1) The raw frontier judge is already strong on ChaosNLI: CE-fit beats it, sustained, only from 16 to 32 ratings on MNLI-m (three of five judges), 64 on SNLI (one judge) and 256 on alphaNLI (one judge), and from 8 to 128 on DICES-350 and 16 on DICES-990 (four judges). For the remaining judge-set pairs it never does within 256. (2) Pearson-fit followed by a distribution calibration on the same ratings (a rank-then-calibrate recipe) is worse than CE-fit on SNLI and alphaNLI, level with it on MNLI-m at 256 ratings and better than it on DICES from 64 ratings on (a few judges); so what helps is fitting a distribution with a proper score, not the correlation step. ChaosNLI SNLI and MNLI-m are subsets chosen for human disagreement, so these break-even sizes may not carry to easier items.

**What each metric can and cannot see.** Pearson and Spearman see only the order and linear scale of the expected score: the first moment, up to level and scale (R1). RPS, CE and TVD see the whole forecast distribution against the human distribution (R2). Top-1 agreement sees only the mode. Ordinal coding is an assumption (NLI: contradiction < neutral < entailment; DICES: No < Unsure < Yes); CE and TVD do not depend on it, RPS and Pearson do.

## 1. Derivation

**R1. Pearson sees only the first moment, up to level and scale.** For a forecast $q_x$ over categories with scores $s_k$, the expected score is $m_q(x)=\sum_k s_k q_x(k)$. Pearson correlation of $m_q(X)$ with the human mean $\mu(X)$ satisfies $\operatorname{Corr}(a+b m_q,\mu)=\operatorname{Corr}(m_q,\mu)$ for every $a$ and every $b>0$. Two judges whose expected scores differ by such a map get the same score whatever their spread, skew or calibration. With $K$ categories the map $q\mapsto m_q$ discards $K-2$ degrees of freedom of the forecast, and a fit that maximizes this correlation cannot choose among forecasts with equal $m_q$. Here the fit chooses among 27 transforms $q\propto p^t\exp(bu_k+cu_k^2)$; transforms that change the level or scale of $m_q$ are invisible to the objective, so which one it picks is decided by noise in the training means.

**R2. CE and RPS are proper; they are minimized only by the full human distribution.** For true distribution $h$ and forecast $q$, $\mathrm{CE}(q)-\mathrm{CE}(h)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h\Vert q)\ge0$ with equality only at $q=h$. The ranked probability score $\mathrm{RPS}(q,y)=\frac1{K-1}\sum_{k<K-1}(F_q(k)-\mathbf 1[y\le k])^2$ is the sum of Brier scores of the $K-1$ cumulative events, so its expectation is minimized at $F_q=F_h$, again only at $q=h$. Both therefore reward getting the mean and every higher moment right, and neither is defined through correlation. TVD$=\tfrac12\sum_k|h_k-q_k|$ is a metric on distributions, zero only at $q=h$.

**R3. Non-circular evaluation.** Every test item is scored against its full empirical human distribution (100, 123 or 69 to 76 ratings), so mean test CE and RPS equal means over individual held-out human ratings. Pearson and Spearman are computed for description only. Top-1 agreement is reported because neither objective optimizes it.

**R4. Break-even.** Let $\Delta_B$ be the mean over test items of (comparator metric minus CE-fit metric) at budget $B$ ratings, positive when CE-fit is better, averaged over 200 training draws. The simultaneous lower bound $L_B=\bar\Delta_B-c \widehat{\mathrm{se}}_B$ takes $c$ as the 97.5% quantile of the maximum standardized bootstrap deviation across the budgets of one condition. The break-even $N^\star=\min\{B: L_{B'}>0\ \text{for all tested } B'\ge B\}$, and for the two primary comparators (Pearson-fit and uniform) both bounds must exceed zero. It is the smallest tested budget, not an interpolation.

**R5. The alphaNLI control.** With $K=2$ the mean $m_q=q(1)$ identifies $q$, so R1 removes no shape information. If CE-fit still beats Pearson-fit there, the gap comes from level and scale, which R1 shows Pearson cannot see, and not from higher moments. It does (Table 1).

## 2. Design

See the PDF (Design section): five judges through the Stanford AI API Gateway (GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5, Kimi K3, GLM 5.3, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash), orchestration and analysis by Claude Sonnet 5.5 in Claude Code; 27-transform head fitted by CE, Pearson or RPS; budgets 4 to 256 (one rating per item) and 10 to 320 (five per item); 5 folds, 200 draws, 4,000 bootstraps; protocol and forecasts committed before any label join.

## 4. Caveats

- Ordinal coding is assumed; alphaNLI is a control.
- ChaosNLI SNLI and MNLI-m are agree_3 subsets (disagreement over-represented); DICES items are adversarial.
- Test scores condition on the item subsample and the 200 draws.
- Frozen parsing turned 0 to 7% of each judge's outputs into the uniform forecast; no lenient re-parse was run.
- Break-even against the raw judge compares a fitted head with an unfitted forecast.
- Only head fitting is tested, not weight training.
- Per-rating prices come from other studies and assumptions; no payment was made.
- Catalog gaps: Jigsaw Unintended Bias (login wall), Chatbot Arena file contents (gated), WebGPT beyond a sample, SHP beyond 6 of 18 subreddits, some licences.
