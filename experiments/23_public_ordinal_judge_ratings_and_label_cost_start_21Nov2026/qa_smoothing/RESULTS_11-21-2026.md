# How the result changes with smoothing of archived GPT-4 votes | 11-21-2026

As of 11-21-2026, adding 0.01 of a vote per grade reversed the raw training-objective comparison for SummEval factual consistency. For relevance, the corresponding change in the sustained result occurred between the tested values 0.01 and 0.05. None of the 16 criterion–smoothing combinations produced a sustained improvement over all four references. These are follow-up comparisons on the same public test articles. The sweep includes both previously inspected values, 0 and 0.5. No setting was selected for deployment. [Plan published before the six additional values were fitted](PLAN_11-21-2026.md)

Each budget counts distinct training summaries with one individual human grade per summary. A sustained win means the cross-entropy (CE)-trained judge's held-out ranked probability score (RPS) was lower than each named comparator's, with strictly positive simultaneous lower bands at that budget and every larger tested budget through 768. The other two references were the smoothed training histogram and the Pearson-trained judge followed by four-bin distribution calibration. Neither yielded a sustained CE-trained-judge win as an individual comparator in any of these 16 settings. RPS averages squared error in the four cumulative grade probabilities against an individual 1–5 grade (0–1, lower is better). **Size and direction.** At 768 training grades, test RPS of the raw Pearson-trained judge minus the raw CE-trained judge for consistency changed from −0.14006 [−0.16256, −0.11756] at zero pseudocount to +0.03864 [+0.03175, +0.04554] at 0.01. Absolute CE-trained RPS changed from 0.28367 to 0.12315; the constant training histogram scored 0.07243 in both cases, and calibrated Pearson scored 0.05429 and 0.05477. A change in the raw comparison can arise from both objectives: for consistency at pseudocount 2, the CE-trained judge scored 0.08659 and the raw Pearson-trained judge scored 0.42827. Positive differences favor the CE-trained judge. [Absolute scores for every method and setting](results/scores.csv) · [Paired differences](results/rps_contrasts.csv)

For a summary with 20 valid GPT-4 samples, adding α to each grade replaces the original empirical distribution with a mixture having uniform weight 5α/(20+5α), before the common tiny floor and candidate transformations. At α=0.01, the additional uniform weight is about 0.25%. An otherwise absent grade now starts with probability approximately 0.000499; at α=0 the common uniform floor alone gives 0.0000012 before any tilt. Log loss depends strongly on probability given to grades absent from the sampled model responses. Such grades occurred among 534/1,920 test human ratings for consistency and 854/1,920 for relevance. This mechanism helps explain why probability smoothing can change the selected judge. [Consistency count](../results/jeffreys_consistency/previously_unseen_grades.csv) · [Relevance count](../results/jeffreys_relevance/previously_unseen_grades.csv)

**Test and uncertainty.** All comparisons use 640 summaries from the same 40 held-out articles and one individual training grade per summary, acquired from 60 other articles. Raw CE, Pearson, and RPS selections exhaustively search the same 28 candidates (27 native transformations and uniform) on each of 64 matched schedules, using 6–768 grades. The test score averages over individual grades within summary, then equally over summaries and schedules. Two-sided 97.5% bands use 4,000 paired article-bootstrap replicates and the maximum absolute standardized deviation over all eight budgets and four comparators within each criterion and smoothing setting. They condition on the 64 training schedules. The sweep has no joint multiplicity adjustment across smoothing values or criteria. The absolute-score table also gives individual-grade CE (natural-log units, lower is better), TVD (total variation distance from the three-vote empirical histogram, 0–1, lower is better), and Brier scores. ## Archived generation settings

The G-Eval paper reports GPT-4 temperature 1, top-p 1, and 20 samples. The pinned GitHub runner defaults to model ID gpt-4-0613 and hard-codes temperature 2, top-p 1, a five-token cap, and 20 completions. Its default output name also differs from the released analysis file names. The actual data establish 20 saved response strings per row. Every one of the two archives’ 3,200 rows has the same eight keys for document ID, system ID, document, candidate, reference, scores, prompt, and response strings. The papers and archived code thus disagree on temperature, while the saved records cannot resolve the effective setting or served model snapshot. This sweep changes the treatment of the saved votes without regenerating any GPT-4 responses. [Paper §3.1, pp. 2513–2514](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.153.pdf#page=4) · [Pinned runner lines 9–47](https://github.com/nlpyang/geval/blob/6f84404ce4503d2ba0cde0293c5d402ecf2976a8/gpt4_eval.py#L9-L47) · [Pinned raw consistency and relevance files](https://github.com/nlpyang/geval/tree/6f84404ce4503d2ba0cde0293c5d402ecf2976a8/results)

From this directory run `python3 run.py /tmp/corr-ce-public23`, then `python3 verify.py /tmp/corr-ce-public23`. The sweep checks both published anchor values for both criteria on every selected judge, all four absolute metrics for every method and budget, paired differences, simultaneous and pointwise interval endpoints and adjusted p-values. A second check derives every threshold from the exported intervals and runs the complete original analysis engine at consistency 0.01 and relevance 0.01 and 0.05. The maximum absolute discrepancy in the latter 1,152 numeric comparisons was below 1.7 × 10⁻¹⁴. [Verification receipt](results/verification.csv)
