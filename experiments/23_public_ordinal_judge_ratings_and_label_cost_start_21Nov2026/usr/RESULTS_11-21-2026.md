# Held-out dialogue-response rating distributions | 11-21-2026

As of 11-21-2026, raw cross-entropy (CE) selection beat raw Pearson selection at the largest tested one-grade budget in all three exploratory dialogue tracks. The strongest separation between the two objectives occurred before distribution calibration. With the same calibration applied to both judges, the Pearson-selected score predicted the distribution of held-out human ratings more accurately on ranked probability score (RPS) in all three tracks at those budgets. All three tracks held out 24 whole dialogue contexts, sharing Topical-Chat contexts across its two tracks. RPS is the average squared error of the four cumulative grade probabilities against an individual 1–5 grade (0–1, lower is better). A positive difference favors CE selection. Raw differences use 4,000 paired held-out-context bootstrap samples and 97.5% simultaneous bands across every budget and four comparators within the track and training depth. Matched calibration uses 4,000 paired held-out-context percentile bootstrap samples and pointwise 95% intervals. Both condition on the 64 fixed matched training draws. These exploratory comparisons reuse the same public benchmarks and raters. [USR components](results/topical_usr/contrasts.csv) · [Topical lexical](results/topical_lexical/contrasts.csv) · [Persona lexical](results/persona_lexical/contrasts.csv)

**When more labels helped.** On PersonaChat, raw CE training beat both raw Pearson and uniform on held-out RPS at all tested budgets from 48 grades with one grade per response (48, 96 and 180), or from 96 grades with three per response (96 and 180). Neither Topical-Chat track reached this two-comparator threshold. No track had a tested budget where raw CE training beat all four comparators simultaneously, including the histogram and calibrated Pearson. At six grades on six responses, the training histogram already beat the raw CE-trained judge in all three tracks. [Complete budget verification](results/verification.csv)

**Other distribution metrics.** Cross-entropy is mean negative log probability of the individual human grade (nats, lower is better). Total variation distance (TVD) is half the absolute discrepancy summed over the five bins against the observed three-grade histogram (0–1, lower is better); the sparse histogram includes sampling noise. At 180 training grades in the component track, the held-out CE/TVD values were 1.6118/0.5875 for raw CE selection, 4.7510/0.6563 for raw Pearson selection, and 1.5928/0.5644 for calibrated Pearson. Applying identical calibration to both selected scores gave Pearson-minus-CE differences of −0.03186 nats [−0.05930, −0.00590] and −0.02487 TVD [−0.03660, −0.01297], respectively (pointwise 95% paired context intervals). Score tables also include the individual-RPS-selected judge, fixed reference, and Brier score. Diagnostics give Pearson and rank correlations, agreement, and absolute errors. ## Source and scope

The USR (UnSupervised and Reference-free) release contains three individual, integer Overall ratings per response. The paper describes dialogue researchers as annotators. Context-level assignments identify five distinct raters in Topical-Chat and four in PersonaChat; the study reuses their ratings, without a rater holdout. Each of the 60 contexts has six Topical-Chat or five PersonaChat responses, including original and newly generated human replies. The full corpora contribute 1,080 and 900 Overall grades, respectively. The component-score analysis reuses 900 of the Topical-Chat grades and shares its contexts with that corpus's lexical analysis. The published paper describes additional dimensions rated during the same assignments. The source does not give a per-rating payment or duration. This run required no new annotation, paid calls, or model sampling. [Authors’ annotation release](https://shikib.com/usr) · [Original study, sections 3.3–3.4](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.64.pdf)

The authors' saved context-and-grounding retrieval, grounding-only retrieval, and masked language model scores align to all 300 Topical-Chat responses from four Transformer decoding settings and one newly written human reply per context. The adapter excludes the original ground-truth system from this track because its responses lack scores in the release. The adapter verifies all 300 archived retrieval/response pairs, grounding and context text fragments, masked-model response suffixes, and unique normalized joins among the five scored systems. The feature inputs exclude the combined USR regression, which used human judgments. The lexical tracks retain all responses. Ten normalized PersonaChat reply strings recur across training and test contexts, and zero do in the Topical-Chat tracks. Reuse of a short reply across contexts limits claims about generalization to entirely novel wording. The original metric developers studied these benchmarks. These fits test limited score-to-distribution models for dialogue-response quality. ## Reproduction

Run `python3 run.py` in this directory. It checks eight public source-file digests, keeps raw dialogue in the external cache, and writes aggregates. For each budget and draw, it exhaustively selects a finite feature-based judge (121 candidates with USR components, 145 with lexical features). It uses the same labels, candidate list and deterministic tie order for CE, Pearson and RPS. It fits each four-bin calibration from the same training grades and training-feature quartiles. Test measurements average individual-grade losses within response, then equally over responses and training schedules. Test data never enter model selection or calibration. [Fixed grid and fitting details](../PROTOCOL_11-21-2026.md) · [Dialogue protocol](PROTOCOL_11-21-2026.md)

Run `python3 verify.py` for a second implementation of the finite candidate grid, centered Pearson fit, individual-grade scoring, symmetric calibrators, and held-out-context bootstrap intervals. It verifies every selected head, 17,024 held-out per-method draw scores, 152 raw paired contrasts and their bands, and all 57 matched-calibration metric comparisons and their pointwise intervals. The greatest absolute discrepancy across raw draw RPS scores was below 4.2 × 10⁻¹⁶. [Verification code](verify.py) · [Replay table](results/verification.csv)
