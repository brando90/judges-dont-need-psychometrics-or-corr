# Exploratory dialogue-response judge learning | 11-21-2026

As of 11-21-2026, this design precedes all fits on the USR annotations. We inspected the public release schema, published paper, score files, repeat counts and text joins. These public benchmark grades have been analyzed by the original authors. This extension is separate from Experiment 23's two primary benchmarks. [Annotations](https://shikib.com/usr) · [Original paper](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.64/) · [Archived score files and model code](https://github.com/Shikib/usr/tree/b1e50bb05f11d193cf9fde96e94d189ad84fc57d/examples)

**Target and inputs.** Predict the distribution of the three individual human Overall ratings on the original ordered 1–5 scale for each candidate response. Fit three tracks separately. (1) Topical-Chat, using the three published USR component scores for the 300 responses with archived model outputs, comprising four transformer decoding choices and the newly written human response per context. The released score data omit the original ground-truth reply. Verify each three-way join against the archived response input and context grouping. Exclude the composite regression, which used human judgments. (2) All 360 Topical-Chat responses, including original ground truth, using four label-free lexical features. (3) All 300 PersonaChat responses, using the same four features. The features are log(1 + response word count), distinct response words divided by total words, fraction of response word tokens found in the context, and fraction found in the fact/persona text. Tokenize with lowercase alphanumeric words; denominators for an empty response are one. No model IDs, rater IDs, other human scores, reference answers, or raw dialogue in outputs.

**Sampling and search.** Both corpora have 60 unique full contexts. Within each corpus, hold out the first 24 contexts by SHA-256 of the string formed by the prefix `corrce23-usr|` and the entire original context. Keep every candidate and grade for a context on one side. Both Topical-Chat tracks have identical held-out contexts. The 36 training contexts have 180 or 216 responses. Use nested cumulative budgets 6, 12, 24, 48, 96, 180 and, where available, 216 individual ratings, separately at one or three grades per sampled response. Reuse Experiment 23's 64 prespecified random acquisition schedules on the training responses and individual grades sampled without replacement. Select from the same feature-based 5-grade Gaussian library with uniform floor, training-only standardization, two training-only principal components, tie ordering, shared input and exhaustive grid enumeration defined in the parent protocol. The three objectives are individual-grade cross-entropy (CE), correlation between expected and observed grades (Pearson), and individual-grade ranked probability score (RPS). Also compare the fixed reference, fixed uniform, smoothed training grade histogram, and four-bin distribution calibration of the selected score. For a symmetric comparison, apply the same four-bin calibration to the CE-selected score and the Pearson-selected score at one grade per response, using the same training labels, cuts and shrinkage. No paid inference.

**Held-out comparison.** Average individual-grade normalized RPS and CE within response, then weight responses equally; report total variation distance (TVD) to each three-vote histogram and rank/agreement diagnostics. Report all budgets and raw baselines. Use the parent procedure's 4,000 held-out-context bootstrap replicates and 97.5% max-standardized bands across all budgets and the four named raw comparators within each separate track and depth. Report first sustained budget improving on Pearson plus uniform, and additionally on histogram plus calibrated Pearson. Provide descriptive 95% paired context-bootstrap intervals for symmetrically calibrated Pearson minus calibrated CE on test RPS, CE and TVD. Report uncertainty conditional on the 64 acquisitions and available annotators. The three dependent exploratory tracks have no joint familywise confirmatory claim. Prediction: component scores may improve raw fitted distributions more than lexical features, but neither a positive CE–Pearson difference nor a distributional benefit over the training histogram is guaranteed. No outcome controls publication.
