# Which public human judgments can we replay? | 11-21-2026

As of 11-21-2026. In experiment `23_public_ordinal_judge_ratings_and_label_cost_start_21Nov2026`, SummEval and KonIQ-10k supply genuinely ordered human ratings, multiple responses per item and an identifiable held-out unit. HelpSteer2, the USR dialogue benchmark and Stanford's raw sentiment judgments also meet those conditions. These choices support different claims about a human judge. A photographer judging image quality, an expert grading a summary and a chat user choosing between two answers perform different tasks. [Experiment 23 design](README.md) · [Experiment 22 candidate shortlist](../22_non_circular_evaluation_protocol_and_public_rating_sets_start_21Nov2026/EVAL_PROTOCOL_11-21-2026.md)

An individual rating list or integer count in each category is sufficient to replay a finite annotation budget by sampling **without replacement within each item**. Counts lose worker identity, order and time. An average alone can support a mean-rating study. An A/B choice can support a probability forecast for that comparison. Neither supplies an ordinal distribution for each individual answer. An annotation replay estimates performance on the released population of items and raters. A deployment cost or a future model's generalization requires separate evidence.

For an ordered rating, score a forecast against each held-out judgment with a proper loss such as cross-entropy (CE) or normalized ranked probability score (RPS), then average within item and across items. Top-category accuracy answers how often the forecast’s modal category matches the observed rating. Rank agreement answers an ordering question. Testing a training objective on genuinely held-out items is legitimate: a strong test correlation shows generalization on association but does not establish agreement on probability, level or spread. A low top-category error likewise leaves the probabilities assigned to the other ratings unresolved. [Study evaluation protocol](PROTOCOL_11-21-2026.md)

## Has the matched CE-versus-Pearson budget comparison been done?

Here a direct comparison means that one judge is **fitted by maximizing Pearson correlation**, another is **fitted by minimizing CE** on identical ratings and the same candidate space, both forecast item-level probability distributions, and each is scored on the same held-out individual ratings with a proper loss across multiple total training-rating budgets. These conditions distinguish the proposed comparison from reporting Pearson as a test metric, fitting to hard versus soft labels, or varying annotators per fixed item.

| Closest primary evidence | What the authors actually compare | What remains open for this experiment |
|---|---|---|
| [Zhang, Gong and Choi, *Capturing Label Distribution*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06859), §3, Tables 1–2 | Compares buying one label on more examples against buying multiple labels for some examples under a fixed training budget. Evaluates held-out label-distribution divergence and majority accuracy. With the smallest studied budget, distribution gains can coincide with an accuracy loss. | Direct precedent for allocating scarce labels and for metric-dependent conclusions. The study uses cross-entropy training on label targets and post-hoc smoothing. There is no maximized-Pearson arm. |
| [Kohli, *Metric-Dependent Annotation Saturation*](https://aclanthology.org/2026.pandora-1.4/), §§3–5 | Subsamples labels per ChaosNLI item, compares soft-label training with hard targets, smoothing and calibration. In those three-class runs, Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence gains mostly saturated around 10 labels per item; correlation between predicted and human **entropy** stabilized around 20–50. | Different estimand and budget unit. Neither threshold gives the total number of human grades required for CE training to beat Pearson training. |
| [Zhou et al., *Distributed NLI*](https://aclanthology.org/2022.findings-acl.79/), Tables 3–4 · [Singh et al., *Distributions In, Distributions Out*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14117), methods/results | The former evaluates held-out distribution divergence and ablates label density in recalibration. Holding out a distribution and reporting correlation do not create a correlation-trained comparison. |
| [Hosu et al., *KonIQ-10k*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06180), §5.2, Fig. 8 and §6.2.3 · [Talebi and Milanfar, *NIMA*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.05424), §II-A Eq. 1 and §III | KonIQ varies training-set size and compares absolute, squared, Huber, CE and earth mover’s losses; Fig. 8 evaluates them by held-out rank correlation. | [Xu et al., *Boosting Image Quality Assessment*](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Xu_Boosting_Image_Quality_Assessment_through_Efficient_Transformer_Adaptation_with_Local_CVPR_2024_paper.html), Eq. 5 and experiment tables | The image-quality model directly minimizes one-half of one minus the batch Pearson correlation between predicted and human mean scores. This was a targeted primary-source search on 11-21-2026 across scholarly and web search, the ACL Anthology, arXiv, authors’ repositories and dataset cards. Searches combined “Pearson/correlation loss,” “cross-entropy,” “human score/label distribution,” “annotation budget,” “held-out KL/RPS,” and domain terms for dialogue, translation, summarization, natural language inference, image quality and speech naturalness. No direct match to all four conditions was identified **in these sources**. Existing work already demonstrates that human-distribution training, annotation-budget allocation, and metric-dependent saturation can matter. A general priority claim would require a systematic review.

## What past annotation charges can tell us

- SummEval reports a target average of **US$12 per hour for its crowd workers**. The paper does not give an expert hourly rate or a verified price per four-attribute expert judgment. Article reading, repeated experts and the revision round affect the cost of a new collection. [Paper §4.3](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.24/)
- In a separate CNN/DailyMail summarization study, Chaganty, Mussmann and Liang report **about US$0.15 per summary** for worker judgments covering fluency, redundancy and overall quality, and **about US$0.40 per summary** for post-editing. These give observed prices for two different operations on summaries. [*The Price of Debiasing Automatic Metrics*, §4](https://www-nlp.stanford.edu/pubs/chaganty2018price.pdf)
- Shaib et al. hired seven professional proofreaders. Each received **US$325** for reading 100 articles and ranking three summaries for each article, and reported **10–13 hours** for the job. That is **US$3.25 per article-level ranking assignment per proofreader**. Their annotation-budget experiment concerns how many articles are needed to distinguish summarization systems. A joint ranking of three summaries does not recover three independent factual-consistency grades. [*How Much Annotation Is Needed to Compare Summarization Models?*, Appendix F](https://aclanthology.org/2024.hcinlp-1.5.pdf)
- For another factual-consistency project, the SummEdits authors report approximately **US$3,000 and 150 hours in total** for annotation. That is a project-level cost for edited summaries and consistency labels. Together with the two studies above, it estimates the scale of comparable work; none supplies the missing expert per-rating charge for SummEval. [SummEdits paper and costing](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.600/)
- ChaosNLI reports roughly a month of collection, **10–20 seconds per example**, and up to **US$0.50 on average per batch of ten examples**. CIFAR-10H reports **US$1.50 per assignment**, 200 regular image categorizations plus attention checks, and **15 minutes average completion time**. These are historical study conditions for short tasks. [ChaosNLI §3.4](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.734/) · [CIFAR-10H methods](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/html/Peterson_Human_Uncertainty_Makes_Classification_More_Robust_ICCV_2019_paper.html)
- For DICES, the recorded time covers a conversation and its safety questionnaire. For HelpSteer2, the card documents trained contractors, variable effort and pay factors. A dollar amount per retained rating could not be established from these public sources. Existing downloadable labels save the marginal expense of commissioning these labels again. Their acquisition, expert recruitment and verification had real costs. [DICES methodology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11247) · [HelpSteer2 collection and ethics statement](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/HelpSteer2)
