As of 11-21-2026. Direct selection by cross-entropy (CE) beat direct selection by Pearson correlation on mean held-out ranked probability score (RPS) at all eight budgets from 6 to 768 training ratings. **No tested budget cleared the prespecified threshold for beating both direct Pearson selection and a uniform forecast.** At 768 training ratings, a training histogram and a calibrated Pearson-selected score each beat direct CE in every one of the 64 draws. ## What the ratings measure

Participants submitted 1–5 stars through the MovieLens recommender service in 1997–1998. Each prediction is a distribution for a random observed reviewer of a held-out movie. The 100K release retains active users who rated at least 20 movies and supplied complete demographic information. The target weights every held-out movie equally, then every retained rating of that movie equally. It describes observed reviews, conditional on choosing to review. The movie descriptors are release year and 19 genre flags. The task involves neither rater-specific predictions nor an evaluation of recommendation quality. The partition holds out movie titles but does not hold out a later time period. [GroupLens release, license and documentation](https://files.grouplens.org/datasets/movielens/ml-100k-README.txt) · [Harper and Konstan, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2827872)

Among the original 1,682 catalog IDs and 100,000 ratings, grouping identical normalized titles produced 1,664 movies. Keeping just the most recent review per user/grouped movie removed 307 duplicates. The primary sample has 1,264 training movies with 75,638 votes and 400 held-out movies with 24,055 votes. Test movies have 1–583 ratings each (median 29). All 943 accounts appear on training movies and 942 also appear on test movies. No test votes were used for fitting. Release year was imputed for one movie. This study bought no reviews. The release gives neither a per-review wage nor an empirical cost for prospectively collecting these reviews, so each budget counts actual historical ratings exposed in one fit. The 64 draws reuse the archive. [Primary coverage](results/all_users/coverage.json)

## Sample size and calibration

The table gives **held-out normalized individual-rating ranked probability score (RPS)**, the mean squared probability error across the four cumulative star thresholds, averaged over votes within movie, 400 movies, and 64 paired training draws. RPS ranges from 0 to 1, lower is better. Each training rating comes from a distinct movie. Direct selections exhaustively searched the same 529 fixed distribution forecasts, using training CE or Pearson between forecast mean and human grade. The calibrator estimates star probabilities in training-score quartile bins with eight prior pseudo-observations. The fixed calibrated reference uses the prespecified increasing release-year score. At 768 ratings, subtracting the direct CE-selected judge's test RPS from each comparator gives **+.11604** for direct Pearson (95% simultaneous band [.10690, .12517]), **+.00023** for uniform [−.00091, .00137], **−.00359** for the training histogram [−.00534, −.00183], and **−.00419** for calibrated Pearson [−.00728, −.00109]. The bands cover 32 comparisons across four comparators and eight budgets using 4,000 paired movie resamples. The direct CE search selected the uniform candidate in 45 of 64 maximum-budget draws. The comparison with uniform was tied in those draws and favored CE in the other 19. The separate histogram adapts all five star probabilities to the sample. The maximum-budget **cross-entropy** (mean negative natural log assigned to an observed rating, nats, lower is better) was 1.61032 for direct CE, 3.97783 for direct Pearson, 1.60944 for uniform, 1.55586 for the training histogram and 1.55436 for calibrated Pearson. The respective **total variation distances** (TVD, half the sum of absolute probability errors against each test movie's empirical histogram, range 0–1, lower is better) were .35442, .56136, .36522, .32010 and .31529. TVD inherits sampling noise when a film has few ratings. [Absolute test scores](results/all_users/scores.csv)

## Separating users as well as movies

The sensitivity holds out both 400 movie titles and a hash-selected pool of 472 users. The other 471 users reviewed 1,147 eligible training movies (36,888 votes). The 472 held-out users reviewed 395 of the 400 assigned test movies (12,353 votes, 1–301 per movie, median 16). There is no shared user. The split changes the observable movie sample, rating histograms, and the training draws. Its results describe these retained users and movies. [Coverage](results/disjoint_users/coverage.json)

At 768 training ratings, the 395-movie test RPS was .18183 for direct CE, .28793 for direct Pearson, .18108 for uniform, .18225 for the training histogram, and .18213 for calibrated Pearson. The pointwise 95% intervals for comparator minus direct CE were +.10610 [.10205, .11015] for direct Pearson, −.00075 [−.00284, .00121] for uniform, +.00042 [−.00209, .00303] for the histogram and +.00030 [−.00282, .00350] for calibrated Pearson. The direct-Pearson difference over all 400 assigned titles could range from +.09228 to +.11728 if each missing title's difference were arbitrary in [−1,1]. The analogous bounds for calibrated Pearson minus direct CE are [−.01220, .01280]. These are missing-outcome bounds on the observed point estimates, separate from sampling uncertainty. The fixed calibrated reference reached .18046 RPS. [All absolute scores](results/disjoint_users/scores.csv) · [Pointwise intervals and missing-title bounds](results/disjoint_users/contrasts.csv) · [Matched calibration](results/disjoint_users/matched.csv)

All movie bootstrap intervals condition on the 64 realized training draws and the available user ratings. Correlation between movies reviewed by the same person, selection into reviewing, and sampling of new users remain outside those intervals. The disjoint-user check removes reviewer overlap across train and test, while the same reviewer can still rate multiple test movies. Neither panel establishes a label threshold for individual customers, contemporary titles, or a personalized recommendation service.

## Deterministic verification

A second implementation replayed budgets 6 and 768 for all 64 draws in each panel. It independently obtained identical selection frequencies for all three selectors and all 27 held-out metric values per endpoint/panel (nine methods × three metrics, tolerance 2 × 10⁻¹²). It checked the ranked probability score both as an ordinal energy score and by directly expanding voluntary votes.
