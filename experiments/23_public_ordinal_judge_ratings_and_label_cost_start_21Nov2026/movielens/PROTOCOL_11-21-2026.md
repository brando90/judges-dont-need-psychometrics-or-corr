# MovieLens field replication protocol | 11-21-2026

As of 11-21-2026, this exploratory extension follows the two existing benchmark analyses. It was specified after inspecting GroupLens release documentation and film metadata, before reading individual rating values or fitting a MovieLens judge.

**Question and population.** Predict the 1–5 stars awarded to a previously unseen movie by a randomly selected one of its observed participating MovieLens users. Use the original 100K public research release, collected on the MovieLens site from 09-19-1997 to 04-22-1998. It contains 100,000 submitted ratings from 943 retained accounts on 1,682 catalog IDs. Only accounts with at least 20 ratings and complete demographics were retained by the publisher. GroupLens describes a recommender service. These historical ratings are self-selected submissions, with no documented per-rating wage. Our targets are reported ratings conditional on having rated the movie. We do not predict individual users' preferences or demand for unrated movies. [Release and terms](https://files.grouplens.org/datasets/movielens/ml-100k-README.txt) · [Harper and Konstan, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2827872)

**Hold out movies.** Use the metadata-only key formed by Unicode NFKC normalization, case folding, and the space-separated word tokens of the title field (which normally includes release year). This merges 18 pairs of repeated catalog entries into 1,664 distinct title keys. Take the first 400 title keys ranked by SHA-256 of `corrce23-movie|` plus key for testing and the other 1,264 for training. Every catalog ID of a grouped title belongs to the same split. If a user rated multiple IDs for a grouped movie, retain the latest timestamp, using smaller catalog ID for a timestamp tie. No minimum rating count beyond a single observable vote. Report retained vote counts and their range. All movies are equally weighted in testing, irrespective of popularity.

**Two user panels.** The primary panel includes all retained users. Count the overlap of train and test contributors. The sensitivity panel fixes a disjoint hash split of users: sort the 943 numeric IDs by the SHA-256 digest of `corrce23-user|` plus decimal ID. Use the first 472 users' ratings on test movies and the remaining 471 users' ratings on train movies. Analyze only movies with an observable vote from the assigned pool. Report losses and intervals for the covered movies, and full-400-movie worst-case bounds on paired RPS differences for uncovered movies (each difference lies in [−1,1]). Never add a grade to represent a missing rating.

**Forecasts and training.** Predictors are the 19 original genre flags and release year. Take the union of genre flags among grouped listings; use the common known release year, training-movie median for a missing year. Use no user identity, title, post-release votes, rating counts, demographic data, timestamp or other label-derived predictor. Reuse the parent experiment’s feature-based candidate generator with year first, then the release's genre order. Standardization and the two principal components use only unlabeled training items in that panel. Search its fixed 529 candidates (22 score features × 24 ordered distribution heads plus uniform). Fit by exact mean individual-rating CE or Pearson between predicted mean and observed grade, with the frozen candidate order and tolerance. A fully undefined Pearson fit selects the first head. Compare to uniform, the half-count-smoothed training histogram, and the calibrated Pearson-selected score. Also report the same calibrated raw-CE-selected score and LOO-calibration-CE-selected score from the [matched calibration sensitivity](../matched_calibration/PROTOCOL_11-21-2026.md).

Use one rating per distinct training movie. For each panel and each of 64 draws use the shared NumPy SeedSequence([20261121,23,draw]) to permute training groups in canonical title-key order. Sample one actual vote per training title with its separate shared draw seed. Use identical nested prefixes of 6, 12, 24, 48, 96, 192, 384 and 768 movies/ratings across all procedures. The default fixed score is the parent library’s first head. A fresh label is one observed user–movie event. Data acquisition uses the existing release. Report training votes and held-out certification votes separately. A monetary cost conversion would be hypothetical.

**Evaluation and threshold.** Report held-out normalized individual-rating ranked probability score (RPS), CE in nats, and TVD to each movie’s empirical histogram; all are lower-is-better. RPS averages four threshold errors separately for each vote, and CE is per observed vote. Average votes within each movie, movies equally, then the 64 draws. Use paired group resampling of entire movies for approximate intervals conditional on the observed users and training draws; do not treat repeated users or training draws as independent populations. In the primary panel use a 95% simultaneous max-t band across all eight budgets and four comparator-minus-direct-CE RPS differences (raw Pearson, uniform, training histogram, calibrated Pearson), with 4,000 held-out movie bootstrap draws using the parent routine. The reported threshold is the smallest tested budget at which both Pearson and uniform lower bounds are positive and remain so for every larger tested budget. Additionally check whether both stronger baselines also clear the bounds. Keep the disjoint-user results descriptive. For matched calibration and the disjoint panel use pointwise 95% movie bootstrap intervals, and identify winner changes. No inference to all customers, future movies or undisclosed ratings. With weak metadata-only predictors we expect the training histogram and calibrated score to be important comparators. Whether direct CE clears them is empirical.

**Checks and data rights.** Verify source SHA-256, no title overlap across splits, no user overlap in the disjoint panel, and training-order size. Replay selections and individual-rated test metrics independently on the smallest and largest budgets. Publish code and aggregate results only.
