# Public ordinal ratings, study design | 11-21-2026

As of 11-21-2026, this design precedes all supervised study results. We inspected the papers, release schemas, counts, feature availability, and reported benchmark results first. Both benchmark populations have been studied publicly. We hold out items for this replication. No claim is made that a foundation model or benchmark developer never saw them.

## Populations and primary comparisons

1. **Expert factual consistency in SummEval.** Three original 1–5 expert ratings per machine summary, 16 submitted systems on 100 source news articles. Reassessments by experts in the original collection limit rater independence. Hash-rank unique article IDs by SHA-256 of the UTF-8 string `corrce23-split|` followed by the ID. The first 40 articles in this order are the test set; all 16 systems appear in both splits. The other 60 supply training cases. The primary judge starts from the empirical distribution of the released GPT-4 G-Eval consistency responses to the same source and summary. Use verified text/ID joins, retain unparseable model generations in the denominator and report parse rates; normalize valid in-range grades per case, or use uniform if none. Distinguish model response samples from paid human grades. Repeat descriptively for expert relevance with the corresponding published GPT-4 prompt and outputs, and for a feature-based consistency judge using released ROUGE-2 F1, ROUGE-L F1, BERTScore F1 and source-to-summary BARTScore. Never feed human scores into predictors. [SummEval](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.24/) · [G-Eval results](https://github.com/nlpyang/geval/tree/6f84404ce4503d2ba0cde0293c5d402ecf2976a8/results)
2. **Technical image quality in KonIQ-10k.** Use the authors’ 7,058 training and 2,015 test images. Reserve the original 1,000 validation images unused by the primary fixed-grid experiment. The target is a randomly drawn observed five-category image-quality vote. Use the seven released technical image indicators. Exclude human means, dispersions, counts, IDs and the categorical content cluster from the predictors. Distinct test images are the inference units because the tables supply no photographer grouping. The authors screened some workers with correlation to preliminary opinion scores. Their separate crowd totals and our released histogram totals are documented with the source. [Source release](https://doi.org/10.18419/DARUS-2435)

## Equal-budget fits

Use 64 fixed training draws per dataset and rating depth. On each draw, uniformly permute training items without replacement (NumPy SeedSequence [20261121, 23, replicate], zero-based replicate); on each item randomly permute three votes sampled without replacement from its actual histogram with a separate seeded generator. The same first observation feeds the one-vote and three-vote fits. Reuse nested prefixes across budgets, models and fit objectives. Each case has equal weight. Primary budgets are 6, 12, 24, 48, 96, 192, 384, and 768 individual human ratings, at one rating per unique item. Extend KonIQ to 1,536, 3,072 and 6,144. Secondary depth is three ratings per item at the same exact totals. The 64 draws reuse the existing training population and measure sensitivity to acquiring a different sample. They are not 64 independent public benchmarks. An item sampling fraction or archive result cannot recover selection into becoming a benchmark rater.

**Candidate judges.** For GPT-4, use experiment 21's 27 transformations of the native probability vector, generalized to five categories (mix in uniform with weight 0.000006; exponent 0.5, 1 or 2; linear and quadratic log-probability tilts −1, 0 or 1 using evenly spaced grade positions from −1 to 1). For the feature-based judges, fit a finite library using each standardized feature and the first two principal-component scores of the training features as predictors. Center and scale using training features only, clip features and standardized component scores to ±3, fix each component sign by making its largest absolute loading positive. For a predictor z, an ordinal forecast gives grade r=1,…,5 probability proportional to exp(−0.5((r−a−bz)/s)²). Use centers a in {1,3,5}, slopes b in {−1.5,−0.75,0.75,1.5}, widths s in {0.65,1.25}, then mix with uniform with weight 0.000006. Include uniform as a candidate in both libraries. The image/metric judge is selected from this library, without training a deep image or language model. Feature-judge reference before fitting is the first raw feature, center 3, slope +0.75, width 1.25. Its convenience makes it less informative as a comparator than the training histogram.

On each matched draw exhaustively minimize mean individual-rating cross-entropy (CE), maximize Pearson correlation of the predicted versus observed per-item mean ratings, or, as a secondary comparison requested by Sanmi, minimize individual-rating ranked probability score (RPS). All search budget and inputs match. A tie within 1e−10 favors the native identity or the designated feature reference, then the declared order. A constant predictor has undefined Pearson correlation; if all correlations are undefined, use the reference. Other baselines are uniform; a constant training grade histogram (0.5 pseudocount per category); the original judge; and **Pearson followed by distribution calibration**. For the latter, form up to four score bins using the 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles of the selected Pearson judge's expected scores on unlabeled training features, dropping duplicate cuts. Estimate the grade distribution in each bin using the same training ratings, adding eight observations' worth of the smoothed global training histogram to each bin. Empty bins predict the global histogram. No additional human ratings enter this comparator.

**Multivariable sensitivity.** For consistency and image quality fit a five-category linear softmax on all four or seven automatic inputs, respectively. Compare directly minimizing CE with directly maximizing Pearson, using matching features and equal budgets. For each of 16 paired draws, use one grade per item and budgets 12, 192 and 768, plus 6,144 images for KonIQ. Each objective separately chooses ridge strength 0.001, 0.1 or 1.0 by RPS on the last quarter of its own training draw; include that quarter in the final refit. Start both fits from the smoothed training prior plus the same two opposite small ordered-class slopes. Use L-BFGS with up to 100 iterations per start, penalizing deviations from the prior. Record convergence and handle constant targets explicitly. Also fit a ridge mean-score judge followed by four-bin distribution calibration. This optimizer has no global guarantee on Pearson; the analysis is descriptive.

## Evaluation, uncertainty, money

Evaluate normalized individual-rating RPS on five ordered categories (average squared cumulative probability error over four thresholds, range 0–1, lower better). This primary measure is outside the two objectives under comparison and corresponds to predicting a human's ordered rating. Also report CE in nats; total variation distance (TVD, 0–1) against each item's empirical histogram; multiclass Brier score; exact grade agreement with a randomly drawn human, modal agreement with human modal ties receiving partial credit; mean grade bias and absolute mean-rating error; agreement in expected scores through Pearson, Spearman, Kendall, and Lin concordance; and cumulative-probability calibration error in ten fixed equal-width bins. For SummEval separately measure agreement in rankings and average grade error across the 16 systems. A human comparator predicts one held-out human vote from the remaining votes with a half-count per class; report the number of votes behind that reference. Scoring on Pearson is appropriate for an association use case and cannot by itself establish distributional agreement. TVD against three expert votes is a noisy diagnostic.

Test estimates average draws first and weight test items equally. At each primary budget compute paired test RPS differences of each comparator minus the CE fit (positive favors CE). Construct approximate simultaneous two-sided bands via 4,000 bootstrap resamples of entire held-out articles or images, using the maximum absolute standardized bootstrap deviation across all primary budgets and the four named comparators (Pearson, uniform, histogram, Pearson-then-calibrate). Within each primary benchmark use the 97.5th percentile of that maximum, giving an approximate joint 95% family across the two primary benchmarks by Bonferroni. A fully constant difference has zero sampling variance. These bands condition on the available training pool, fixed schedules, systems and rater mix; separately report variability across training draws. Report Pearson wins when the full interval is negative. Report every tested budget, individual joint wins, and the smallest tested budget from which all larger tested budgets also win. No interpolation to untested N. Primary success requires CE beat both Pearson and uniform. A deployment recommendation additionally requires improvement over the training histogram and the calibrated Pearson baseline. Report directional evidence, absolute performance, and a descriptive materiality scenario of 0.005 RPS; that margin has no claimed universal utility.

For practical annotation costs report unique items and distinct rating events, original documented pay or survey timing when available, evaluation-label overhead separately, and explicit illustrative marginal rates of $0.10, $1 and $5 per rating when observed prices are unavailable. One SummEval assignment scores several dimensions and can reuse a source across summaries; an hourly wage alone does not identify a per-rating cost. A human-rating budget for model training differs from the cost to independently certify the result. Released-data acquisition cost for this run is zero.

**Predictions and checks.** Exact outcome distributions minimize expected CE and RPS; a point mass on the observed grade scores zero on both. Uniform has CE log(5), constant means give undefined correlations. Permuting item assignments should destroy conditional association in expectation while preserving a marginal histogram. We expect ceiling-skew and quantization to weaken uniform as a baseline and make distribution fitting helpful at some budgets, with score-then-calibration narrowing the gap. The direction of ranking agreement, the strength of the histogram and the existence of a sustained practical threshold remain empirical. No prospective loss result was inspected before this document.
