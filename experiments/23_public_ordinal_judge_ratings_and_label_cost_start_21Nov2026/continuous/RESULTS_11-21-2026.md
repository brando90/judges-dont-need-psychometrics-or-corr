# Multivariable judges fitted on sparse human grades | 11-21-2026

As of 11-21-2026. Cross-entropy (CE) fitting gave lower held-out ranked probability score (RPS) than the Pearson fit for six of seven tested dataset–budget combinations. The exception was SummEval at 12 grades, where the mean difference was small and its interval included zero. Fitting a mean-score model and then calibrating its distribution had lower mean RPS than either direct fit at 12 grades on both tasks. Most Pearson fits reached the iteration cap. [Method and data split](METHOD_11-21-2026.md) · [Frozen implementation](https://github.com/brando90/judges-dont-need-psychometrics-or-corr/commit/8d238e6)

SummEval held out 640 machine summaries from 40 news articles and tested against the three observed expert factual-consistency ratings per summary. KonIQ-10k held out 2,015 photographs and tested against 215,415 technical image-quality votes. At each budget we repeated training on 16 matched draws, with one human grade per item. The direct judges fitted all four automatic summary metrics or all seven image indicators using five-category linear softmax. Both used the same two fixed starts and 100 L-BFGS iterations per start and separately chose their ridge penalty by RPS on the last quarter of each training draw. The calibrated score baseline fitted a ridge mean-score model and estimated a five-category distribution within each training-score quartile. No model used human grades as input features. [Source and training details](../PROTOCOL_11-21-2026.md)

### Held-out distribution forecasts

Normalized individual-rating RPS averages squared cumulative probability errors over the four grade thresholds, from 0 to 1, lower is better. The two difference columns subtract the CE fit’s test RPS from the named comparator’s test RPS on the same examples. Brackets give pointwise 95% percentile intervals from 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples of held-out articles or images, conditional on the same 16 training draws. The two datasets are public and have each been studied previously.

**Variation across training samples.** At 12 grades on SummEval, CE fitting had lower test RPS than Pearson fitting on 10 of 16 draws despite slightly higher mean RPS. Larger losses on the other six draws changed the mean. On KonIQ at 12 grades, CE fitting beat Pearson fitting on 12 of 16 draws and the calibrated score on 6 of 16. At 768 grades, CE fitting beat Pearson fitting on all 16 draws in each dataset, and the calibrated score on 16 of 16 SummEval draws and 13 of 16 KonIQ draws. Each draw evaluates the same test set. [Counts for every budget and comparator](results/verified_draw_wins.csv)

**CE and total variation distance (TVD).** Test CE is average negative log probability of a human grade, in nats. Test TVD is half the summed absolute probability differences from the empirical histogram, with range 0 to 1. Both are lower-is-better. At 12 SummEval grades, the CE fit’s test CE was 0.83192 nats versus 0.68535 for the Pearson fit and 0.62658 for the calibrated score. Its TVD was 0.26956 versus 0.28335 and 0.29397. At 6,144 KonIQ grades, the CE fit, Pearson fit and calibrated score had test CE 1.03772, 1.19995 and 1.04639 nats, respectively, with TVD 0.21390, 0.31208 and 0.21918. The three-grader empirical TVD in SummEval has greater sampling noise than a dense histogram. ### Optimization and verification

All 112 selected CE fits converged. Of 112 selected Pearson fits, 106 reached the 100-iteration cap (42 of 48 SummEval and all 64 KonIQ), four converged and two SummEval training samples had constant observed grades and returned the prior. Across all validation starts the counts were 672 converged for CE and 613 capped, 35 converged and 24 undefined for Pearson. Each of 112 training draws and budgets was fitted under both objectives. Changing the starting sign altered held-out RPS by more than 0.001 on 31 of 112 Pearson fits and zero CE fits. The alternative sign’s change in test RPS ranged from −0.01736 to +0.04945 on SummEval and from −0.00425 to +0.02691 on KonIQ. The fitting procedure selected the sign using the penalized training objective. These differences describe finite-optimizer behavior. [SummEval optimizer receipts](results/summeval_convergence.csv) · [KonIQ optimizer receipts](results/koniq_convergence.csv)

An independent central-difference calculation on synthetic inputs verified both penalty-inclusive gradients to maximum absolute errors $2.30\times10^{-11}$ (CE) and $4.32\times10^{-9}$ (Pearson). A full replay recomputed validation choices and all 224 final selected fits, checked each final penalized training value and used the discrete energy-distance identity for ordinal RPS to independently score all held-out probabilities. Absolute CE and TVD and both endpoints of every paired interval were also independently scored and compared. Run the commands below in this directory, after reconstructing both local data archives. ### Exploratory 1,000-iteration audit

As of 11-21-2026, after observing the original fits reach the iteration cap, we registered a tenfold increase and reran all seven budgets, with the same two objectives, starts, penalty grid and validation procedure. The higher cap gave 39 converged Pearson fits, 71 capped fits and two constant-target prior fallbacks among the 112 selected fits. All 112 chosen CE fits and the calibrated-score baseline reproduced their original predictions’ scores. Pearson's validation-selected penalty changed on 41 of 112 draws. The split, fitted procedures, positive-favors-CE orientation and 10,000 paired test-group bootstrap are the same as in the original results. At 12 SummEval grades the sign changed and both intervals include zero. At 12 KonIQ grades the gap fell from 0.00668 to 0.00175. At 6,144 KonIQ grades all selected Pearson fits still reached the higher cap. Across the 32 starts for those 16 final fits, the median range of the Pearson judge's predicted **training** means was 0.000458 grade points, versus 1.842 in the CE fits. Their median training Pearson correlations were 0.5314 and 0.5120, respectively. Nineteen of the 32 Pearson starts spanned less than 0.001 grade point. On SummEval at 12 grades, 28 starts had nonconstant observed targets. Their median training correlation was 0.9952, with a median predicted mean range of 0.000626. A numerically high correlation can therefore accompany almost constant predicted grades. Both caps measure the attained fits of the specified algorithm; neither certifies a global optimum for Pearson.
