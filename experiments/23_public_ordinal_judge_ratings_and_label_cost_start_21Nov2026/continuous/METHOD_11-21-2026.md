# Continuous multivariable judge sensitivity | 11-21-2026

As of 11-21-2026, the procedure below was specified before fitting either population. This companion to the [main protocol](../PROTOCOL_11-21-2026.md) uses four automatic summary metrics for SummEval consistency and seven technical image indicators for KonIQ-10k. It holds out the same articles or images as the primary experiment. Each comparison uses the first 16 shared training draws, one observed grade per item, at budgets of 12, 192 and 768 grades. KonIQ also uses 6,144 grades. [Runnable source](run.py)

**Forecast and fit.** Standardize every feature by the full unlabeled training split's mean and standard deviation, then clip each to ±3 and append an intercept. For the fitting subset, let $p_0$ be its empirical five-category histogram with 0.5 added per class. The forecast is $q(x)=\operatorname{softmax}(\log p_0+xW)$. Use mean individual-grade cross-entropy (CE) or one minus the correlation between expected and observed grades, adding $\lambda\lVert W\rVert_F^2/2$ to either loss. Every coefficient, including intercept offsets, is penalized. The centered grade values use five bins. Shifting every grade by one produces the same Pearson and softmax fits.

Both objectives use L-BFGS-B without bounds and two fixed starts. The starts have zero intercept offsets and opposite slope matrices with Frobenius norm 0.1. Before normalization the matrix is the outer product of the positive feature indices 1 to $d$ and the centered class indices −2 to 2. A run permits 100 iterations and 20 line-search steps per iteration, with function tolerance $10^{-10}$ and gradient tolerance $10^{-7}$. Choose the start with the lower final finite penalized training objective, prioritizing the positive start within $10^{-12}$. Finite solutions at a numerical stopping limit retain their status and prediction. When the target is constant or the inputs have no variation, the Pearson procedure returns the fitting subset's smoothed prior and records the fallback. A candidate with expected-grade range at most $10^{-12}$ receives Pearson loss 2. The paper should distinguish solutions with negligible mean variation or line-search failure from a certified Pearson optimum.

For each objective choose $\lambda$ from 0.001, 0.1 and 1 by normalized individual-rating ranked probability score (RPS) on the last quarter of the sampled training items. All parameters and the prior in these candidate fits use the first three quarters. Choose the first penalty within $10^{-12}$ of the minimum, then refit with all sampled grades. For the baseline, fit ridge regression of grade on the same inputs with a free intercept, minimizing half the mean squared error plus $\lambda\lVert\beta\rVert^2/2$. Estimate the five-category distribution in each training-score quartile bin, adding eight pseudocounts distributed as $p_0$. Tied scores at a cut go to the higher bin and an empty bin yields the prior. Tune the ridge penalty and refit on the same validation split and budget.

**Reporting.** Average individual-grade RPS and CE over observed ratings within each held-out item. Compute total variation distance (TVD) to the item’s empirical five-bin histogram. Average the 16 draws, then weight test items equally. Each contrast subtracts the CE-trained judge's test loss from the comparator’s loss on identical items. Descriptive pointwise 95% percentile intervals use 10,000 paired resamples of the 40 held-out articles or 2,015 held-out images with seed 20261123. The 16 fitted draws, participating raters and feature specification are held fixed. The two evaluation panels have equal numbers of test items per group. The script also records both starts’ convergence status, iterations, attained objective difference, selected mean range and difference in held-out RPS. The competing start’s test performance is a diagnostic only.
