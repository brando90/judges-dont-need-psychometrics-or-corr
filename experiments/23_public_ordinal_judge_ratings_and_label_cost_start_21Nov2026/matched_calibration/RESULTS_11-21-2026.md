# What remains after every score gets the same probability calibration? | 11-21-2026

As of 11-21-2026. Once all scalar judge scores receive the same smoothed distribution estimates, the answer depends on what the score retains about the item. On SummEval, a raw cross-entropy (CE) selector often preferred a uniform forecast, so its score could no longer distinguish summaries. Selecting the score by leave-one-out calibrated CE kept most of the information available to the Pearson-selected score. On KonIQ, Pearson found useful sharpness information with fewer votes than leave-one-out CE. From 768 votes onward, all three supervised selectors gave exactly the same calibrated forecast for every held-out image on every training draw. This exploratory question was specified after we saw the main results. [Protocol commit](https://github.com/brando90/judges-dont-need-psychometrics-or-corr/commit/45e42f4)

**How the judges were fitted.** All methods use the same one-vote-per-item training budgets and the same 64 draws. For each candidate, form four bins from quartiles of its expected grade across all unlabeled training items. Each bin estimates its five grade probabilities from the available training votes, smoothing toward the global half-count prior with eight pseudocounts. Only the choice of input score varies. The fixed score comes from the original GPT-4 forecast on SummEval or the default brightness-based head on KonIQ. Raw CE and Pearson refer to selection among the original candidate forecasts by their respective training objectives. Leave-one-out (LOO) CE searches the same complete candidate list by the training CE of the calibrated score, removing each vote from both its bin and the global prior for its own prediction. It then refits the probabilities using all votes. All searches are exhaustive within 28 candidate forecasts for SummEval or 217 for KonIQ. The frozen candidate order resolves numerical ties. [Calibration and selection equations](PROTOCOL_11-21-2026.md) · [Runnable implementation](run.py)

**Held-out test RPS.** Normalized individual-rating ranked probability score (RPS) measures squared cumulative probability error on the four grade thresholds, ranging from 0 to 1 with lower better. Differences in the final two columns subtract the indicated alternative’s *test RPS* from the calibrated Pearson-selected judge’s *test RPS*. Positive favors the alternative. Pointwise 95% percentile intervals use 10,000 paired resamples of the held-out group, after averaging the losses of the 64 fitted procedures. They condition on these training draws and this public collection. The intervals do not establish a simultaneous sample-size threshold.

### Absolute CE and distributional distance

Test CE is the average negative log probability of an observed human grade, in nats. Total variation distance (TVD) is half the summed absolute difference from each item’s observed grade proportions (0–1). Lower is better. At the largest SummEval budget, raw CE, Pearson, and LOO CE scores had test CE of 0.54702, 0.42135 and 0.42155 and TVD of 0.22799, 0.17096 and 0.17044. At the largest KonIQ budget, the three had the same test CE of 1.07173 and TVD of 0.23373. The fixed brightness score gave test CE of 1.14186 and TVD of 0.27386. The underlying CSVs include the fixed reference and all three supervised selections at every budget. [SummEval absolute losses](summeval/scores.csv) · [KonIQ absolute losses](koniq/scores.csv)

### How much signal is available in four bins?

An empirical oracle uses the actual test grades twice, first to estimate each bin’s five-category histogram and then to evaluate it. The best candidate partition and the best histogram for each bin are chosen on those same outcomes. This gives an optimistic lower bound on empirical test RPS within this fixed class of score partitions. The item-specific minimum also uses the observed outcomes and can be optimistic with few ratings.

| Population | Single-bin RPS | Best four-bin RPS | Maximum RPS gain from splitting | Item-specific minimum RPS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SummEval, 640 summaries | .07231 | .05354 | .01876 | .01059 |
| KonIQ, 2,015 images | .10540 | .09206 | .01334 | .06968 |

At the largest training budget the calibrated LOO CE fit scored 0.05425 on SummEval and 0.09215 on KonIQ. The differences above the optimistic four-bin bounds were approximately 0.00071 and 0.00009. The gap remaining to the item-specific score describes heterogeneity within the partitions and finite test-rating noise. Population performance outside the studied source articles, images, raters and candidate families was not measured.
