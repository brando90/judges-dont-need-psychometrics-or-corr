# Matched distributional calibration of scalar judge scores | 11-21-2026

As of 11-21-2026, the primary results were already known. This exploratory protocol was written before running its matched-calibration comparisons. It asks whether the scalar score selected by cross-entropy (CE) or Pearson carries useful information when every candidate gets the same supervised conversion from scores to grade probabilities.

**Inputs and budget.** Reuse the frozen primary forecast families, splits and all 64 one-rating training draws in [the main design](../PROTOCOL_11-21-2026.md). SummEval consistency uses the 27 transformations of the released GPT-4 probability forecast plus uniform. KonIQ-10k uses the 216 indicator-based heads plus uniform. Use all eight registered budgets for summaries (6–768 ratings) and all eleven for images (6–6,144 ratings). Each training vote belongs to a different item within a draw. Each forecast produces its scalar expected grade in 1–5. For reference, use the native identity head for SummEval and the declared fixed default indicator head for KonIQ. The latter is an experiment-defined baseline.

**Identical calibration.** For each candidate set the three quartile cutpoints of its expected grade using all unlabeled training items, as in the published primary procedure. Deduplicate coinciding cutpoints and place ties on their right. Assign labeled training and test items to those same bins. With $B$ training votes, pooled grade counts $N_r$, and grade counts $n_{jr}$ in bin $j$, the prior is $p_r=(N_r+0.5)/(B+2.5)$. The calibrated forecast is $(n_{jr}+8p_r)/(n_j+8)$ where $n_j=\sum_r n_{jr}$. A bin without a training vote receives the prior. Use this one map for four score sources:

1. The fixed reference head.
2. The head selected by CE on the raw probability forecasts and the available training grades.
3. The head selected by Pearson on the original scalar scores and the available training grades.
4. The candidate minimizing leave-one-training-label-out CE of the calibrated forecast. For each observed grade $r$ in bin $j$ its own forecast probability is
   $[n_{jr}-1+8(N_r-1+0.5)/(B-1+2.5)]/(n_j-1+8)$. Minimize mean negative log probability over the $B$ training votes, then refit using all $B$. The cutpoints never use labels. All four sources spend the same $B$ grades. Search over the complete prespecified candidate list; within $10^{-10}$ of the best value choose its earliest candidate. Reuse the exact published choices for methods 2 and 3.

**Evaluation.** Report absolute held-out normalized individual-rating ranked probability score (RPS), CE in nats and total variation distance (TVD) to each item's empirical rating histogram. All are lower-is-better. For each alternative report the calibrated Pearson-selected fit’s loss minus the alternative’s loss on identical items. Average predictions’ losses over the 64 draws, weight held-out items equally, and use pointwise 95% percentile intervals over 10,000 paired held-out group resamples (seed 20261124). The units are 40 held-out source articles with 16 summaries each, or 2,015 held-out images. These bands condition on training orders and participating raters. Draw win counts use a numerical tie tolerance of $10^{-12}$. No multiplicity-adjusted claim or budget threshold is planned.

**How much signal could this score representation retain?** After the training-only forecasts are fixed, calculate a descriptive, deliberately optimistic score on the same test outcomes. For each fixed candidate's score bins assign the mean of the test items’ empirical histograms within each bin. This minimizes empirical mean RPS and CE within that partition. Report the smallest attainable RPS across the finite partitions, its improvement over the same-test single-bin oracle, and the item-specific empirical-histogram RPS. Compute the analogous CE minima separately. The oracle uses test outcomes and is excluded from predictive comparisons. These are ceilings on the measured improvements available to this particular representation on these observed labels, not population irreducible scores. The arithmetic mean need not minimize TVD, so no TVD ceiling is asserted.

**Checks.** Compare the calibrated Pearson arm to the published version on all common budgets and metrics. Verify the leave-one-out formula by explicitly refitting the bin and marginal histograms after deleting one vote on a generated fixture and on sampled draws. Predict that calibrating a constant score gives the same distribution for every item, and that identical partitions produce identical optimal calibrated training CE. The ordering of held-out methods remains open.
