# Fitting rating distributions from open- and closed-weight judge outputs | 11-21-2026

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/judges-dont-need-psychometrics-or-corr/blob/main/experiments/19_small_data_moment_recovery_open_and_closed_judges_start_21Nov2026/human_judges/PROTOCOL_11-21-2026.md>

Date: 11-21-2026

**TLDR:** Fit identical distributional calibration heads by cross-entropy (CE) and Pearson correlation on observed outputs of four named language models. Test whether scarce expert ratings favor Pearson for the first moment or CE for the higher moments. The study is retrospective and reuses earlier expert judgments, with no new model calls.

## Inputs and matched training

Use the 75 previously rated artifacts, 25 tasks, 19 task families and 195 individual integer ratings on 0–5 from experiment 18. Use only the original P0 human-mirror prompt’s elicited six-category probabilities (verbal / JSON) on each artifact. Take two open-weight backbones, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and Qwen2.5-Coder-14B-Instruct, and two closed-weight application programming interface (API) backbones, gpt-5.4-mini and gpt-6.1-sol. The public releases and API catalog are identified in the model manifest. These are purposefully selected to match output representation and family sizes, without comparing their outcome metrics. Open/closed status is descriptive and confounded with provider and backbone. Preserve all uniform parse-failure fallbacks. Use six input sets: the four individual backbones and an equally weighted arithmetic probability mixture of the two backbones within each class. The two mixtures are the primary input sets, with per-backbone sensitivity. Normalize each archived row to unit sum and smooth once as (1−6ε)p+ε, ε=10⁻⁶, including the identity control.

Use the same 250 fixed ordered training/test family splits and seeded rating subsamples as experiment 18. Four families are held out per split. Cross 3, 7 or 15 training families with the first one or two randomly permuted observed ratings per training artifact. Score every available rating on held-out artifacts. No raters were held out. The test items and outcomes have historical exposure. Upstream judge reference provenance matched the annotation version on 15 of 75 artifacts for experiment 10, and was unresolved for the other 60. Human ratings and original unpublished artifact text stay in their source directories.

For each input probability vector p, fit an artifact-dependent six-category prediction q(r) proportional to exp(t log p(r) + b u(r) + c u(r)²), u(r)=(r−2.5)/2.5. Three globally shared calibration coefficients alter confidence, level and spread across artifacts. Fit all 27 triples in {0.5,1,2} × {−1,0,1} × {−1,0,1} by (i) individual-rating CE or (ii) Pearson correlation of candidate means against per-artifact training means. Both are exact searches over the same grid and share examples, transforms, compute count and tolerance 10⁻¹². Order ties by squared distance (log₂(t))²+b²+c² from identity, then ascending (t,b,c). Constant-target / all-undefined Pearson falls back to identity. An explicitly secondary Pearson arm breaks training-Pearson ties by training normalized ranked probability score (RPS) and then the same distance/lexicographic rule. It has no extra labels. Include untreated identity inputs and a constant training histogram smoothed by half a count per category. No validation hyperparameters, gradient training, language-model parameter updates or new prompts.

## Estimands, uncertainty and predictions

- Primary endpoint: held-out normalized individual-rating RPS, the mean of five squared cumulative-threshold errors [0,1], lower is better. Compare Pearson-fitted minus CE-fitted RPS on the same test artifacts. Positive favors CE. Also compare each fitted method against the training constant on normalized first- and second-moment losses. No practical margin or hypothesis test (p-val=n/a). Only directional pointwise intervals excluding zero support a directional claim on this archive.
- Let z=R/5 and m_j(q)=Σ_r q(r)(r/5)^j. For j=1,…,5 the individual-rating moment loss is (m_j(q)−z^j)², in [0,1], lower is better. Its population risk equals squared conditional moment error plus an irreducible Var(z^j|artifact). Paired losses cancel that noise term in expectation. The second raw moment combines level and dispersion; it is not the conditional variance. Also report a *centered spread* diagnostic, squared error of forecast Var(z) against the unbiased sample variance of the two/three held-out ratings. This target is noisy and eligibility uses at least two ratings, which all archived artifacts have. Estimates are distribution- and metric-specific; loss scales differ across powers. Comparisons always use the same moment and the same test ratings.
- Other endpoints: CE (nats per rating, ≥0), total variation distance (TVD; half the L1 distance to the empirical histogram, [0,1]), mean absolute error and signed prediction bias versus artifact mean ratings (points), E[forecast mean] and E[human mean], Pearson and midrank Spearman correlations versus raw observed means (−1 to 1, higher is better). Use raw integer-label means and preserve numerical ties within 10⁻¹² in predicted means for ranking. Means, signed bias and ranks use the 0–5 scale. Output all six budgets, all six input sets, all methods, meaningful denominators and fallback counts; retain undefined correlations as missing.
- Artifact weighting: average individual rating losses within each artifact, then average artifacts within the held-out split and average the 250 split averages equally. Correlations are within-split. Use shared pointwise 95% empirical-support Bayesian family bootstrap intervals (fixed seed 20261123, 1,000 exponential-weight draws on the 19 families) for every input/budget's Pearson-minus-CE RPS, moment losses and variance diagnostic; also give CE/each moment and RPS skill of fitted forecasts against constant (fitted minus constant; lower favors fitted). Reselect parameters on the reweighted training artifacts in each draw and weight test artifacts by the same family weights. To refit the constant under a draw, normalize training-artifact weights to sum to the original number of training artifacts before adding half a count per class. All intervals condition on the observed human raters, subsamples, candidate backbones and split schedule. Do not treat splits as independent. Report unadjusted pointwise intervals and avoid inferring equivalence from an interval crossing zero.
- Predictions fixed before measurement: (1) in the smallest pooled input cells (3 families, one rating), CE will improve first-moment squared loss versus constant in at least one of the two pools; (2) in a pool with such a first-moment improvement, at least one higher moment or centered spread will remain unresolved versus constant (interval contains zero); (3) mean-based Pearson training need not maximize held-out Pearson and need not improve first-moment squared loss over CE, because it allows affine errors. No forced RPS winner or monotonic sample threshold. If both the mean and higher moments improve, report that counterevidence to a mean-only conclusion. If neither improves, that also fails to show a mean-only regime.
- Checks: verify source digests, joins and split/retained labels against experiment 18; independent reconstruction of training optima, identity and constant, held-out metric formulas with individual ratings and centered variance; validate paired endpoints and bootstrap for selected draws. Code and aggregate output only. Local CPU, zero additional inference cost.
