# How to read the small-data rating-moment study (11-21-2026)

Date: 11-21-2026

**Doc link:** <https://docs.google.com/document/d/1qA1MyA0qch1ZA8cb869dNf7iaznoN3iO1lkp6RWFZTk/edit>

The [summary](README.md) gives the mathematical claim, its finite-sample limits and the smallest-budget comparisons. Both [protocols](https://github.com/brando90/judges-dont-need-psychometrics-or-corr/commit/8b2c3dd) preceded measurement. The two experiments answer different questions.

- **Historical human ratings.** The scores estimate held-out loss after fitting the same 27 three-parameter probability transforms to outputs from four named, frozen language models. Four families per split are held out from that fit. The historical labels had prior exposure. The primary ranked probability score (RPS) averages five squared cumulative-probability errors on the 0–5 rating scale. Lower values on the 0–1 scale are better. Compare both fits with the original forecast and the constant training histogram. Intervals reweight the same 19 task families across the overlapping splits and refit both methods in each of 1,000 draws. The intervals are pointwise and conditional on these data. [Definitions, units, model manifest and reproduction commands](human_judges/HOW_TO_READ_RESULTS.md)
- **Exact finite-sample risk.** Three synthetic input types have a known conditional law, so mean, variance and whole-distribution errors can be measured directly. The study averages over every possible training sample and future ratings at the same types. Six ratings permit 729 ordered response sequences in the independent cross-check. Both objectives exhaustively search a finite candidate family. The risk is conditional on which law is true, what the learner knows and how ties are resolved. These exact selection probabilities have no sampling interval. [Definitions, units and reproduction commands](theory_simulation/HOW_TO_READ_RESULTS.md)

The root figure is generated by `python3 plot_budget.py` from the historical [paired results](human_judges/contrasts.csv). Its differences subtract cross-entropy-fitted loss from Pearson-fitted loss for the same test metric and test artifacts. Positive favors the cross-entropy fit. All three plotted losses range from 0 to 1.
