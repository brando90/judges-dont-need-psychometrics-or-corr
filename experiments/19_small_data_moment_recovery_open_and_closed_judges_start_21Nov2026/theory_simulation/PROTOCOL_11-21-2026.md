**TLDR:** Compare cross-entropy (CE) and mean-based Pearson by enumerating every possible training histogram under each of four known probability laws. Score the conditional mean, second moment, variance and distribution on fresh individual ratings from the same three equally weighted input types. This is an analytic synthetic control for the real-judge comparison.

## Sampling law fixed before measurement

Take rating R=2+D where D∈{−1,0,1}. The three supported integer ratings (1,2,3) lie on the paper's 0–5 scale. All candidates assign zero to 0,4,5. Three input types x=0,1,2 have equal test probability. The two possible conditional means of D are vectors a=(−1,0,1)/5 and b=(1,−1,0)/5. Two possible conditional second moments about rating 2 are t=2/5 (low spread) and 4/5 (high spread). For mean coordinate m and shape t use P(D=−1,0,+1)=((t−m)/2,1−t,(t+m)/2). Every candidate is strictly positive on the common support. All six category probabilities and their sums remain defined. The four possible true laws are exactly the four candidates. This is a deliberately tractable control. It does not model a particular rater population.

Main design: k=1,2,3,4,6,8,12 independent training ratings at each of the three input types, total N=3k. Boundary design: choose one of the input types uniformly and take one training rating total. Test against independent future ratings from the same input-type distribution exactly, by population averaging. These are new draws at known types, with no unseen type. No validation set, stopping, optimization initialization, rater sharing, model calls or label smoothing.

## Information, search and controls

For each of four possible true laws, compare (i) mean and spread both unknown (4 candidates), (ii) conditional mean curve supplied by an oracle, spread unknown (2 candidates), and (iii) spread supplied by an oracle, mean unknown (2 candidates). Every optimizer exhaustively compares the same allowed candidates on exactly the same training sample. Minimize total individual-rating negative log probability for CE (equivalent here to maximum likelihood), or maximize Pearson correlation between the candidate means and the three observed input-type means. Candidate means are centered at 2 and have identical norms. A Pearson maximum in the balanced design can be found exactly by maximizing the integer dot product of a candidate's centered mean pattern and the three signed rating totals. All samples with constant observed mean have undefined Pearson; use the same deterministic candidate priority over all allowed candidates and count this probability. With just one total rating, Pearson is always undefined and any resulting advantage belongs to its stated fallback. Training samples with ties or undefined correlation stay in the denominator.

Shared primary tie priority: mean a before b and, within tied mean, low spread before high. Sensitivity: same rule, high spread first in both objectives. Also report a 50:50 randomization over these two whole tie policies, independent of data. Do not choose a tie rule from test scores. Keep the true law explicit, since the first-listed Pearson shape can be exactly right or wrong.

## Outcomes and predictions

For the fitted conditional distribution, average over every possible training sample and all three fresh input types. Report conditional mean squared error (rating units squared), raw second-moment squared error (units to the fourth), conditional variance squared error (units to the fourth), and squared error in E[(R−2)² | x] as a measure of spread with the location fixed at rating 2. Mean and variance are separate targets. For probability forecasts report expected individual-rating ranked probability score (RPS; mean squared cumulative error over five thresholds; 0–1, lower better), CE (nats, lower better), total variation distance to true conditional probabilities (0–1, lower better), and Pearson and tied-rank Spearman between predicted and true conditional means (−1 to 1, higher better). The true conditional law is the oracle reference. Mean, second-moment and variance recovery probabilities are also available through the underlying exact selection probabilities.

Primary difference: population-test squared error in conditional variance for the Pearson-selected judge minus the same error for the CE-selected judge, conditional on the true law, control and sample size. Positive favors CE. The two competitors' absolute errors and the conditional mean target qualify this result. Summarize equal-prior mean-curve averages but preserve all four separate true laws. Also derive the prior probability of high spread at which CE and Pearson have equal risk for the fixed-center second moment with known mean. This threshold describes the chosen two-alternative model, not human behavior. These are exhaustive finite expectations. No bootstrap, p-value or simulation interval applies. Include minimum and maximum budget results plus machine-readable full curves.

Before measurement, we expect both objectives to have exactly zero mean error when supplied the true mean, and different spread errors whenever the tie policy picks an incorrect fixed shape. Under the correct Pearson shape default, a finite-sample CE learner can choose the wrong shape and lose; if Pearson always defaults to the wrong shape, CE can learn the correct shape and win even from a small sample. With fixed spread, the two objectives can use different information to select a mean curve. Their finite-sample mean errors need to be calculated, and neither is required to win. We do not predict a universal crossover.

Verification: rational probabilities must sum exactly to one under every true law and sample size, tie and undefined events must be retained, oracle losses and moment risks must match analytical formulas, any defined mean Pearson must be invariant across shapes, and an independent ordered-sequence calculation for k=1,2 must reproduce the histogram selector probabilities. Report all numerical or logical failures.
