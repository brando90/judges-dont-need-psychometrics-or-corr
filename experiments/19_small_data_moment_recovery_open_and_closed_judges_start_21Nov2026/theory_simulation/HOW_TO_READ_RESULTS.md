# Reading the exact moment-recovery calculations | 11-21-2026

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/judges-dont-need-psychometrics-or-corr/blob/main/experiments/19_small_data_moment_recovery_open_and_closed_judges_start_21Nov2026/theory_simulation/HOW_TO_READ_RESULTS.md>

Date: 11-21-2026. As of 11-21-2026, the verifier also reports sensitivity to the priority of the two mean curves.

**TLDR:** Each row conditions on a true law, number of training ratings, the information given to the learner, and its tie policy. Compare methods on the same conditional mean, variance or distribution target.

1. Start with the [results and figure](RESULTS_11-21-2026.md), [mathematical derivation](THEORY_11-21-2026.md) and [dated protocol](PROTOCOL_11-21-2026.md). All expectations cover fresh synthetic ratings at the same three known input types. All possible training ratings are included. This is a finite population calculation with no sampling uncertainty or p-values.
2. In [population_risk.csv](population_risk.csv), the selectors are cross-entropy (ce) and Pearson correlation of predicted and observed means (pearson). joint means both conditional mean and spread must be estimated, mean_known supplies the true mean, and spread_known supplies the true spread. The four candidate laws combine mean curve A or B with low or high spread. The primary tie rule is low_first for both selectors. high_first reverses only the spread priority, and random_half averages the two policies equiprobably. All three recorded policies prioritize mean curve A over B. The post-hoc check in the results separately reverses or fairly randomizes curve priority. Every comparison uses the same admissible candidate pool and same exhaustive search budget. The original table should be filtered by true law when considering a particular target.
3. mean_mse, second_moment_mse, variance_mse and fixed_center_second_moment_mse are average squared errors in E[R | x], E[R² | x], Var(R | x) and E[(R−2)² | x]. The first uses squared rating points and the latter three use rating points to the fourth, with zero ideal. recover_mean, recover_spread and recover_variance_function are probabilities on 0–1 (higher is better). The bracketed test targets are population quantities under the known synthetic law. rps is normalized individual-rating ranked probability score on five thresholds, ce_nats is individual-rating negative log probability, and population_tvd is total variation distance to the known conditional distribution. These score a future individual rating or its distribution, with zero optimal for distance and lower better for losses. The true distribution's expected individual-rating losses are generally positive.
4. In [exact_selection_mass.csv](exact_selection_mass.csv), divide each of the four candidate mass columns by common_mass_denominator to get its exact selection probability. undefined_pearson_mass uses the same denominator and refers to the training data, including on rows used to evaluate CE. The one_random_type training design has one total observation on a random input, and its Pearson value is always undefined. All other budgets have equal positive integer ratings per input type. The fixed-center spread crossover for the low-first rule and known mean appears in [known_mean_prior_threshold.csv](known_mean_prior_threshold.csv).
5. Run `python3 enumerate_risk.py`, `python3 verify.py` and `python3 plot.py` in this directory with NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib. The enumerator records the exact likelihood/count check per true law at each budget. The verifier replays both spread priorities at three and six ratings directly from ordered observations. For the four-candidate search under low-spread truth it also tallies strict curve wins and ties at every budget, checks the exact A-first selection counts on both curves, and prints mean squared error averaged equally over the two true curves for both selectors. This average also gives the error under either true curve if a fair coin sets curve priority.
