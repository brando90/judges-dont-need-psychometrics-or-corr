# How to read the Experiment 60 results

1. **Which data.** Individual human ratings of three public multi-annotator sets: alphaNLI (400 items), DICES-350 (350 items) and DICES-990 (400 items). For every item the ratings are split at random into half A, which builds the two judges, and half B, the held-out ratings that every number is measured against.
   Nothing is fitted on half B. The experiment is an oracle check on empirical rating laws: no language model is involved, so no number here describes a real judge. The ratings of these sets were seen in earlier experiments.
2. **Which metric.** Pearson correlation r (range -1 to 1, higher is better, 0 means no linear association) between a judge's output and the held-out single human ratings, over all (item, rating) observations of half B. The truthful judge outputs a score drawn from the half-A rating distribution of the item; the mean judge outputs the half-A mean rating.
   rho is the between-item share of the variance of a single rating (range 0 to 1; DICES about 0.19, alphaNLI about 0.65). Skill = 1 minus (risk of the forecast / risk of the human base-rate forecast) on half B (0 is the base rate, 1 is perfect, higher is better); risk is the unbiased per-sample proper score matched to the labels:
   Brier score (range 0 to 2) on alphaNLI and normalized ranked probability score (RPS, range 0 to 1; the squared distance between the cumulative forecast and the cumulative rating divided by the number of labels minus one) on DICES.
3. **What is subtracted.** "r of the mean judge minus r of the sampled judge" subtracts the same metric (Pearson r on half B) for two judges built from the same half A; positive means the mean judge looks better to Pearson. "Risk of the collapsed forecast minus risk of the truthful forecast" subtracts the same proper score on the same half B;
   positive means the truthful forecast is the better forecast. Every correlation here is against single ratings and is not the item-level correlation of Experiments 46 and 57.
4. **Uncertainty.** Percentile 95% intervals from 4,000 item-clustered bootstrap draws (each item is a cluster); the statistic is averaged over 200 random half-splits inside every draw. The intervals cover item sampling, not the finite number of human raters.
5. **How the judges were chosen.** Nothing was fitted. The truthful judge is the half-A empirical rating law (floored at 1e-6 for the forecasts); the collapsed forecast is the point mass at the grid label nearest the half-A mean; the base rate is the pooled half-A histogram. Closed-form correlations are checked by Monte Carlo sampling on 20 splits per set.

Decisions are in `results/hypotheses.json`: H1 supported (mean judge's r above the sampled judge's on all three sets), H2 supported (theory reproduces the correlations on 3 of 3 sets; this is an algebraic consequence of the construction, as the protocol says), H3 supported (the truthful forecast has lower matched-rule risk on all three sets).
Figure 1 (`results/figure1_sampled_score.png`): panel (a), the correlation of each judge against rho with the curves r = rho (dashed) and r = sqrt(rho) (solid), open markers for the sampled readout and filled markers for the mean readout, bars are 95% intervals; panel (b), skill of the truthful and the collapsed forecast on half B, error bars are 95% intervals, the zero line is the human base rate.
