The transcript adds the following requirements to this **unrun plan**. It does not supply a new empirical result or a paid-run approval.

### Formal relationship: state the random variables and the limits

- Make the connection to standardized squared distance explicit, with its nonzero-variance conditions. Distinguish correlation of a forecast with an individual human rating from correlation with the conditional human mean; those are different quantities. Also distinguish attaining a correlation optimum from identifying a unique forecast or its calibration. The existing information-gap calculation is not a sample-complexity result.
- Preserve the call's separation of the feasible judge family, the search procedure, and the objective. A result about cached global mixtures tests that family and search procedure; it does not establish a result for prompt search or weight training generally. Describe exactly which scalar rating the Pearson objective reads and retain probability forecasts for the proper-loss objectives.

### Real-data comparison: missing design details

1. **Paired, conditional predictions.** Compare the human and model on the same task and artifact, with the same available context. The call explicitly rejects comparing unrelated, pooled response marginals. Define the target as a rating distribution conditional on that artifact and on the stated rater population; condition on a named persona only when that persona is actually observed in the data. Fit individual observed human ratings with cross-entropy, keeping the model prediction probabilistic; do not make its output one-hot or require a densely measured human histogram before training.
2. **Which human variability is measured.** Separate variation across raters from repeated ratings by one rater. A collection of different raters does not by itself measure one person's repeatability. Record rater identity, available repetitions, context and any persona assignment; do not invent a persona or count copies as independent ratings. If repeated ratings are absent, mark within-rater variability as unmeasured.
3. **Rating-count sensitivity at fixed population.** The call asks how many ratings are needed before correlation misses consequential information, for fixed personas or a specified rater population, and notes dependence on the task/distribution. Plan a prespecified sensitivity analysis using only existing ratings, retaining their task/rater structure. Sparse subsampling is exploratory and cannot establish a general required sample size. Synthetic persona distributions can be a separately labeled control, never extra human evidence. New ratings or model-generated ratings are a separate future scope.
4. **Practical cost on held-out ratings.** The call asks whether the missing information matters in practice, beyond a more attractive mathematical definition. Keep ranked probability score (RPS) as the previously chosen primary endpoint; cross-entropy (CE), total variation distance (TVD), Pearson and Spearman remain secondary. Forward Kullback–Leibler divergence against an empirical histogram may be reported as a distribution diagnostic alongside CE, with the same sparse-histogram limitation as TVD; it is not a population-distribution measurement. Use the same held-out artifacts and matched search budgets for both methods. Explain the optimizer, starting point, convergence/certificate and tie rule. A synthetic example alone is insufficient evidence of a real-data advantage.
5. **Freeze interpretation before measuring.** Write the primary contrast as test RPS of the Pearson-trained judge minus test RPS of the CE-trained judge; positive favors the CE-trained judge. Define the split, prior exposure, aggregation, paired resampling unit and interval procedure in the runnable protocol before new measurements. Preserve experiment 02's frozen historical endpoint rather than relabeling it. The proposed association between estimated information gap and the held-out RPS difference is an exploratory hypothesis, not a consequence proved by the information-gap identity. Fix its analysis before looking at outcomes, account for sparse-rating estimation, and retain null or reversed results.
