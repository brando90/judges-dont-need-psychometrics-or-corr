# How to read the results of experiment 35

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/judges-dont-need-psychometrics-or-corr/blob/main/experiments/35_brier_vs_corr_math_l2_pearson_lean_start_26Nov2026/HOW_TO_READ_RESULTS.md>

**TLDR:** `results.txt` is the stdout of one seeded synthetic script. Identity lines should have an absolute difference near zero. There is no train, validation, or held-out human test split, and no confidence interval.

1. **Which data.** There is no human-rating split and no prior exposure of human ratings, because no human ratings are used. Part 1 uses one fixed distribution on ratings `{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5}`, `p* = (0.30, 0.05, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.35)`. The Monte Carlo block draws `N` independent ratings from that `p*`, `200` times, for each listed `N`. The column `emp Brier(p_hat)` is in-sample: it scores the empirical frequencies on the same `N` draws that produced them. The column `true E[BS(p_hat)]` is the population expected Brier score of those frequencies under `p*`. Parts 2 and 3 are further draws from the same NumPy Generator, seed `0`, in the order printed. Expected mean squared error (MSE), expected Brier score, and expected ranked probability score in Parts 3b and 3c are population expectations under the drawn conditional laws. Correlations and the Part 3a decomposition are computed on the same finite draw that is printed, so those lines are in-sample algebra checks.

2. **Which metric.**
   - **Brier score.** For a forecast `q` and an outcome `y`, `sum_j (q_j - 1{y = j})^2`. The script reports its expectation under the true law. Lower is better. For a probability vector the score lies in `[0, 2]`. The minimum population value on a law `p` is `1 - sum_k p_k^2` (the Bayes Brier score): `0.75` for the Part 1 law and `0.50` for the balanced law on `{0, 5}`.
   - **Scalar L2 / MSE.** Expected squared error of a real-valued forecast against the rating. Lower is better. On `{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5}` a perfect point forecast of a non-degenerate law still has risk equal to the conditional variance. The Part 2 bimodal law has variance `6.25`.
   - **Pearson correlation.** The ordinary sample correlation of two finite vectors. Range `[-1, 1]`. Higher means stronger positive linear association. It is not a proper score for a distribution.
   - **Ranked probability score (RPS) in this script.** `E[sum_{k=0}^{K-2} (F_q(k) - 1{Y <= k})^2]`, with `K = 6`. Lower is better. This sum is **not** divided by `K - 1`. It is not the project's normalized individual-rating RPS on `[0, 1]`. A value such as `1.000` is on this unnormalized scale.
   - **Identity residuals.** `abs diff` is the absolute difference of the two sides of one identity on the same inputs. A value near `1e-16` to `0` is floating-point agreement. The reference scale is the loss itself (order `1`).

3. **What is subtracted.** An `abs diff` column subtracts the two sides of one identity. It does not subtract two different metrics. Where two forecasts are compared, use the same column. On the Part 3c population rows, expected Brier of the collapsed two-point forecast minus expected Brier of the true conditional distribution is `1.4706 - 0.6058 = 0.8648` at the printed precision. Positive means the collapsed forecast has the higher expected Brier score. The same order for unnormalized expected RPS is `1.4007 - 0.8692 = 0.5315`. Printed Pearson correlation and printed expected MSE match exactly across those two forecasts.

4. **Uncertainty.** No interval is computed. `p-val=n/a`. The Monte Carlo table reports the mean over `200` replications of independent and identically distributed ratings, not a confidence interval. The population identities are exact algebraic checks, up to the printed rounding and the Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno (BFGS) numerical tolerance.

5. **How the forecast was chosen.** No judge is trained and no hyperparameter is selected. The truth, the uniform distribution, point masses, two-point laws with a prescribed mean, and the maps `0.1 E[Y|x] + 4` and `5 E[Y|x] - 10` are closed forms. Softmax-parameterized BFGS (`gtol = 1e-12`) is only a numerical check that the expected Brier score is minimized at `p*`. It is not a certificate. The certificates are the Lean theorems in `BrierCorr.lean`. Seed `0`. No model call. Cost `$0`.
