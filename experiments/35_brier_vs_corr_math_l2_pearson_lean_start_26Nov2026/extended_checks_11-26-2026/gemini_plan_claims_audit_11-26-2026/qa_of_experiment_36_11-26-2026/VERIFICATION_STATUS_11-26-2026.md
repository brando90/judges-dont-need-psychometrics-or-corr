# Which findings of the Opus 5.5 max review I checked myself (11-26-2026)

**TLDR:** Brando asked for a QA of Cursor's Experiment 36 and of my own audit with Claude Opus 5.5 at maximum effort. The review (`QA_OPUS55_MAX_REPORT_11-26-2026.md`) is a claim, not a fact, so this note records, finding by finding, whether I reproduced it, confirmed it by reading the committed file, took it from the review's saved output, or did not re-check it. Every headline finding held up: the committed Pearson-trained head is crippled by its penalty, its features carry almost no signal, matched calibration removes most of the proper-score gap, the validation split is never read, and the "pre-registered" sentence cannot be verified from the history. Two statements in the review needed a correction (the HelpSteer2 label and its "5 of 5 sets" correlation sentence), and three numbers it could not check, I did (Kohli's tables, the SummEval expert revision, the POPQUORN pay arithmetic).

**Data and metric behind the numbers.** All numbers are held-out test numbers from the committed code of Experiment 36 at commit `036cafa` on five real multi-annotator rating sets (SummEval expert ratings, ChaosNLI SNLI+MNLI, DICES-990 and two binary views), prompt-grouped 60/20/20 split, seed 0. Normalized ranked probability score (RPS) is the RPS divided by K − 1, lower is better, range 0 to 1; cross-entropy (CE) is in nats, lower is better. Differences always subtract the same metric on the same test items: "Pearson-trained head minus CE-trained head" on a loss, positive means the CE-trained head is better; "CE-trained head minus Pearson-trained head" on correlation, positive means the CE-trained head correlates more. Intervals are 95% percentile intervals of a paired item-clustered bootstrap, B = 1000. The Pearson-trained head maximizes the correlation of a sampled rating (or, in the review's extra arms, of the expected score) with an L2 penalty that was fixed in the committed code or chosen on the validation split, as each item says; the optimizer is L-BFGS-B from a zero start unless an item says otherwise, so a result describes that optimizer, start and budget and not "the best Pearson-trained judge".

**Status vocabulary.** REPRODUCED BY ME: I re-ran or recomputed it and saved the output here. CONFIRMED BY READING: I read the committed code, table or git history and it says what the review says. REVIEW'S SAVED OUTPUT: I read or re-tallied the review's saved result files; I did not re-run its code. NOT RE-CHECKED: the review's statement only.

## A. Experiment 36

1. **The Pearson-trained head is crippled by the shared penalty (review: CONFIRMED). REPRODUCED BY ME.** With the committed penalty of 0.01 the training correlation of a sampled rating is 0.0324 on SummEval experts, 0.0142 on ChaosNLI and 0.0255 on DICES-990; with no penalty it is 0.2497, 0.1514 and 0.1923; 35 and 300 iterations give identical numbers at the committed penalty. Held-out normalized RPS of that head rises as training gets harder, for example on SummEval 0.249, 0.304, 0.4505 and 0.507 for penalties 0.01, 0.001, 0.0001 and 0. (`my_scripts/qa_exp36_fairness_check.py`, output `.out`.) The review's further statement that the same objective value gives very different held-out CE after a longer multi-start run (4.73 versus 14.69 on SummEval) is NOT RE-CHECKED; its output is in `agent_outputs/fair_*.json`.

2. **Matched calibration removes most of the proper-score gap (review: CONFIRMED). REPRODUCED BY ME with my own script, equal to the review's script.** Held-out normalized RPS of the Pearson-trained head minus that of the CE-trained head falls from +0.1088, +0.0566, +0.0603 (SummEval experts, ChaosNLI, DICES-990) to +0.0025 [0.0018, 0.0032], −0.0005 [−0.0012, +0.0002] and +0.0009 [0.0004, 0.0014] once both heads get the same temperature and class offsets fitted on validation CE; the two binary views go from +0.0546 and +0.0727 to +0.0000 and +0.0008. (`my_scripts/qa_exp36_matched_calibration.py`, `agent_outputs/calib2.out`.)

3. **With penalties chosen on validation, correlation is mostly indistinguishable, and a calibration-free comparison still favours the CE-trained head on proper scores. REVIEW'S SAVED OUTPUT, re-tallied by me** (`tally_fair_json.py`, output `tally_fair_json.out`). Over 5 sets × 4 validation-selected Pearson variants = 20 comparisons, the CE-trained head has significantly higher held-out correlation in 4, the Pearson-trained head in 0, and 16 are not distinguishable (one of the 20 has an undefined point estimate because that head's predictions were constant; the saved interval is still reported). On normalized RPS the CE-trained head is significantly better in 20 of 20 before calibration. **Correction to the review's sentence:** "not significant on 4 of 5 sets for the sampled surrogate and 5 of 5 for the expected-score objective" holds when the penalty is chosen on validation correlation; when it is chosen on validation normalized RPS it is 3 of 5 (sampled) and 4 of 5 (expected score).

The intervals are the review's (`agent_outputs/marginal_gain.out`).

## What this means for the thesis

The experiment supports a narrower claim than its headline: a correlation objective leaves the scale and calibration of a forecast unidentified, so an uncalibrated correlation-trained head scores badly on proper scores (20 of 20 validation-selected comparisons), and a small calibration step fitted on validation ratings recovers almost all of it (gaps of at most 0.0025 normalized RPS). It does not show that a CE-trained judge generalizes better than a fairly tuned and calibrated correlation-trained judge on informative features; the features here are nearly signal-free. That reading is consistent with the project's rule that the title and the hypothesis do not establish the outcome.
