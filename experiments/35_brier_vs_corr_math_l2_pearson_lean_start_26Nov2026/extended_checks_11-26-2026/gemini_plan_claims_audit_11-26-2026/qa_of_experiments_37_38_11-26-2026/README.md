Metrics: the held-out test cross-entropy (CE) in nats, lower is better, uniform reference log 6 = 1.7918 on the six-grade FSC scale; "head" means the held-out softmax head Cursor fits (8 or 24 principal components of a hashed bag of tokens, L2 0.01). How a judge was chosen: Cursor's heads are fit by L-BFGS-B at fixed settings (not tuned on the validation split in Experiment 37; temperature only in Experiment 38), so they reflect that optimizer, start and iteration cap. ## Findings, most important first

1. **The FSC splits ignore the sealed splits (both experiments).** The sealed split is per task and lives in the sister repo (`exp27_split`, identical in the 11-24 and 11-26 files for all 50 tasks). Experiment 37 splits 106 items (native main plus warm-up rows) by its own seed: 24 of its 62 training items and 4 of its 20 validation items belong to sealed-test tasks, 12 of its 24 test items belong to sealed-train tasks, and 6 training items sit in tasks that are not in the sealed list. Experiment 38 splits the 100 main-form items by item id: 20 of its 60 training items belong to sealed-test tasks, only 13 of its 20 test items are sealed-test items, and all 20 test items share a task with a training or validation item (16 of 16 test tasks also appear in training). Consequences: neither run is comparable to sister Experiments 27 and 28; a head trained there must never be reused in a sealed evaluation; and Experiment 38 breaks the prompt-level split (no rater of a prompt in two splits) because items of one task straddle splits. Evidence: `my_outputs/exp37_fsc_split_vs_sealed_run1.out`, `my_outputs/exp38_fsc_split_vs_sealed_and_constant_run1.out`.
2. **Neither FSC head beats the train-marginal constant forecast.** Experiment 37 (24 test items): head test CE 1.1245, interval 0.7249 to 1.5686; constant forecast 1.0463, interval 0.7464 to 1.3865. Experiment 38 (20 test items): head 0.4626, interval 0.2924 to 0.6507; constant 0.5951, interval 0.4023 to 0.8166. The intervals overlap widely in both; paired intervals were not computed. The test sets are skewed (share of fives in the test ratings 0.694 in Experiment 37 and 0.840 in Experiment 38), so the constant forecast is the right reference, not uniform log 6. Statements such as "the head carries signal on FSC" are not supported by these runs. Evidence: `my_outputs/exp37_fsc_constant_forecast_run1.out`, `my_outputs/exp38_fsc_split_vs_sealed_and_constant_run1.out`.
3. **Experiment 37 reads a live directory, so its FSC numbers move.** The loader pools every rating file in the shared collection's folder from a hard-coded path (`/tmp/ttj/...`, or `VERIBENCH_FSC_DIR`). At sister commit `f0d0fc3` it sees 262 ratings from 7 raters and the committed numbers reproduce; at `7855746` it sees 265 ratings from 8 raters and the constant-forecast CE on the same split is 0.9789 (interval 0.6802 to 1.3036) instead of 1.0463. Use a frozen snapshot with a commit hash, as Experiment 38 does for its commit, and read the sister repository's frozen anonymized bundle (sorted ratings with rater identity removed, plus `exp27_split` and `cv5_fold`) instead of the live per-rater files.
4. **Experiment 38's "conditional-spread collapse" table is built, not observed.** `conditional_collapse()` in `run_real.py` constructs the "uncollapsed" scalar as the expected rating plus a within-prompt residual that is exactly orthogonal to the centered human ratings, with the judge's predictive variance. Zero covariance with a larger variance gives a lower correlation or kernel alignment by construction. The table's note says so, but its title reads as a measurement on held-out ratings. Label it a constructed illustration of the Lean theorem; Experiment 37's half-split collapse is the observational evidence.
5. **What Lean proves and what it does not.** (a) Experiment 38's matrix results are general (`ckaSq_right_scale_orth` is stated for matrices of any size over the rationals, a nonzero scale and an orthogonal matrix; `linearCKA1d_eq_sqPearson`, `sqPearson_scale_shift`, `center_full`); the reversal and strict-collapse results (`reversal_brier_strict`, `collapse_cka_strict`) are concrete instances, and say so. (b) Experiment 37's canonical-correlation part is an explicit 2-by-2 diagonal instance with four concrete preimages (`shear_preimage`, `sign_flip_preimage`, `swap_preimage`, `mix_preimage`) and a diagonal two-aspect law; invariance of canonical correlations under an arbitrary invertible map is textbook and is checked by numerics only, not by Lean. (c) `admitsFiniteCE` is a predicate (`p = 0 ∨ 0 < q`), not the cross-entropy itself, so "the point mass has infinite cross-entropy" is encoded, not derived from a logarithm. Label each result "general", "instance", "numerics only" or "cited".
6. **The saved Lean evidence is smaller than the Lean.** Experiment 37's `lean_check.txt` states in prose that every named theorem depends only on the three axioms and lists 60 names, but it contains no compiler output for them, and 7 of the 67 theorems are not named. The Lean file itself prints axioms for 35. Experiment 38's record prints axioms for about 19 of 57. The standard asks for the compiler output saved with the proof for the whole file. My appended-print outputs (`my_outputs/exp37_lean_all_axioms_run1.out`, `my_outputs/exp38_lean_all_axioms_run1.out`) show all of them pass, so this is a record fix, not a proof fix.

## Smaller points

1. The wrong expansion "functional semantic correctness" for FSC appears in Experiment 37 (`PROTOCOL.md`, `run_real.py`, `REPORT.md`), Experiment 38 (`results.json`, `run_real.py`) and Experiment 36. The sister repo's expansion is "formal specification coverage (FSC)" (92 files); it never uses the other for the ratings. The plan Brando pasted calls it "CertJudge/FSC R_v1", which does not give an expansion.
2. Experiment 38 splits ChaosNLI by item id; Experiment 29 groups by premise (713 test items in 659 premise groups). Use the premise so two hypotheses of one premise cannot straddle splits.
3. Experiment 38's canonical-correlation sums of squares are quoted without the SciPy or NumPy version. Either raise the iteration cap until the heads converge, or print the environment beside those numbers.
4. Experiment 37 and 38's heads are fitted at fixed settings, and Pearson-trained heads are not compared with CE-trained heads here, so neither experiment tests the project's central comparison on real data. They test the invariance claims. Say so in the READMEs.
5. Everything from my Experiment 36 audit that Cursor has not yet applied still applies to these experiments where relevant: validation-based hyperparameter selection, a matched Pearson head with the same calibration as the CE head, normalized RPS and total variation distance beside CE, paired intervals for every comparison, and the k ceilings (FSC 2 to 4 ratings per item, SummEval 3, POPQUORN 3 to 10).

## A check I ran that did not find a problem

Does splitting FSC items by item id instead of by task inflate the head's apparent skill? I repeated 5-fold cross-validation 30 times on the 60 FSC items whose tasks are sealed train or validation (15 tasks; no sealed-test item touched), once folding by item and once folding by task, with Cursor's own feature and head code, and compared the head to the add-one train-marginal constant forecast. Item folds: head test CE minus constant test CE is -0.0031 nats (standard deviation over repeats 0.1642; item-bootstrap 95% interval -0.1608 to +0.2281). Task folds: +0.0246 (0.1318; interval -0.0873 to +0.1959). Positive means the head is worse. Both intervals contain zero, so I measured no inflation; with 60 items this check cannot exclude a small one. Script and outputs: `my_scripts/e38_leakage_cv.py`, `my_outputs/exp38_leakage_item_vs_task_cv_run1.out` (identical on two runs).
