# How to read the results of experiment 35 (extended checks)

**Data.** Everything is synthetic or exact arithmetic. No human ratings are used, so there is no train/test split of human data and no prior exposure. Checks N1 to N21 and N25 are exact identities, exact rational counterexamples or seeded random instances (seeds fixed in `check_math.py`). Only the training demonstration (N22 to N24, figure 2, `results/q4_training_tvd_vs_n.csv`) has a split: N training ratings, one rating per synthetic item with x uniform on [-2, 2], and 4,000 fresh held-out items with known true distributions. The Pearson plus recalibration arm fits the Pearson judge on 70 percent of the N ratings and the affine map on the other 30 percent; the other arms use all N.

**Metrics.** Brier score: the sum over the K categories of the squared gap between forecast and one-hot rating; range 0 to 2, lower is better; for two categories this is twice the squared error of the probability. Cross-entropy (CE): negative log probability of the observed rating in nats, lower is better. Ranked probability score (RPS): the Brier score of the cumulative distribution divided by K - 1, range 0 to 1, lower is better. Total variation distance (TVD) to the true distribution: half the sum of absolute probability gaps, range 0 to 1, lower is better; the CE- and Brier-trained arms reach about 0.004 at N = 100,000 (printed in the output). Root mean squared error (RMSE) of the mean rating: in rating points on the 0-to-5 scale, lower is better. Pearson correlation: higher is better, range -1 to 1.

**Comparisons.** No differences between two metrics are ever taken. Counterexamples compare the same metric on the same items for two named judges (for example, the expected Brier score of judge C against that of judge D). Figure 2 compares held-out TVD across objectives at equal N.

**Uncertainty.** Figure 2 shows the median and the min-to-max range over 6 runs (3 random initializations times 2 data seeds); no confidence intervals are claimed.

**How each judge was chosen.** Model: softmax over ratings 0 to 5 with logits a_r + b_r x (r = 0 fixed at zero; 10 parameters). Objectives: mean CE, mean Brier, mean squared error of the forecast mean against the numeric rating, and minus the Pearson correlation of the forecast mean with the rating, all on training ratings. Optimizer: scipy L-BFGS-B with analytic gradients from Normal(0, 1) starts, at most 2,000 iterations (500 for Pearson). CE is convex in these parameters, so its fit reflects the data; Brier, MSE and Pearson are not convex, so their results reflect this optimizer, these starts and this budget, and for Pearson the level and scale are by construction set by the start and the optimizer (DERIVATIONS.md, C13). The best judge is not guaranteed for the non-convex objectives.
