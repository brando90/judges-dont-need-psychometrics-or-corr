# Experiment 61 PROTOCOL: post-hoc convention and comparator checks for Experiments 58, 59 and 60

Written 11-28-2026 (Pacific), in a commit that precedes every run of `scripts/run_exp61.py`. CPU only, no model calls, API spend $0.

## Label: POST HOC and descriptive
These are NOT pre-registered hypotheses. They answer three questions that independent reviewers raised after Experiments 57 to 60 had been run and committed (Codex review, Fable 5.2 review, Opus 5.5 paper review, all 11-28-2026). No decision rule, no threshold and no "supported/falsified" label is defined here. The committed results of Experiments 57 to 60, and three review reports that state the issues below in words. ## Data (read-only, shared with Experiments 57 to 60)
The frozen bundle read by `experiments/57_*/scripts/frozen.py`: five frozen frontier-judge forecast sets (GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5, Kimi K3, GLM 5.3, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, temperature 0) and the individual human ratings of ChaosNLI SNLI (600 items), MNLI-m (600), alphaNLI (400), DICES-350 (350) and DICES-990 (400). Primary strictly proper score per set as in Experiments 57 to 60: Brier for the NLI sets and alphaNLI, normalized ranked probability score (RPS) for DICES.

## C1: how much does Experiment 60's "collapsed forecast" depend on how a mean becomes a forecast?
Experiment 60 scored a forecast that is a point mass at the grid label nearest the half-A mean rating ("rounded mean", frozen there). A judge that outputs the conditional mean has no unique forecast, so this check scores four ways of turning the half-A rating law of each item into a forecast, on the same 200 half-splits as Experiment 60 (seed `SeedSequence([20261128, 60, set_index, split])`, via Experiment 60's own `split_once`) and the same item-clustered bootstrap weights (4,000 draws, seed `SeedSequence([20261128, 60, 99, set_index])`):
1. `rounded_mean`: point mass at the grid label nearest the half-A mean, halves round up (Experiment 60's frozen convention; must reproduce its skill and interval exactly).
2. `modal_label`: point mass at the most frequent half-A label (ties go to the lowest label).
3. `median_label`: point mass at the lowest label whose half-A cumulative probability reaches 0.5.
4. `mean_preserving_two_point`: probability 1 - t on the label floor(mu) and t on the next label, with t = mu - floor(mu), so the forecast has the same mean as the half-A law and the least spread on the grid. With two labels this is exactly the truthful forecast (a code check).
Each is floored at 1e-6 like every forecast. Reported per set: skill over the half-A base rate on half B for the truthful forecast, the four collapsed forecasts and the base rate, with percentile 95% intervals; skill difference (truthful minus each collapsed forecast, same score, same half B); and, on the two three-label DICES sets, the share of items whose rounded-mean label and whose modal label is the middle label ("Unsure"), averaged over splits. Skill is 1 minus risk over the base-rate risk (0 is the base rate, higher is better).

## C2: how much of the hard-label error in Experiment 58 is a gap between two human quantities?
Experiment 58 compared each judge's pass-rate estimate with the human rater-share rate (the mean over items of the share of human ratings in the event). A hard-label estimator counts items whose most likely label is in the event, which estimates a different quantity: the share of items whose human majority label is in the event. Both are real, different properties of the human ratings. This check measures the difference using only the human ratings and the same definitions as Experiment 58 (`hard_label_share`, ties in the most likely label split equally; the same events: primary for all sets, secondary "Unsure or Yes" for DICES). For every set and event:
1. the human rater-share rate, the human majority rate and their difference, which is the hard-label error of a perfect judge (a judge whose forecast equals each item's human distribution), with an item-clustered bootstrap 95% interval (SNLI items that share an image are one cluster; 4,000 draws; seed `SeedSequence([20261128, 61, 1, set_index])`);
2. the share of items whose human most likely label is tied;
3. for each of the five judges: the hard-label rate, its error against the rater-share rate (Experiment 58's quantity) and its error against the human majority rate, with intervals from the same draws; and the number of the 25 primary (set, judge) cells in which the absolute error against the majority rate is smaller than against the rater-share rate.

## C3: Experiment 59's selection paths against a uniformly random pick
Experiment 59 compared three ways of choosing one of the five judges from n labeled items: A (highest Pearson correlation), B (lowest three-fold cross-validated recalibrated proper risk), C (lowest raw proper risk), by regret against the oracle choice. It had no do-nothing comparator. This check reruns the identical 4,000 draws (same seeds `SeedSequence([20261128, 59, set_index, n_index, draw])`, same code path, copied from `run_exp59.py` with one addition: it also records the five judges' calibrated test risks) and adds the regret of choosing a judge uniformly at random, which is the mean over the five judges of their regret (no randomness). It asserts that the recomputed picks and regrets of A, B and C equal the committed `draws.csv` of Experiment 59 exactly. Reported per (set, labeled-item budget n in {25, 50, 100, 200}): mean regret of A, B, C and of the random pick in percentage points of skill (regret is divided by the base-rate test risk), and the paired mean difference of each path minus the random pick with a percentile 95% interval from 4,000 bootstrap draws over the 200 pool/test splits (seed `SeedSequence([20261128, 61, 2, set_index, n_index])`; this covers the choice of the labeled pool, not item sampling, as in Experiment 59). Across the 20 (set, n) cells: how many have a path's mean regret above the random pick's, and how many of those have the interval above 0.
