# How to read the Experiment 61 results

This experiment is POST HOC and descriptive: three checks that three reviewers asked for after Experiments 58, 59 and 60 had run. It has no decision rules and changes no earlier result. Every number is printed by `scripts/run_exp61.py` into `results/`; the script first reproduces Experiment 60's frozen skills and all 4,000 draws of Experiment 59.

1. **Which data.** The frozen bundle of public multi-annotator rating sets of Experiments 46 to 60: ChaosNLI SNLI (600 items) and MNLI-m (600), each with exactly 100 human ratings per item; ChaosNLI alphaNLI (400 items, 100 ratings each); DICES-350 (350 items, 123 ratings each) and DICES-990 (400 items, 69 to 76 ratings each). All ratings were seen in earlier experiments (prior exposure). Nothing is fitted in C1 and C2. In C3 the draws are Experiment 59's: five frontier judges at temperature 0 and in-domain human labels; in each draw the item groups are split at random into a pool and a test set of half each, and n items from the pool with one randomly drawn rating each are the labeled items; no judge call is made here.
2. **Which metric.**
   - C1 (`results/exp60_conventions.csv`): skill = 1 minus (risk of a forecast over the risk of the base-rate forecast) on the held-out half B of each item's ratings; 0 is the base rate, 1 is perfect, higher is better. Risk is the unbiased per-sample proper score matched to the labels: Brier score on alphaNLI (two labels; range 0 to 2) and normalized ranked probability score (RPS; the squared distance between the cumulative forecast and the cumulative rating divided by the number of labels minus one; range 0 to 1) on DICES. The base rate is the pooled half-A histogram. The collapsed forecast is a point mass or a two-label forecast built from the half-A rating law of the item; four conventions are shown because a mean does not fix a forecast.
   - C2 (`results/exp58_estimand_gap.csv`, `results/exp58_hard_vs_majority.csv`): rates and errors in percentage points. The human rater-share rate is the mean over items of the share of ratings in the event; the human majority rate is the share of items whose most likely human label is in the event (a tie in the top label is split equally). The perfect-judge hard-label error is the majority rate minus the rater-share rate: the error of a hard-label judge that knows each item's human distribution exactly.
   - C3 (`results/exp59_random_baseline.csv`, `results/exp59_draw_risks.csv`): regret = the skill lost by not picking the judge with the best calibrated test risk, in percentage points of skill in the tables and on the 0-to-1 scale in the CSV columns `regret_*` (CSV value times 100 is the table value); lower is better. "Random pick" is the mean regret of the five judges (a uniformly random choice among them).
3. **What is subtracted.** "Truthful minus collapsed" in C1 is the same proper score on the same half B for two forecasts of the same item; positive means the truthful forecast is better. In C2, majority rate minus rater-share rate subtracts two human rates of the same event on the same items; negative means a perfect hard-label judge reads lower than the raters' share. In C3, "path minus random" subtracts the mean regret of two selection rules on the same draws (the same labeled pool and test set); positive means the path is worse than picking one of the five judges at random. Path A chooses the judge with the highest Pearson correlation with the labeled ratings; path B the lowest three-fold cross-validated recalibrated proper risk on them; path C the lowest raw proper risk on them (a sensitivity run of Experiment 59).
4. **Uncertainty.** 95% percentile intervals. C1: 4,000 item-clustered bootstrap draws, each statistic averaged over Experiment 60's 200 random half-splits. C2: 4,000 bootstrap draws over item groups (SNLI items that share an image share a group). C3: Experiment 59's 4,000 draws, each a random split of the groups into a labeled pool and a test set, paired across paths; the interval covers the random split into pool and test set and the draw of the labeled items, not the sampling of items from a population (it is not an item-clustered interval).
5. **How the judges were chosen.** Nothing is fitted in C1 and C2 (the forecasts are empirical rating laws, floored at 1e-6 for the proper scores). In C3 the five judges are Experiment 59's, each recalibrated by the Experiment 46 map fitted on the n labeled single ratings and scored on the test items using all their ratings, the oracle is the judge with the lowest calibrated test risk, and the selection paths are exactly Experiment 59's; this experiment only adds the random comparator and recomputes each path's regret from the stored per-judge test risks.

What each result may be used for. C1 says how large the gap between conventions is on real rating laws and that the point-mass forecast of Experiment 60 is below the base rate under all four; it does not say which convention a deployed judge uses. C2 says that part of every hard-label error on DICES against the rater-share rate is a difference between two human quantities; it does not say a hard-label judge is unbiased. C3 says that Experiment 59's pre-registered proper-score selector was worse than a random pick in 11 of 20 cells, so its 20-of-20 loss to correlation selection is partly about that selector; it does not say a proper score cannot select a judge well (the raw proper score, path C, did better than correlation on all eight DICES cells).
