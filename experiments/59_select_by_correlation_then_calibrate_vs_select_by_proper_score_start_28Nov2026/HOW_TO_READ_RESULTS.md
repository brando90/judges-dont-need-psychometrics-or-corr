# How to read the Experiment 59 results

1. **Which data.** The five frozen frontier judges and five public multi-annotator rating sets of Experiment 57. Each of 200 draws per (set, label budget) splits the groups of a set at random into a pool and a test set of half the groups each. Labels (one human rating per labeled item) come only from the pool; every test number is on test items held out from selection and calibration.
   The ratings of these sets were seen in earlier work (Experiments 28, 46, 48, 55, 56), as the protocol states. Label budgets are 25, 50, 100 and 200 items; on DICES-350 the pool has only 175 items, so the largest budget there is 175 (amendment A1).
2. **Which metric.** Regret: the calibrated test risk of the judge a path chose, minus the calibrated test risk of the best of the five judges for the same labeled set, divided by the risk of the pool's human base-rate forecast on the test items. Risk is the unbiased per-sample proper risk (Brier score on the NLI sets, normalized ranked probability score on DICES), lower is better.
   Regret is in skill units and is shown in percentage points of skill (times 100); 0 means the path chose the best judge or one as good. Skill is one minus risk divided by the base-rate risk.
3. **What is subtracted.** "A minus B" is the regret of selecting by correlation (A) minus the regret of selecting by cross-validated calibrated proper score (B) on the same 200 draws; positive means B is better, negative means A is better. In the sensitivity columns, "A minus C" and "B minus C" use path C, selection by the raw proper score, in the same way.
   The pre-registered comparison is A minus B only; the C columns were not part of any decision.
4. **Uncertainty.** Percentile 95% intervals from 4,000 bootstrap draws over the 200 splits, one weight matrix per cell shared by all paths (paired). They describe variation over labeled pools and splits given these items, not sampling of items from a population. Equivalence is judged against a margin of 0.5 points of skill fixed in the protocol before any result.
5. **How the judge was chosen.** That is the object of the experiment: path A takes the highest Pearson correlation with the single labeled ratings (ties and undefined values go to the fixed order Astra, Opus, Kimi, GLM, DeepSeek); path B the lowest three-fold cross-validated proper risk of the calibrated forecasts on the labeled items; path C the lowest raw proper risk on them.
   The calibration map is the Experiment 46 multinomial logit on [log forecast, 1] with a ridge of 1e-3 toward the identity, fitted by L-BFGS-B from the identity; it is the answer of that optimizer on the labeled items, not a certified optimum.

Decisions are in `results/hypotheses.json`: H1 falsified (proper-score selection has lower regret in 0 of 5 sets at n = 25), H2 falsified (DICES-990 differs by 1.02 points at n = 200, correlation better), H3 "none". The figure (`results/regret_figure.png`): top row, mean regret of the three paths against the number of labeled items; bottom row, the paired difference A minus B with its 95% interval (below the zero line means correlation selection is better; dotted lines are the 0.5-point equivalence margin).
