## Training and scoring on UNT

Each fold holds out all 16 answers of one homework. Its other nine assignments supply 144 answers and 288 ratings. Within every fold generate 200 independent seeded nested training orders (NumPy SeedSequence [20261121, held-out assignment number, repetition index 0–199]); randomize the order of answers within each training assignment and balance the first 1–9 selections over randomly ordered training assignments, reshuffling the assignment order every round. For one-rating fits choose the grader independently by a seeded fair coin per answer. Use identical cases across methods and judges. The primary two-rating condition uses 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 or 128 distinct answers to give exactly 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128 or 256 training ratings. A secondary one-rating condition uses 4 through 128 answers at the same powers-of-two budgets.

Reuse experiment 19’s 27 transformations. Smooth six input probabilities by mixing with uniform with weight 0.000006. Each transformed probability is proportional to the smoothed probability raised to t times exp(bu+cu²), for u=(rating−2.5)/2.5, t in {0.5,1,2} and b,c in {−1,0,1}. Exhaustively minimize mean individual-rating cross-entropy (CE) or maximize Pearson correlation between the predicted and observed mean ratings across training answers. Use the grid in order of squared parameter distance to identity, then lexicographic order, with ties within 1e−12; a wholly undefined Pearson fit returns identity. The grid, probability smoothing and tie order are fixed, with no separately selected hyperparameters. Compare both fits with the uniform distribution, the original smoothed judge and the training histogram with 0.5 pseudocount per bin. Neither judge’s weights change.

Compute normalized individual-rating ranked probability score (RPS) as the average squared cumulative probability error over five thresholds, 0–1. Test CE is negative natural log probability of the observed grade, in nats. Test total variation distance (TVD) is half the sum of absolute differences from the empirical two-grader histogram per answer, 0–1. Lower is better for all. Compute the two proper scores separately for both raters, averaging within each answer. Average all metrics equally over answers, training draws and homework assignments. Include the two baselines' absolute scores and both calibration methods at every budget, selection counts and failures. Report Pearson and Spearman between predicted and observed mean grades as diagnostic values with defined counts.

## What qualifies as beating both baselines

For each judge use the primary two-rating design and test descending budgets 256, 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4 in that order. In each of ten held-out assignments compute two paired test RPS differences, comparator minus CE fit, averaging the 200 label draws. Compare the lower one-sided 97.5% bound with zero for each difference, with one-sided alpha 0.025 per judge (two fixed judges). Use the fold-overlap-adjusted t approximation with 9 degrees of freedom and standard error equal to the standard deviation over ten fold differences times sqrt(1/10 + 1/9). A budget passes only if both differences versus Pearson and versus uniform pass. Stop formal claims for a judge at its first failure; report all point estimates and local results beyond that budget descriptively. A pass does not imply that unsampled intermediate budgets pass. Report an additional training-histogram comparison to decide whether baseline grading frequencies explain the apparent win. A lower bound above zero versus the histogram at a jointly passing budget is an additional descriptive check.

These are approximate bounds conditional on one course and two graders; t coverage is not guaranteed with ten nonrandom, interdependent homework folds. Give the 1,024 fold-sign permutations as a sensitivity under symmetric fold differences (also affected by overlap), fold win counts and variation across training draws. The repeated draws are never counted as independent families. CE, TVD, the one-rating condition, individual-model effects and post-hoc analyses are descriptive.
