# When does cross-entropy calibration beat Pearson and uniform on fresh task families? | 11-21-2026

As of 11-21-2026, **Qwen3.5-9B passed at every tested training budget, starting at four human ratings. Claude Sonnet 5 passed at no budget under the prespecified descending test.** Both results concern the ranked probability score (RPS) of grade distributions on held-out computer-science homework assignments. Both foundation models stayed frozen. We calibrated their six reported grade probabilities. ## What the four-rating result buys

Two graders each scored 160 student answers to 67 questions in one University of North Texas course. In each of ten folds, one entire 16-answer homework assignment was reserved for testing. For every training draw, the open and closed judges received identical grading messages. Both fitting objectives exhaustively searched the same 27 calibration settings, with ties going to the setting closest to the original. Each of the 200 draws per homework used the same training ratings for both judges and objectives. A training budget of four means two distinct answers graded twice. The corpus was previously unused in this project. Potential public-data exposure during model pretraining is unknown. Lower is better for all three test metrics. Normalized individual-rating RPS averages squared cumulative probability error over five grade thresholds (0–1). Cross-entropy (CE) is the average negative log probability assigned to the observed grade, in nats. Total variation distance (TVD) is half the sum of absolute differences from each answer’s empirical two-grader distribution (0–1). A difference in the primary comparison subtracts the CE fit’s test RPS from the comparator’s test RPS on identical answers. Each interval below is a nominal 95% overlap-adjusted paired t interval across ten homework assignments. The 200 training draws quantify variability within an assignment; they do not supply additional independent families. [All budgets, absolute scores, four comparators, and uncertainty](short_answers/RESULTS_11-21-2026.md)

- **Qwen.** At four ratings, RPS improved by 0.0715 against the Pearson fit (95% interval 0.0502 to 0.0927) and 0.0949 against uniform (0.0541 to 0.1356). The joint criterion passed at 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128 and 256. However, the single training grade histogram achieved lower observed RPS than CE-calibrated Qwen at all seven budgets, and uniform achieved lower test CE throughout. Of 320 held-out grades, 204 were the top grade of 5, which makes grade frequencies a meaningful comparator. At 256 grades the Pearson fit actually scored higher on held-out mean-rating correlation (0.6198 versus 0.5556 for the CE fit), while predicting an average grade of 2.79 compared with the human 4.31. The evaluation metric reversed which fit looked better.
- **Claude.** At four ratings, local RPS improvements were 0.0315 against the Pearson fit (0.0184 to 0.0445) and 0.1503 against uniform (0.1129 to 0.1877). Both local bounds favored the CE fit at every tested budget from 4 through 128. At 256 ratings, the Pearson comparison was 0.000141 (−0.000083 to 0.000365). Because the confirmatory test started at 256, that result stopped the sequence. Local comparisons against the training histogram also favored the CE fit at 4, 8, 16 and 32 ratings. The small-budget Claude pattern is descriptive. At 256 ratings both objectives chose exactly the same calibration setting in 1,981 of 2,000 Claude fits. [Paired calibration settings and the reason for convergence](short_answers/HEAD_AGREEMENT_11-21-2026.md)
- **At the smallest budget**, two distinct training means give Pearson only two possible defined values, +1 and −1. Multiple calibration settings can tie. All 27 correlations were undefined in 623/2,000 Qwen fits and 597/2,000 Claude fits at four ratings, triggering the preregistered original-setting fallback. At 32 ratings the corresponding counts were zero for both. In the separate one-grade-per-answer analysis the CE fit had lower observed RPS than both comparators at all six budgets (4–128) for both models. ## Fresh VeriBench forecasts

We also ran the matched judges on all 100 fresh VeriBench artifacts, covering 25 source tasks, and 75 historical artifacts. Only eligible historical training/validation items supplied training grades. The current 34 main sheets held zero final grades across 3,400 assigned rows when checked. Native and calibrated forecasts were frozen before fresh ratings became available. The strict prospective panel uses only historical training families judged disjoint from the test operations. The held-out losses and a rating threshold for VeriBench await released human ratings.
