# Why the two fitting objectives converged for one judge | 11-21-2026

As of 11-21-2026, this post hoc analysis replays the primary two-grader condition at the smallest and largest budgets. Each of ten held-out homework assignments contributes 200 matched draws, for 2,000 fits per judge and budget. Every fit searches the same 27 transformations of the fixed judge probabilities. These descriptive counts reuse the observed training draws and provide no independent replication or new hypothesis test. [Protocol](../PROTOCOL_11-21-2026.md) · [Primary results and intervals](RESULTS_11-21-2026.md)

Of the 320 individual grades on the 160 sampled University of North Texas (UNT) answers, 204 (63.75%) were 5. The mean was 4.30625 on the 0–5 scale. Both fitted means remained below this level. At 256 training grades, the Qwen cross-entropy (CE) fit predicted a held-out mean of 3.6668 and the Pearson fit predicted 2.7938. Their held-out Pearson correlations with the two graders’ answer means ran in the opposite order, 0.5556 and 0.6198. For Claude the two fitted means were 3.9471 and 3.9463, and correlations were 0.8142 and 0.8140. Correlations here first compare answer means within each held-out homework, then average across defined draws and homework assignments. [Absolute diagnostics](results/diagnostics.csv)

Every count is out of 2,000. The coefficient tuple gives the probability exponent, linear grade tilt, and quadratic grade tilt. The identity tuple is (1, 0, 0). The shared (0.5, +1, +1) head reduces the native log probabilities’ contribution and applies both tilts. At 256 grades, Qwen’s most frequent Pearson head was (1, −1, −1), selected 887 times. [Complete head-selection frequencies](results/selections.csv)

**Four grades provide only two points for training Pearson.** Two graders score each of two distinct training answers drawn from different homework assignments. When both human means and both predicted means differ, Pearson is exactly +1 or −1 for that head according to their order. Tied human means occurred on 583 of the 2,000 draws. With distinct human means, all 27 heads nevertheless predicted constant means across the two answers on another 40 Qwen draws and 14 Claude draws. The identity fallback therefore applied on 623/2,000 Qwen fits (31.15%) and 597/2,000 Claude fits (29.85%). Even when Pearson was defined, multiple heads tied for best on 1,360/1,377 Qwen fits and 1,396/1,403 Claude fits. The frozen rule favors the head closest to identity and then uses lexicographic order. [Undefined fits](results/undefined_pearson.csv) · [Paired replay](results/head_agreement.csv)

**At 256 grades, identical choices explain Claude’s near-zero test-score gap.** Test ranked probability score (RPS) averages squared cumulative probability error at five grade thresholds across the two individual grades and the held-out answers. It ranges from 0 to 1, lower is better. On 1,981/2,000 Claude draws the two fitted heads were identical and the difference was exactly zero. On the remaining 19 draws the Pearson-fitted minus CE-fitted test RPS averaged +0.014841. Averaging over all draws gave +0.000141. Qwen selected different heads in all 2,000 draws, with an average difference of +0.118652. At four grades the corresponding overall differences were +0.031473 for Claude and +0.071450 for Qwen. The sparse two-answer ranking comparison and the fixed transformation grid explain features of this specific experiment. These counts do not establish a threshold for other courses, grade distributions or judges. [Paired differences and intervals](results/contrasts.csv)
