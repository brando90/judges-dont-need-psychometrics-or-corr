# Experiment 18 results: when does CE win over correlation? (11-21-2026) — QA corrections 11-26-2026

**QA correction (11-26-2026):** Read the [dated correction and evidence limits](QA_CORRECTIONS_11-26-2026.md) before the historical text below; original scored tables remain unchanged.

Primary test metrics: RPS, CE excess (KL to the true distribution), TVD; Pearson/Spearman only as secondary. "CE wins" = 95% bootstrap interval over seeds of the paired difference is above 0 on both RPS and CE excess (rule fixed in README before any cell ran). Tools: Claude Code (Sonnet 5.5) wrote and ran everything; sweeps ran on skampere1 (CPU only, $0, no model calls).

## Answer first
1. **CE (and RPS) win whenever the judge has to output a distribution and enough data exist.** A judge trained on Pearson correlation never learns the distribution: its test RPS stays flat as n grows (figures/fig_A_rps_vs_n.png), it is worse than the constant forecast in 36/36 cells at n=512 (34/36 at n=256, 12/36 at n=8), and CE-trained beats it in 36/36 cells at n>=256 and 31/36 at n=64.
2. **It depends on data amount and rater noise.** Crossover n (smallest n from which CE-trained beats Pearson-trained at all larger n, median over 12 scenario x J combos): 16 with 10 ratings per item, 32 with 3, 64 with 1. Below n=16 the verdict is mostly "mixed" (Pearson-trained has lower RPS but worse CE: 25/36 cells at n=8). More judge flexibility moves the crossover up a little (median 32, 32, 48 for J=1, 3, 6).
3. **Not every Pearson arm loses.** Pearson-select-then-calibrate (fit the score by correlation, then an affine head by RPS, Sanmi's point 2) beats CE-trained at n>=128 when the rating spread is a function of the mean (scenarios C, D at n=512: head wins 14/18 cells, 4 mixed, CE none), and loses when spread varies independently of the mean (scenarios A, B at n=512: CE wins 13/18, 5 mixed; A all 9 cells, B at J=6 all 3). So the gain is from fitting the shape, and CE is one way to get it; a calibrated 1-D head is enough if shape depends on the mean only.
4. **Selecting one judge from a fixed pool (toy, 24 candidates differing in calibration):** CE or RPS selection beats Pearson selection with native probabilities (60/60 cells at n>=16); with 3-vote histograms 23/60 CE, 24 tie, 13 mixed, 0 Pearson better. This pool differs in calibration by construction, which favors CE; on the real pools below it does not hold.
5. **Real human ratings (75 artifacts, 25 tasks, 195 expert ratings, 400 task-grouped splits, 4 to 16 training tasks):** (a) fitted mixtures: CE and RPS mixtures beat the Pearson mixture at every training size (RPS difference +0.048 to +0.090 for the 44-judge pool, both floors, significant in 8/8 settings; +0.003 to +0.019 for the 5-judge pool, significant at 4 and 8 tasks only); (b) single-judge selection: Pearson selection is as good as or better than CE selection (never significantly worse; significantly better for the 44-judge pool with floor 1e-6 at 8, 12, 16 tasks, RPS -0.034 to -0.037; the one CE win is the 44-judge pool, floor 5e-2, 4 tasks, +0.011). The gap in Pearson's favor grows with n_train in all four single-judge settings (it plateaus after 8 tasks for the 44-judge pool, floor 1e-6).
6. **Local Codex run (its Doc, read by me; numbers not re-run by me):** CE selection of 1 of 15 judges beat Pearson selection on held-out RPS at all six annotation budgets (3 families, 1 rating: 0.1500 vs 0.1831; difference +0.0331 [0.0168, 0.0573]; 15 families, 2 ratings: +0.0174 [-0.0226, 0.0327]); its simulation had Pearson win at 8 artifacts x 1 rating when the tie-break matched the truth. That disagrees with my 5(b) for single-judge selection: different pool (15 vs 44 or 5 judges), family-based splits and annotation subsampling. Not reconciled.

## Limits
- Sweeps A, B are synthetic (Beta-Binomial scenarios A-D from experiments 08, 11); 20 seeds per A cell, 100 per B cell; intervals are over seeds, not over real tasks.
- "CE-trained" here uses a ridge chosen by CV RPS (a tuned fit), so it is partly an RPS-selected model; plain unregularized CE would do worse at small n (see experiment 11).
- The real-data sweep subsets the 20 training tasks to 4 to 16; the n=20 point is experiment 16. Up to 39 of 400 splits per setting have optimizer-certificate failures for the CE mixture (up to 29 for the RPS mixture, 0 for the Pearson mixture); counts are in each results.json and launcher.log.
- Pearson/Spearman are in summary_A.csv and summary_B.csv as secondary columns; CE-trained is within 0.02 Spearman of Pearson-trained in 81% of cells.

TLDR-end: [Corr vs CE] CE/RPS win once there is data (crossover n about 16/32/64 for 10/3/1 ratings per item) and always on distribution metrics; Pearson-then-calibrate ties or beats CE when spread follows the mean, and on real data single-judge selection by Pearson is not worse than by CE.
