## What correlation can and cannot learn, and WHEN (11-21-2026)

**Bottom line.** Correlation (Corr) reads one number per artifact: the judge's mean rating $`m_i`$, and only up to a shift and a positive rescaling. Whether that costs anything compared with cross-entropy (CE) depends on three things: the rating scale ($`K`$), the model class (how much freedom it has beyond the mean) and the amount of data. In the population limit Corr loses level and scale always, and shape whenever $`K\ge 3`$ and the class has free shape; with little data the order can flip, because CE has more to estimate. "Derived" below means proved here (by hand, and numerically spot-checked where stated); "measured" means read off experiment 19 or 20; nothing is both unless said.

**Notation.** Ratings $`r\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}`$, item $`i=1,\dots,n`$, judge distribution $`p_i`$, target distribution $`p^\ast_i`$, means $`m_i=\sum_r r\,p_i(r)`$ and $`t_i=\sum_r r\,p^\ast_i(r)`$, $`\tilde t_i=t_i-\bar t`$. A model class is a set $`\mathcal Q=\{q_\theta\}`$; $`M(\theta)=(m_\theta(x_1),\dots,m_\theta(x_n))`$ is its mean map.

### W1. Binary ratings ($`K=2`$): the mean is the distribution

With $`r\in\{0,1\}`$: $`m_i=0\cdot p_i(0)+1\cdot p_i(1)=p_i(1)`$ and $`p_i(0)=1-m_i`$. So $`p_i`$ is a function of $`m_i`$; the fibre $`\{p:\sum_r r\,p(r)=m\}`$ is a single point ($`K-2=0`$ shape directions). By R2 the Corr-optimal set is $`m_i=a+bt_i`$ with $`b>0`$ and $`m_i\in[0,1]`$; CE (and RPS, which for $`K=2`$ equals $`(F_p(0)-F_{p^\ast}(0))^2=(m_i-t_i)^2`$, the Brier score) is minimized only at $`m_i=t_i`$. Hence Corr loses *nothing about shape*; it loses level and scale only, and the cost of that is

```math
\text{excess Brier}=\frac1n\sum_i\big(a+(b-1)t_i\big)^2,\qquad
\text{excess CE}=\frac1n\sum_i \mathrm{KL}\big(\mathrm{Bern}(t_i)\,\Vert\,\mathrm{Bern}(a+bt_i)\big).
```

*Example (computed).* $`t=(0.2,0.5,0.8)`$, $`a=0.3`$, $`b=0.5`$: $`m=(0.4,0.55,0.7)`$, $`\rho=1`$ exactly, yet excess Brier $`=(0.2^2+0.05^2+0.1^2)/3=0.0175`$ and excess CE $`=0.0408`$ nats.

### W2. Three or more levels: shape is free at fixed mean

For $`K\ge3`$ the fibre of the mean map at an interior point is a polytope of dimension $`K-2`$ (unknowns $`p(0),\dots,p(K-1)`$; two linear constraints: $`\sum p=1`$, $`\sum r\,p=m`$). *Example, $`K=3`$, $`m=1`$.* Put $`s=p(2)`$. Then $`p(1)=m-2s=1-2s`$ and $`p(0)=1-m+s=s`$, so $`p=(s,\,1-2s,\,s)`$ for $`s\in[0,\tfrac12]`$ and $`\mathrm{Var}=\sum_r (r-1)^2p(r)=2s`$, from $`0`$ (the point mass at 1) to $`1`$ (half at 0, half at 2). Every $`s`$ has mean 1, so Corr cannot tell them apart. The cumulative distribution is $`F=(s,\,1-s)`$ at the two thresholds, so the (unnormalized) RPS between the point mass ($`s=0`$) and the split ($`s=\tfrac12`$) is $`(0-\tfrac12)^2+(1-\tfrac12)^2=\tfrac12`$. With $`n`$ items this is $`n(K-2)`$ free shape dimensions plus $`(a,b)`$, which is R2.

### W3. Model class: a one-parameter family per item (shape $`=f(\text{mean})`$)

Let $`q_\eta(r)\propto e^{\eta r}`$ (tilted-uniform; binomial and similar families behave the same way). Its log-partition $`A(\eta)=\log\sum_r e^{\eta r}`$ is convex with $`A'(\eta)=m(\eta)`$ strictly increasing, so $`\eta\leftrightarrow m`$ is a bijection and $`\mathrm{Var}_\eta=A''(\eta)=V(m)`$ is a function of the mean: shape is tied to the mean.

1. *Corr, free $`\eta_i`$ per item.* Optimal set $`m_i=a+bt_i`$, i.e. $`\eta_i=A'^{-1}(a+bt_i)`$: level and scale free, shape tied.
2. *CE, any target $`p^\ast`$ (inside the family or not).* $`\mathrm{KL}(p^\ast\Vert q_\eta)=\text{const}-\eta\,t+A(\eta)`$, whose derivative $`-t+A'(\eta)`$ vanishes at $`m(\eta)=t`$. So CE matches the target mean exactly (level and scale included) and the variance it then outputs is $`V(t)`$, *not* the target's: the family cannot express more.
3. *Price of Corr's freedom.* For a mean error $`\Delta=m-t`$, $`\mathrm{KL}(q_{\eta(t)}\Vert q_{\eta(m)})=A(\eta_m)-A(\eta_t)-A'(\eta_t)(\eta_m-\eta_t)\approx\tfrac12 A''(\eta_t)(\Delta\eta)^2`$, and $`\Delta\eta\approx\Delta/V(t)`$, so

```math
\mathrm{KL}\approx\frac{(m-t)^2}{2\,V(t)}.
```

(Computed check, $`K=6`$: $`t=2.5`$, $`m=2.7`$: exact KL 0.00689, approximation 0.00686; $`t=3.5`$, $`m=3.0`$: 0.0476 vs 0.0544. Misspecified target $`p^\ast=(.3,.05,.05,.05,.05,.5)`$: CE optimum has mean 3.0000, the target mean 3.0.) So in this class CE's advantage over Corr is *calibration* (level and scale), weighted by $`1/V`$; neither learns spread beyond $`V(m)`$.

### W4. Parametric, well-specified classes: Corr can lose nothing

Suppose $`p^\ast_i=q_{\theta^\ast}(\cdot\mid x_i)`$. CE is strictly proper, so (with identifiability) its population optimum is $`\theta^\ast`$. The Corr-optimal set is

```math
\Theta_\rho=\{\theta:\ M(\theta)=a+b\,M(\theta^\ast),\ b>0\}\ \supseteq\ \{\theta^\ast\}.
```

Corr loses nothing iff $`\Theta_\rho=\{\theta^\ast\}`$ (the class contains no other affine copy of the true mean function and $`M`$ is injective there). Whether that holds is a property of the class, not of Corr:

- *Binary logistic-linear* $`m_i=\sigma(w\phi_i+w_0)`$. An affine copy $`a+b\,\sigma(\cdot)`$ is again a logistic of an affine score only for $`a=0`$, $`b=1`$ (the map $`z\mapsto\sigma(z)`$ is not closed under affine maps). Computed check: $`n=50`$ items, truth $`(w,w_0)=(1.3,-0.4)`$, 30 random starts maximizing $`\rho`$: the 13 that converged ($`\rho>1-10^{-6}`$) all reached the truth; the others stopped on saturated sigmoids. So here Corr identifies the truth (it need not do so for $`K=6`$).
- *Near-affine closure.* For a softmax-linear head with logits $`u=c\,W\phi`$ and small $`c`$, $`m(cW)=m_0+c\,L(W)+O(c^2)`$ with $`L`$ linear in $`W`$ (derivation: expand the softmax mean at $`u=0`$, $`L(W)=\frac1K\sum_r(r-m_0)(W\phi)_r`$). So the class contains approximate affine copies $`a+b\,m`$ with $`b=c`$ of any *weak-logit* truth, and $`\rho`$ cannot choose among scales. For a strong-logit truth the copy is not close to affine (computed: with logit standard deviation about 2.4, $`\mathrm{corr}(m(0.1W),m(W))=0.85`$ although the slope is $`0.10`$). Whether experiment 20's world is in the weak-logit regime was not tested; this is a possible reason, not a finding, why its Corr-trained heads kept a compressed mean (their mean RMSE 1.16 to 1.20 vs 1.14 to 1.15 for a constant forecast, measured).

### W5. Linear mean model: Corr learns the best linear predictor, nothing more

Let $`m_i=a+w^\top\phi_i`$, $`\Sigma=\mathrm{Cov}(\phi)`$, $`c=\mathrm{Cov}(\phi,t)`$, $`\sigma_t^2=\mathrm{Var}(t)`$. Then $`\rho(w)=\dfrac{w^\top c}{\sqrt{w^\top\Sigma w}\;\sigma_t}`$. Cauchy–Schwarz in the $`\Sigma`$-inner product gives $`(w^\top c)^2=\langle\Sigma^{1/2}w,\Sigma^{-1/2}c\rangle^2\le (w^\top\Sigma w)(c^\top\Sigma^{-1}c)`$, with equality iff $`\Sigma^{1/2}w\propto\Sigma^{-1/2}c`$, i.e.

```math
w=\beta\,\Sigma^{-1}c\ (\beta>0),\qquad \rho_{\max}^2=\frac{c^\top\Sigma^{-1}c}{\sigma_t^2}=R^2_{\mathrm{OLS}} .
```

So Corr learns the OLS direction (scale $`\beta`$ and offset $`a`$ free), and $`\rho_{\max}<1`$ whenever the true mean is not linear in $`\phi`$; there is no output for spread or skew. (Computed check, 4 features, $`n=400`$ with a nonlinear target: $`\rho`$ at $`\Sigma^{-1}c`$ is 0.79785, a numerical maximizer reaches 0.79785 at cosine 0.999997 with it.) A softmax-linear CE head has per-category gradient $`\partial L/\partial W_r=\mathbb E[\phi\,(q_r-p^\ast_r)]`$ (R3), so its optimum matches the feature-weighted frequency of every category, hence the dependence of mean, spread and skew on $`\phi`$ as far as the log-linear form can express it.

### W6. Nonlinear mean model: nonlinear mean yes, spread no

If the model outputs a mean $`m_\theta(x)`$ from a rich class (a neural network) then $`\rho(m_\theta,t)`$ can reach $`1`$ whenever $`t`$ lies in the class, so a nonlinear mean is learned (up to $`(a,b)`$). Any extra output $`s_\theta(x)`$ (a spread or shape head) that does not enter $`m_\theta`$ has $`\partial\rho/\partial s_\theta=0`$ (R1: $`\rho`$ depends on $`\theta`$ only through $`M(\theta)`$): Corr never trains it. CE trains it through $`\partial L_{CE}/\partial u_i(r)=p_i(r)-p^\ast_i(r)`$.

### W7. Amount of data

**(i) The Corr target is noisy, and the noise is the spread.** With $`k`$ ratings per item the observed mean is $`\hat t_i=t_i+\varepsilon_i`$, $`\mathbb E\varepsilon_i=0`$, $`\mathrm{Var}\,\varepsilon_i=\sigma_i^2/k`$ where $`\sigma_i^2=\mathrm{Var}(p^\ast_i)`$ is the spread Corr ignores. If $`\varepsilon`$ is independent of $`m`$, $`\mathrm{Cov}(m,\hat t)=\mathrm{Cov}(m,t)`$ and $`\mathrm{Var}(\hat t)=s_t^2+\bar\sigma^2/k`$, so

```math
\rho(m,\hat t)=\frac{\rho(m,t)}{\sqrt{1+\bar\sigma^2/(k\,s_t^2)}}.
```

(Computed check by simulation, $`s_t^2=1`$, $`\bar\sigma^2=1.5`$: $`k=1`$ gives 0.564 against 0.566 from the formula, $`k=3`$ gives 0.730 against 0.730; illustrative numbers, not from the experiments.) A flexible Corr-trained head can reach $`\rho=1`$ against $`\hat t`$ by $`m_i=a+b\hat t_i`$, i.e. it memorizes the noise, which is why training correlation is not evidence of test quality at small $`n`$.

**(ii) CE has more to estimate.** CE must fit a full distribution. For a well-specified head with $`P`$ free parameters and $`N`$ ratings, the classical (Wald) expansion gives expected excess risk $`\approx P/(2N)`$ nats for $`N\gg P`$; for $`N`$ of order $`P`$ or below the expansion does not apply and CE can overfit (a zero-probability category at a held-out rating costs unbounded loss, floored at $`10^{-12}`$ in the experiments). Corr's floor $`B`$ (W1–W6: level, scale, shape) does not depend on $`N`$. A heuristic crossover is where $`P/(2N)\approx B`$ in the same units: $`N^\ast\approx P/(2B)`$, so the more shape and scale the class leaves free, the larger $`B`$ and the earlier CE wins; the more parameters CE must fit, the later. This is a derivation of the form only: $`B`$ was not measured in nats. Measured (Table 2): the head has 6 features plus an intercept, so $`P=(K-1)\times7=35`$ identifiable weights (the experiment's own summary says about 40); CE loses at 4 items (12 ratings), ties at 8 items (24), wins from 16 items (48 ratings), or from 32 items with 1 rating each, the same order as $`P`$.

**(iii) Exact tiny-sample case (experiment 19, Codex's derivation).** If the mean curve is known and the candidates differ only in spread, every defined Corr value is equal, so Corr can only follow its tie rule, whatever $`N`$. CE with three ratings (one per input type) already moves: conditional-variance squared error 0.02464 (high-spread truth) against 0.16 for Corr's low-spread default; for a low-spread truth Corr's default is exactly right (0) and CE is worse (0.04768). So Corr can win at tiny $`N`$ only when its default happens to match the truth; it cannot improve with more data.

**(iv) Selecting one judge.** With fixed judges, Corr selects the best-ranking judge, CE the best-calibrated. Which wins depends on whether the pool's calibration differs more than its ranking: measured, Corr selection wins in the 44 historical 3-vote judges, CE selection in the closed native-probability 15, the open-weight 39 and all 59 (Table 2). A reason (coarse three-vote probabilities with zero entries are expensive under CE) is plausible and was not tested here.

## Only the first conditional moment enters Pearson

On each artifact $`x`$, a judge predicts six probabilities $`q_x(r)`$ for ratings $`r=0,\ldots,5`$.
Pearson correlation of mean ratings reads just $`m_q(x)=\sum_r r q_x(r)`$, the first conditional
moment. The map from probabilities to $`m_q`$ is linear. A judge can use nonlinear input features
to predict $`m_q(x)`$, and Pearson's normalization is nonlinear.
Even perfect correlation leaves a common offset and positive rescaling of the predicted means undetermined.

**What can go missing?** Uniform forecasts on $`\{1,2,3\}`$ and on $`\{0,2,4\}`$ both
have mean 2, but their variances are $`2/3`$ and $`8/3`$. Replacing one with the other on an artifact leaves
every mean prediction and hence every defined mean-based correlation unchanged.
With six categories, fixing total mass and mean leaves four independent directions in which the probabilities can vary at an interior distribution.

**What cross-entropy learns.** For a target distribution $`p_x`$, expected cross-entropy (CE)
has excess risk $`D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_x\Vert q_x)`$ in nats. Its unique population optimum over all
distributions is $`q_x=p_x`$, so it recovers the mean and every higher moment. For any
integer $`j\ge 1`$, let $`u_j(r)=(r/5)^j`$. Since $`0\le u_j\le1`$, total variation distance (TVD)
and [Pinsker’s inequality (Thm. 4.4)](https://www.stat.yale.edu/~yw562/teaching/598/lec04.pdf) give

```math
\left|\mathbb E_{p_x}u_j(R)-\mathbb E_{q_x}u_j(R)\right|
\le \mathrm{TVD}(p_x,q_x)\le\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_x\Vert q_x)/2}.
```

This bound uses population excess CE. With few training ratings, sampling noise and model
restrictions can change which fitted judge has lower held-out loss.
The [small-data moment study](../19_small_data_moment_recovery_open_and_closed_judges_start_21Nov2026/README.md) tests this separately for open-weight and closed-weight judges.

## What correlation can and cannot learn (11-21-2026)

**In one sentence.** Mean-based Pearson reads one number per artifact, $`m_i=\sum_r r\,p_i(r)`$. Changing a feasible distribution while preserving this mean leaves every defined correlation unchanged. Pearson is undefined if either vector of means is constant. Normalized individual-rating ranked probability score (RPS) and CE assess the distribution using individual ratings. Total variation distance (TVD) compares two distributions.

**1. The dependence is through the means only (R1).** Since $`\sum_i\tilde t_i=0`$, the numerator of the box is $`\sum_i\tilde t_i m_i`$, so

```math
\rho=\frac{\sum_i\tilde t_i\,m_i}{\sqrt{\sum_i\tilde t_i^{\,2}\cdot\sum_i(m_i-\bar m)^2}}=F(m_1,\dots,m_n),\qquad m_i=\sum_{r\in R} r\,p_i(r),
```

a function of the $`n`$ numbers $`m_i`$ and of the fixed target means $`\tilde t_i`$. The numerator is linear in the judge's distribution: the judge enters through the $`n`$ linear functionals $`p_i\mapsto\sum_r r\,p_i(r)`$.

**2. Level and scale are not identified (R2).** For $`b>0`$ and any $`a`$,

```math
\rho(a+b\,m)=\rho(m),\qquad\text{so}\qquad \rho=1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ m_i=a+b\,t_i\ \ (b>0).
```

With nonconstant target and forecast means, any allowed forecast with $`m_i=a+bt_i`$ and $`b>0`$ attains the maximum 1. A restricted judge family may contain no such forecast.

**3. Spread and higher moments are unidentified at fixed means (R2).** The distributions uniform on $`\{1,2,3\}`$ and $`\{0,2,4\}`$ share mean 2, with variances $`2/3`$ and $`8/3`$. Substituting one for the other preserves all defined mean-based correlations across artifacts. Their summed squared cumulative distribution function (CDF) difference is $`D=2/9`$ (Lean-checked). On the six-category scale this is normalized excess expected RPS $`D/5=2/45`$. At an interior distribution, fixing the mean leaves $`K-2`$ independent shape directions per artifact.

**4. The gradient lives on one direction per item (R3).** With $`p_i=\mathrm{softmax}(u_i)`$,

```math
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial u_i(r)}&=p_i(r)\,(r-m_i)\,g_i &&(\text{one local direction in }r,\ \text{scaled by the number }g_i),\\
\frac{\partial L_{CE}}{\partial u_i(r)}&=p_i(r)-p^\ast_i(r) &&(\text{category-specific errors}).
\end{aligned}
```

A Pearson update follows the mean gradient and can incidentally change spread. Distributions with the same means receive identical objective values. CE supplies a separate discrepancy in each category. R3 differentiates the correlation $`\rho`$ to maximize. The minimized loss has derivative $`\partial(1-\rho)/\partial u_i(r)=-p_i(r)(r-m_i)g_i`$. The CE gradient shown is for the summed loss; averaging over items divides it by $`n`$.

**5. What CE, RPS and TVD read (R4–R6).** Expected individual-rating CE and RPS uniquely favor the true forecast over unrestricted distributions. TVD to the true law vanishes only at equality. A sparse empirical histogram gives a noisy TVD diagnostic. The shape gap $`G(P)`$ in R4 is the excess CE of the best log-affine-in-rating forecast. For general forecasts an additional signed cross term can cancel that gap.

| | mean $`m_i`$ | level, scale | variance, shape, $`\mu_{k\ge3}`$ | minimized only at $`p^\ast`$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Corr ($`\rho`$) | read | not identified | not read | no (R2, R6) |
| CE | read | read | read | yes |
| RPS | read | read | read | yes |
| TVD to the true law | read | read | read | yes |

**What this shows, and what it does not.** It shows, at the population level (the human distribution $`p^\ast_i`$ known exactly, a mathematical population target), what each objective can see and where each is optimal. It does *not* show the limited-data claim: whether a CE-trained judge also does better with few ratings is empirical (experiments 02, 08, 11, 16, 17 and the sweep in this folder), and experiment 19 reports retrospective human-rating comparisons and exact synthetic risks.

## Pearson between mean ratings

Replace the placeholder by

```math
\boxed{\ \mathrm{Corr}\big(p^\theta_j,p^\ast_j\big)=\frac{\sum_{i}\tilde t_i\,\sum_{r\in R} r\,p^\theta_{j}(r\mid x_i,y^\ast_i,\hat y_i;\theta)}{\sqrt{\sum_i \tilde t_i^{\,2}\ \cdot\ \sum_i\big(m_i-\bar m\big)^2}}\ }
```

where, one line each:

- $`m_i=\sum_{r\in R} r\,p^\theta_j(r\mid x_i,y^\ast_i,\hat y_i;\theta)`$: the judge\'s mean rating on item $`i`$;
- $`t_i=\sum_{r\in R} r\,p^\ast_j(r\mid x_i,y^\ast_i,\hat y_i;h)`$: the target mean rating (human or persona $`h`$);
- $`\tilde t_i=t_i-\bar t`$, with $`\bar t=\frac1n\sum_i t_i`$: the centered target mean;
- $`\bar m=\frac1n\sum_i m_i`$: the average judge mean;
- $`\sum_i=\sum_{i=1}^{n}`$: the sum over items $`i`$ (the page\'s $`v\in V`$).


The judge's distribution $`p^\theta_j`$ enters only through the $`n`$ numbers $`m_i`$ (its mean ratings), in the numerator linearly (coefficient $`r\,\tilde t_i`$ on $`p_i(r)`$) and in the denominator through their spread. Everything about the shape of $`p_i`$ beyond its mean is invisible. CE, RPS and TVD are functions of the whole vector $`p_i`$. Section 6 lists what is and is not shown.

## 1. Setting and notation
Categories $`R=\{0,\dots,K-1\}`$, $`K=6`$. Items $`i=1..n`$ (the page's $`v\in V`$; each item is $`(x,y^\ast,\hat y)`$ or a variant i–iv). Judge distribution $`p_i=p^\theta_j(\cdot\mid\cdot;\theta)\in\Delta^{K-1}`$. Target $`p^\ast_i=p^\ast_j(\cdot\mid\cdot;h)`$ for a human or persona $`h`$; with $`k`$ sparse ratings it is the empirical histogram, and $`t_i`$ is then the mean of the $`k`$ ratings (a noisy estimate of the true score). Means $`m_i=\sum_r r\,p_i(r)`$, $`t_i=\sum_r r\,p^\ast_i(r)`$; $`\bar m,\bar t`$ their averages over items; $`\tilde t_i=t_i-\bar t`$.

## 3. Results (proofs by hand)
**R1 (the formula in the box).** $`\sum_i(m_i-\bar m)(t_i-\bar t)=\sum_i\tilde t_i m_i-\bar m\sum_i\tilde t_i=\sum_i\tilde t_i m_i`$ because $`\sum_i\tilde t_i=0`$; insert $`m_i=\sum_r r\,p_i(r)`$. The denominator $`\sqrt{\sum\tilde t_i^2\sum(m_i-\bar m)^2}`$ for a fixed target is a function of $`m`$ alone. ∎

**R2 (positive affine invariance).** By R1, $`\rho=F(m_1,\dots,m_n)`$. Two judges with the same $`m_i`$ for all $`i`$ have the same $`\rho`$ whatever their shapes; and $`\rho(a+bm)=\rho(m)`$ for $`b>0`$ (numerator and denominator both scale by $`b`$, shifts cancel by centering). So the set of correlation-optimal judges is $`\{p:\ m_i=a+b\,t_i,\ b>0\}`$ (when this set intersects the judge family). With unrestricted per-artifact distributions and nonconstant targets, the stratum with all probabilities positive has relative dimension $`n(K-2)+2`$. At a boundary the feasible shape directions may be fewer. ∎

**R3 (gradients: Pearson uses the mean gradient, CE uses category errors).** With $`p_i=\mathrm{softmax}(u_i)`$:

```math
\frac{\partial L_{CE}}{\partial u_i(r)}=p_i(r)-p^\ast_i(r),\qquad \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial u_i(r)}=p_i(r)\,(r-m_i)\,g_i,\quad g_i=\frac{\zeta_i-\rho\,z_i}{n\,s_m},
```

$`z_i=(m_i-\bar m)/s_m`$, $`\zeta_i=\tilde t_i/s_t`$. Proof: $`\partial\rho/\partial p_i(r)=r\,g_i`$ (R1), and $`\partial p_i(r')/\partial u_i(r)=p_i(r)(\delta_{rr'}-p_i(r'))`$, so $`\partial m_i/\partial u_i(r)=p_i(r)(r-m_i)`$. ∎ The correlation gradient on item $`i`$ is proportional to the local direction $`p_i\circ(r-m_i)`$ (the score direction of the mean); varying the target allows CE gradients to span the $`(K-1)`$-dimensional tangent space.

**R4 (exact decomposition of CE: the "linear decomposition").** Let $`q_m`$ be the tilted-uniform (max-entropy) distribution on $`R`$ with mean $`m`$, $`G(P)=H(q_{m(P)})-H(P)\ge0`$ (experiment 05). For interior target mean $`m(P)`$ assume $`q(r)>0`$ for every category, so each term below is finite. At an endpoint mean the target and its maximum-entropy representative are the same point mass.

```math
\mathrm{CE}(P,q)=\underbrace{H(P)}_{\text{irreducible noise}}+\underbrace{G(P)}_{\text{shape gap}}+\underbrace{\mathrm{KL}(q_{m(P)}\,\Vert\,q)}_{\text{level/scale/shape of }q}+\underbrace{\sum_r\big(P(r)-q_{m(P)}(r)\big)\big(-\log q(r)\big)}_{C(P,q)\ \text{(cross term)}}.
```

Proof: $`G+\mathrm{KL}(q_m\Vert q)=-H(P)+\mathrm{CE}(q_m,q)`$, and $`\mathrm{CE}(P,q)-\mathrm{CE}(q_m,q)=C`$. ∎ If $`\log q(r)=\alpha+\beta r`$, mass and mean matching give $`C=0`$. Within this log-affine family the minimum excess CE is $`G(P)`$, at $`q=q_{m(P)}`$. An unrestricted judge can have $`q=P`$ and zero excess CE even when $`G(P)>0`$. For positive $`P`$, $`C(P,P)=-G(P)-\mathrm{KL}(q_{m(P)}\Vert P)`$ in that case. A scalar-to-distribution model can represent other shapes.

**R5 (decomposition of excess expected RPS).** Write $`d_k=F_p(k)-F_q(k)`$, $`k=0,\ldots,K-2`$, and let $`D=\sum_kd_k^2`$ be the unnormalized squared CDF distance. For a fresh rating $`Y\sim p`$, the project score has expected value
```math
\mathbb E_p S(q,Y)=\underbrace{\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_k F_p(k)(1-F_p(k))}_{\mathbb E_p S(p,Y)}
+\frac{D}{K-1}.
```
This follows by expanding the squared error of each Bernoulli indicator. Since $`\sum_k d_k=m_q-m_p`$, the normalized excess is
```math
\frac{D}{K-1}=\frac{(m_q-m_p)^2}{(K-1)^2}
+\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_k(d_k-\bar d)^2.
```
The excess equals the first term exactly when all CDF differences are equal. On the six-rating scale its mean-error denominator is 25. Also $`|m_p-m_q|\le(K-1)\mathrm{TVD}(p,q)`$ (step 5 below).

**R6 (propriety).** Expected individual-rating CE and RPS have unique minimum at the true distribution. R5 gives the strictly positive RPS excess for any different forecast. TVD to the true distribution is zero only at equality. With one observed rating $`y`$, $`\mathrm{TVD}(p,\delta_y)=1-p(y)`$. Its expectation $`1-\sum_r p^\ast(r)p(r)`$ favors forecasts supported on the most probable ratings. Positive affine changes of mean leave a defined Pearson correlation unchanged. In the archived example (30 synthetic items; probability floor $`10^{-12}`$), three forecasts have $`\rho=1.0000`$ to the reported precision. The archived column named RPS is the summed CDF distance $`D`$. For comparison the last column converts it to normalized excess expected RPS.

**R7 (correlating the probability bins).** Within an item with nonuniform target profile $`p^\ast_i`$, a forecast $`p_i=U+b_i(p^\ast_i-U)`$ with $`U=(1/K,\ldots,1/K)`$ and $`0<b_i\le1`$ achieves Pearson 1 across categories. A uniform forecast or target makes this profile correlation undefined. Stacking all item/category pairs gives a different coefficient: using one common $`b>0`$ attains 1 when the stacked target has positive variance and the forecast is feasible. Both objectives read probability-bin contrasts and can distinguish some equal-mean shapes. Neither uniquely identifies the distribution. The main analysis uses Pearson across artifact means.

## 4. Step-by-step derivations of R1 to R6 (11-21-2026)

Every symbol is as in the box above: $`p_i`$ is the judge's distribution on item $`i`$, $`m_i=\sum_r r\,p_i(r)`$ its mean, $`t_i`$ the target mean, $`\tilde t_i=t_i-\bar t`$, $`n`$ items, $`R=\{0,\dots,K-1\}`$.

**R1, step by step.**

1. Pearson correlation across items: $`\rho=\dfrac{\sum_i(m_i-\bar m)(t_i-\bar t)}{\sqrt{\sum_i(m_i-\bar m)^2\sum_i(t_i-\bar t)^2}}`$, and $`t_i-\bar t=\tilde t_i`$.
2. Expand the numerator: $`\sum_i(m_i-\bar m)\tilde t_i=\sum_i m_i\tilde t_i-\bar m\sum_i\tilde t_i`$.
3. $`\sum_i\tilde t_i=\sum_i t_i-n\bar t=0`$, so the second term vanishes and the numerator is $`\sum_i\tilde t_i m_i`$.
4. Insert $`m_i=\sum_r r\,p_i(r)`$: the numerator is $`\sum_i\sum_r (r\,\tilde t_i)\,p_i(r)`$, linear in $`p`$ with fixed coefficients $`c_{i,r}=r\,\tilde t_i`$.
5. In the denominator, $`\sum_i\tilde t_i^2`$ is a constant of the target and $`\sum_i(m_i-\bar m)^2`$ depends on the judge only through $`m`$. Hence $`\rho=F(m_1,\dots,m_n)`$. ∎

**R2, step by step.**

1. Replace $`m_i`$ by $`a+b\,m_i`$ with $`b>0`$. The average becomes $`a+b\,\bar m`$, so every centered value becomes $`b\,(m_i-\bar m)`$ and $`a`$ cancels.
2. Numerator: $`\sum_i\tilde t_i\,b\,(m_i-\bar m)=b\sum_i\tilde t_i m_i`$ (R1).
3. Denominator: $`\sqrt{\sum\tilde t_i^2\cdot b^2\sum(m_i-\bar m)^2}=b\sqrt{\sum\tilde t_i^2\sum(m_i-\bar m)^2}`$, using $`b>0`$.
4. The factor $`b`$ cancels, so $`\rho(a+b\,m)=\rho(m)`$.
5. $`\rho=1`$ iff the centered vectors $`(m_i-\bar m)`$ and $`(\tilde t_i)`$ are positively proportional (equality in Cauchy–Schwarz), i.e. $`m_i-\bar m=b\,\tilde t_i`$ with $`b>0`$, i.e. $`m_i=a+b\,t_i`$ with $`a=\bar m-b\,\bar t`$.
6. Counting the positive-probability stratum of the unrestricted optimum set: each of $`n`$ separate distributions has $`K-1`$ dimensions. Fixing its mean leaves $`K-2`$. A nonconstant target makes the two feasible affine parameters locally independent. Boundary or shared-model constraints may reduce this dimension.

**R3, step by step.**

1. Write $`\rho=\dfrac{N}{\sqrt n\,s_t\,\lVert m-\bar m\rVert}`$ with $`N=\sum_i\tilde t_i m_i`$, $`s_t^2=\frac1n\sum\tilde t_i^2`$, $`s_m^2=\frac1n\sum(m_i-\bar m)^2`$, so $`\lVert m-\bar m\rVert=\sqrt n\,s_m`$.
2. $`\partial N/\partial m_j=\tilde t_j`$. Because $`\sum_i(m_i-\bar m)=0`$, the centering term drops and $`\partial\lVert m-\bar m\rVert/\partial m_j=(m_j-\bar m)/\lVert m-\bar m\rVert`$.
3. Quotient rule: $`\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial m_j}=\dfrac{\tilde t_j}{\sqrt n s_t\lVert m-\bar m\rVert}-\dfrac{N\,(m_j-\bar m)}{\sqrt n s_t\lVert m-\bar m\rVert^3}=\dfrac{\zeta_j-\rho\,z_j}{n\,s_m}=g_j`$, with $`\zeta_j=\tilde t_j/s_t`$, $`z_j=(m_j-\bar m)/s_m`$.
4. $`\partial m_i/\partial p_i(r)=r`$, so $`\partial\rho/\partial p_i(r)=r\,g_i`$.
5. Softmax $`p_i(r')=e^{u_i(r')}/\sum_s e^{u_i(s)}`$ gives $`\partial p_i(r')/\partial u_i(r)=p_i(r')(\delta_{rr'}-p_i(r))`$. Hence $`\dfrac{\partial m_i}{\partial u_i(r)}=\sum_{r'}r'p_i(r')(\delta_{rr'}-p_i(r))=p_i(r)\,(r-m_i)`$.
6. Chain rule: $`\partial\rho/\partial u_i(r)=p_i(r)(r-m_i)\,g_i`$: the vector $`p_i\circ(r-m_i)`$ times the single number $`g_i`$.
7. CE: $`L_{CE}=-\sum_i\sum_r p^\ast_i(r)\log p_i(r)`$, so $`\dfrac{\partial L_{CE}}{\partial u_i(r)}=-\sum_{r'}\dfrac{p^\ast_i(r')}{p_i(r')}\,p_i(r')(\delta_{rr'}-p_i(r))=-p^\ast_i(r)+p_i(r)\sum_{r'}p^\ast_i(r')=p_i(r)-p^\ast_i(r)`$. ∎

**R4, step by step.**

1. Let $`q_m`$ be the maximum-entropy distribution on $`R`$ with interior mean $`m`$ (an exponential tilt of the uniform: $`q_m(r)\propto e^{\lambda r}`$), and $`m(P)`$ the mean of $`P`$. Define $`G(P)=H(q_{m(P)})-H(P)`$; it is $`\ge0`$ because $`q_{m(P)}`$ maximizes entropy among all distributions with interior mean $`m(P)`$, and $`P`$ is one of them.
2. Add and subtract: $`\mathrm{CE}(P,q)=\mathrm{CE}(q_{m(P)},q)+C(P,q)`$ with $`C(P,q)=\sum_r\big(P(r)-q_{m(P)}(r)\big)(-\log q(r))`$.
3. $`\mathrm{CE}(q_m,q)=H(q_m)+\mathrm{KL}(q_m\Vert q)`$ (the identity $`\mathrm{CE}=H+\mathrm{KL}`$), and $`H(q_m)=H(P)+G(P)`$.
4. Together: $`\mathrm{CE}(P,q)=H(P)+G(P)+\mathrm{KL}(q_{m(P)}\Vert q)+C(P,q)`$.
5. If $`-\log q(r)=\alpha+\beta r`$ (log-affine in the rating): $`C=\alpha\sum_r(P-q_m)(r)+\beta\sum_r r\,(P-q_m)(r)=\alpha\cdot0+\beta\,(m(P)-m(P))=0`$, since $`P`$ and $`q_{m(P)}`$ both sum to 1 and both have mean $`m(P)`$.
6. Then $`\mathrm{CE}(P,q)=H(P)+G(P)+\mathrm{KL}(q_{m(P)}\Vert q)`$: the only part $`q`$ controls is the KL term, a function of the two means. $`G(P)`$ is the same for all judges in this log-affine family. ∎

**R5, step by step.**

1. $`\sum_{k=0}^{K-2}F_p(k)=\sum_{k=0}^{K-2}\sum_{r\le k}p(r)=\sum_r p(r)\,\#\{k:\ r\le k\le K-2\}=\sum_r p(r)(K-1-r)=K-1-m_p`$ (for $`r=K-1`$ the count is $`0=K-1-r`$).
2. Hence $`\sum_k d_k=\sum_kF_p(k)-\sum_kF_q(k)=m_q-m_p`$.
3. Variance identity over the $`K-1`$ values $`d_k`$ with mean $`\bar d=\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_kd_k`$: $`\sum_kd_k^2=(K-1)\bar d^{\,2}+\sum_k(d_k-\bar d)^2=\dfrac{(\sum_kd_k)^2}{K-1}+\sum_k(d_k-\bar d)^2`$.
4. The archived sum $`D=\sum_kd_k^2`$ has mean term $`(m_p-m_q)^2/(K-1)`$. Divide by $`K-1`$ for normalized excess expected RPS: $`D/(K-1)=(m_p-m_q)^2/(K-1)^2+\sum_k(d_k-\bar d)^2/(K-1)`$. The shape term vanishes exactly when all differences are equal.
5. TVD bound: $`m_p-m_q=\sum_r r\,(p-q)(r)=\sum_r\big(r-\tfrac{K-1}2\big)(p-q)(r)`$ because $`\sum_r(p-q)(r)=0`$. Since $`\lvert r-\tfrac{K-1}2\rvert\le\tfrac{K-1}2`$, $`\lvert m_p-m_q\rvert\le\tfrac{K-1}2\sum_r\lvert p-q\rvert=(K-1)\,\mathrm{TVD}(p,q)`$. ∎

**R6, step by step.**

1. CE: $`\mathrm{CE}(p^\ast,p)-\mathrm{CE}(p^\ast,p^\ast)=\mathrm{KL}(p^\ast\Vert p)\ge0`$, with equality iff $`p=p^\ast`$ (Gibbs' inequality). So CE is minimized only at $`p^\ast`$.
2. Expected RPS excess $`D/(K-1)`$ vanishes exactly when every CDF gap is zero, equivalently $`p=p^\ast`$. Expected RPS itself also includes the true forecast’s irreducible score.
3. TVD $`=\frac12\sum_r\lvert p(r)-p^\ast(r)\rvert=0`$ iff $`p=p^\ast`$.
4. $`\rho\le1`$, and by R2 it equals 1 on the whole set $`\{p:\ m_i=a+b\,t_i,\ b>0\}`$, which contains many $`p\ne p^\ast`$. So $`\rho`$ is not uniquely maximized at $`p^\ast`$, and it cannot rank two judges whose means are affinely related to $`t`$. ∎

## 5. Evaluation, as the page says it is "wrong"
Using correlation of the judge's mean with human mean ratings as the evaluation metric has the R2 blindness: level, scale and spread are invisible, and three judges with $`\rho=1`$ (R6) differ by 18 nats of CE. The metrics that read the whole distribution are test CE, test TVD and test RPS (all lower is better), with Pearson and Spearman as sanity columns; Sanmi's point 4 (distributional accuracy when the output is read as a rating or distribution; rank agreement for ranking uses) matches. Selecting on training correlation can yield higher or lower test correlation. The empirical sweep reports both Pearson and Spearman.

**Shows:** Pearson of expected ratings depends only on the means and is invariant to a common positive affine transformation. **Does not show:** (1) That a CE-trained judge generalizes better with few ratings. That is empirical (exp 02, 08, 11, 16, 17 and the sweep in this folder). **TLDR-end:** [exp 18 math] An unrestricted mean-based Pearson objective cannot select among equal-mean distribution shapes. CE and individual-rating RPS identify the conditional distribution in expectation. R4 quantifies the special log-affine restriction; R5 gives the normalized RPS excess.
