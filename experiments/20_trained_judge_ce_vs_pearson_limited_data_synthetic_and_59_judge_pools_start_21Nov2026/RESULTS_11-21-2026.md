## Bottom line (one paragraph)

**The claim held only above a data threshold, and failed at the smallest sizes.** With known human distributions (synthetic, 200 seeds, same softmax-linear judge, only the objective differs), the CE-trained judge beats the Pearson-trained one on distribution score (RPS excess) from 16 training items with 3 ratings each (48 ratings): +0.034 [0.031, 0.036], CE better in 96% of seeds in the world where spread and shape vary (+0.020, 90% when only the mean varies). With 1 rating per item the crossover is at 32 items (+0.030, 94%; +0.019, 89%). Below that it fails: at 4 items CE loses on RPS (-0.027 [-0.032, -0.021], CE better in 24% of seeds) and on test CE (6.14 nats vs 1.83 for the Pearson-trained judge), because CE overfits while the Pearson-trained judge stays near a uniform forecast; at 8 items RPS is a tie (+0.002 [-0.002, +0.007]) and Pearson still wins on test CE. CE does learn what correlation cannot see: at 64 items (3 ratings) CE ranks items by true variance inside same-mean groups with Spearman 0.89 against 0.33, and its mean error is 0.34 points against 1.16, since Pearson does not identify the scale of the mean; at 512 items 0.21 vs 1.20 and RPS excess 0.003 vs 0.075. On the 75 real artifacts (all ratings per artifact) a CE head on judge scores beats a Pearson-trained head by +0.035 to +0.058 RPS from 7 training families up in the closed-weight 15 and all-59 pools (and by +0.027 to +0.044 in the 44 historical 3-vote judges from 7 families); all these intervals exclude 0, and by +0.021 [+0.007, +0.041] already at 3 families in the closed 3-vote 5; it ties at 3 families in the closed 15, all-59 and historical-44 pools, and loses at 3 families in the open-weight 39 (-0.037 [-0.055, -0.019]) and ties there at 7 and 15. Pearson-trained heads also miss the spread: variance squared error is 4.0 to 4.9 points^4 worse than CE in every one of the 15 cells (intervals exclude 0). Selecting a single judge by Pearson beats selecting by CE in the 44 historical 3-vote judges at every size (-0.030 to -0.033, intervals exclude 0) and in the closed 3-vote 5 at 15 families (-0.024 [-0.040, -0.010]); CE selection wins in the closed native-probability 15, the open-weight 39 and all 59 (+0.011 to +0.063, all 9 intervals exclude 0, the lowest bound being +0.002 for open-weight 39 at 15 families); the closed 3-vote 5 ties at 3 and 7 families. The ridge strength moves the small-data crossover (sensitivity tables), so "CE wins with little data" holds as "with enough data to fit about 40 parameters", not at 4 items.

## What Codex's experiment 19 covers, what this adds, and whether they agree

- **Codex (experiment 19, Brando's local Codex session):** (1) exact enumeration of every small training sample for two fixed conditional laws (theory_simulation), (2) CE vs Pearson calibration of four judges' saved probabilities (two Qwen open-weight, gpt-5.4-mini and gpt-6.1-sol closed-weight) on the 75 artifacts with a 27-point calibration grid (human_judges).
- **This experiment adds:** (a) a synthetic sweep from 4 to 512 training items with trained softmax judges and known same-mean-different-spread distributions, with moment errors (mean, variance, skew); (b) trained heads and judge selection on wider pools: the 15 native-probability closed-weight judges, the 5 closed 3-vote judges, the 39 open-weight 3-vote judges, all 59 and the 44 historical ones; (c) an independent cross-check of Codex's numbers.
- **Do they agree? Yes on the numbers:** my own re-implementation of Codex's calibration grid reproduces 144 of its held-out RPS numbers (6 inputs x 6 budgets x CE-fit, Pearson-fit, identity, constant) with a largest absolute difference of 5.6e-17 (closed pair, 3 families, 1 rating: CE-fit 0.15647, Pearson-fit 0.15796, difference +0.00149; untreated forecast 0.13985). In that cell the untreated forecast beats both fits, and the CE-minus-Pearson sign matches Codex's table in every one of the 36 input x budget cells. Experiment 18's reconcile run is reproduced for Pearson selection exactly in all 27 comparable cells; CE selection differs by at most 0.0058 RPS in 11 of 27 cells (mine averages CE over ratings, reconcile over artifacts, which matters when artifacts have 2 or 3 ratings).

## Caveats

- Synthetic worlds are well specified (the true distribution is inside the judge's family) so differences are estimation, not model mismatch; real data are not.
- The Pearson-trained judge is only defined up to the initial draw and the ridge: Pearson is blind to the scale and level of the judge's mean, so with a ridge it stays close to a uniform forecast. That is the point of the derivation (what correlation can identify), but it also means "Pearson-trained" results depend on this stated convention; lambda sensitivity is reported.
- CE at 4 to 8 items overfits with 42 parameters; a validation-tuned ridge or early stopping was not used (no tuning on test, fixed lambdas reported side by side).
- Real-data intervals cover the sampling of held-out families only; splits overlap and are not independent. The 75 artifacts have historical exposure from experiments 9, 10, 18 and 19.
- Layer 4 (new open- and closed-weight judge runs) was not run: it needs Brando's plan-router usage gate and no new calls were made. The 3-vote pools carry 3 samples per artifact, so their probabilities are coarse.
- Open-weight 39 and closed 5 are 3-vote histograms, closed 15 are native probabilities: pool type is confounded with open versus closed.
