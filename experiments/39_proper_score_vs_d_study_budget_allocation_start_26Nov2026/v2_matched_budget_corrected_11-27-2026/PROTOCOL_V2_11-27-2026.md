## 2. Metric (corrected description)

The reported "RPS" is the **normalized individual-rating ranked probability score** averaged over each test item's ratings, items weighted equally: $\frac1{K-1}\sum_{k=0}^{K-2}\bigl[F(k)^2+G(k)(1-2F(k))\bigr]$ with $F$ the forecast and $G$ the item's empirical rating distribution. It equals the mean over the item's ratings $y$ of $\frac1{K-1}\sum_k (F(k)-\mathbf 1\{y\le k\})^2$, so it contains the forecast-independent floor $\frac1{K-1}\sum_k G(k)(1-G(k))$. Differences between forecasts are unaffected. Lower is better; $K=3$ for SNLI, MNLI-m and DICES, $K=2$ for alphaNLI. The NLI labels are nominal, so RPS depends on the imposed order (contradiction, neutral, entailment); CE and total variation distance (TVD) are reported as order-free secondary checks.

Per-cell **reference values** are reported: the floor above, the reducible excess (total RPS minus floor), and the RPS of a constant base-rate forecast (development-pool class frequencies with $+0.5$ smoothing).

## 3. Information rule and rating accounting (fix c)

- **Pilot table.** One table of 80 development items × 16 ratings per replication, same draws as v1. Pilot cost $P=80\times16=1{,}280$ ratings.
- **Strict rule.** Every pilot-using selector (the D-study selectors and the proper-score selectors) receives **only** this table. The proper-score selector's validation targets are the 16-rating histograms of the held-out pilot items (the empirical per-rating risk is exactly the unbiased per-sample RPS over those 16 ratings), and its inner training ratings are $n_r$ of the 16 pilot ratings of each training item. It never reads an item's full rating pool (v1 did, using 69–123 ratings per pilot item).
- **Total ratings.** A pilot-using selector consumes $P+B$ ratings; a no-pilot fixed rule consumes $B$. Every table lists $P$, $B$ and $P/B$ (20, 10, 5, 2.5 for $B=64,128,256,512$). The pilot cost is not netted out of the comparison, so a pilot-using selector must beat a fixed rule *despite* spending extra ratings that the fixed rule could have used on deployment; any win is therefore an upper bound on net value.

## 4. Selectors (fix b)

At each budget $B$ with candidate set $\mathcal C_B$:

| Name | Rule | Uses pilot? |
|---|---|---|
| `fix_items` | the design with the largest $n_i$ | no |
| `fix_raters` | the design with the largest $n_r$ | no |
| `ds_u1` | v1 U1: minimize $\widehat v_e/n_r$ (one-facet ANOVA on the pilot, $\widehat v_e\ge10^{-12}$) | nominally; analytic check, equals `fix_raters` |
| `ds_u2` | v1 U2: minimize $\widehat v_u/n_i+\widehat v_e/B$ ($\widehat v_u\ge0$) | nominally; analytic check, equals `fix_items` when $\widehat v_u>0$ |
| `ds_rel80` | **reliability-target D-study (primary adaptive D-study).** $\widehat\rho(n_r)=\widehat v_u/(\widehat v_u+\widehat v_e/n_r)$ is the estimated generalizability coefficient of an item mean over $n_r$ ratings. Choose the candidate with the smallest $n_r$ such that $\widehat\rho(n_r)\ge0.80$; if none reaches 0.80, the largest $n_r$ | yes, decision depends on $(\widehat v_u,\widehat v_e)$ |
| `ds_rel90` | same with target 0.90 (sensitivity) | yes |
| `ps` | **proper-score selector (primary).** 5-fold × 8 inner draws on the pilot table only; lowest mean validation RPS of the CE-fitted 27-transform forecast | yes, pilot table only |
| `ps_mix` | the same under the Amendment A1 mixture $q=(1-\alpha)q_{\text{transform}}+\alpha\widehat\pi$, $\alpha\in\{0,.25,.5,.75,1\}$ (secondary) | yes, pilot table only |

`ds_u1` and `ds_u2` are reported as **analytic corner rules**: under one-facet G-theory at fixed $B$ the per-item criterion depends only on $n_r$ and the grand-mean criterion only on $n_i$, so they never change their decision with the data. `verify_v2.py` asserts `ds_u1 == fix_raters` and `ds_u2 == fix_items` in 100% of replications of every comparable cell. They are therefore *not* evidence that variance-component estimation helps or hurts. `ds_rel80` is the pre-declared test of that question.

## 5. Oracles and ties (fix a)

- Oracles (for diagnosis only, never selectors): `or_rps` (lowest deployed test RPS), `or_rps_mix`, `or_item` (lowest per-item mean-estimation error), `or_grand`.
- **Tie rule (all selectors and oracles).** Designs whose score is within $10^{-12}$ of the best form the tied set. The reported pick is drawn uniformly from the tied set (sorted by $(n_i,n_r)$) with `SeedSequence([M, 8, k, rep, B, role])`, `role` in `{ps:0, ps_mix:1, or_rps:2, or_rps_mix:3, or_item:4, or_grand:5}`. The rule is deterministic and favours neither more items nor more raters.
- Every table reports, per cell, the tied-set size distribution of `or_rps` (fraction of replications with a unique best design, with 2 tied, with 3 tied), the **tie-inclusive agreement** (selector $\in$ tied oracle set) and the **fractional-credit agreement** (selector $\in$ set, divided by set size), next to the neutral-pick agreement. No statement about "which design the oracle prefers" is made from the neutral pick alone.

## 6. Statistics (fix d)

For two selectors $A,B$ and a metric, the paired **gain of $A$ over $B$** is $L_B-L_A$ (positive means $A$ has the lower loss), as the mean over test items and replications of the per-item loss difference.

- **Primary interval and $p$-value: two-way bootstrap**, 10,000 draws, resampling the 150 replications and the test items independently with replacement (percentile 95% interval; two-sided $p=2\min(\Pr^*[\text{stat}^*\le0],\Pr^*[\text{stat}^*\ge0])$ with a $+1$ correction, capped at 1). It covers pilot, training-draw and test-item sampling, not development-item or rater-population sampling. Per-item test losses are rebuilt from the stored transform index (the head is deterministic given the transform), checked against the stored replication means to $10^{-12}$.
- **Replication-only Student-$t$ interval and paired-$t$ $p$**, as in v1, reported for comparability.
- **Holm–Bonferroni** across the 20 comparable cells, separately within each comparison family below (family-wise error 0.05 within a family; no adjustment across families, stated in every table).
- **"Beats"** means lower mean loss **and** Holm-adjusted two-way-bootstrap $p<0.05$. Raw (unadjusted) significant counts are reported only next to the adjusted counts.
- Effect sizes: each gain is also expressed as a percentage of total RPS and of the reducible excess.
- Metrics: RPS is primary. CE and TVD are secondary and use the same procedure on the primary head. The A1 mixture (`ps_mix`, `or_rps_mix`) is secondary and uses replication-only $t$ intervals (per-item mixture losses are not rebuilt).

**Comparison families** (gain of the first over the second, RPS): F1 `fix_items` vs `fix_raters`; F2 `ps` vs `fix_raters`; F3 `ps` vs `fix_items`; F4 `ds_rel80` vs `fix_raters`; F5 `ds_rel80` vs `fix_items`; F6 `ps` vs `ds_rel80`; F7 (secondary) `ds_rel90` vs `fix_items`. Sanity rows on the mean-estimation targets (per-item mean squared error, grand-mean squared error) compare `fix_raters` and `fix_items` with replication-only intervals and are labelled analytic.

## 7. Pre-declared predictions (before the run)

1. **F1.** `fix_items` has lower mean RPS than `fix_raters` in a majority of the 20 cells, but significantly so (Holm, two-way bootstrap) in at most 6 of 20, with gains below 3% of total RPS.
2. **F3.** `ps` does not significantly beat `fix_items` in any cell after Holm (0 of 20).
3. **F6.** `ps` does not beat `ds_rel80` in a majority (at least 11) of the 20 cells.
4. **Adaptivity.** `ds_rel80` has at least two distinct modal designs across the five datasets at a given budget for at least two of the four comparable budgets (it responds to the data across datasets); within a dataset its pick may vary across replications.
5. **Ties.** The fraction of replications with a unique best RPS design (`or_rps`) is below 0.5 overall (v1 reported 75% with ties).
6. **v1 regression.** `fix_items` equals v1 U2 and `fix_raters` equals v1 U1 in the deployed test RPS of every replication (bit-identical), and `ds_u1`, `ds_u2` equal the fixed rules in 100% of replications.

## 8. Decision rules (before the run)

- **D1 ("more items beats more raters for held-out RPS")** is reported as the Holm-adjusted count of cells where `fix_items` beats `fix_raters`, with effect sizes. It is not stated as a general law: the grid has $n_r\ge2$ and $n_i\le64$ (no $n_r=1$ designs; the one-rating-per-item regime is Experiment 40), and the head has three effective parameters.
- **D2 ("proper-score selection adds value")** holds only if `ps` beats the best no-pilot fixed rule `fix_items` in at least one cell after Holm. Otherwise the report says that no evidence was found that pilot-based proper-score selection improves on a fixed rule at this pilot size.
- **D3 ("a pilot-based D-study adds value")** holds only if `ds_rel80` beats both fixed rules in at least one cell after Holm.
- **D4 ("proper-score selection tracks the distributional target better than the D-study")** holds only if `ps` beats `ds_rel80` in at least 11 of the 20 cells after Holm.
- None of the four is a statement that D-theory is wrong or unnecessary for a universe-score target; per-item mean precision is a different target, and v2 reports it as an analytic sanity row.

## 10. Limitations declared in advance

Only the 27-transform recalibration head on five frozen judges; one pilot size (80 × 16, much larger than the deployment budgets); grid without $n_r=1$ and with $n_i\le64$; ChaosNLI has no rater identities, so crossed rater effects (DICES) are not modelled by the one-facet D-study; the two-way bootstrap ignores development-pool sampling; no new human or model data.
