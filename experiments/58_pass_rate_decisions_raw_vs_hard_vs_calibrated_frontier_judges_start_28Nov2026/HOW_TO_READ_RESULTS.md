# How to read the Experiment 58 results

1. **Which data.** The same five frozen frontier judges and five public multi-annotator rating sets as Experiment 57; every number uses all items of a set. The raw soft rate and the hard-label rate use no fitted quantity, so they are not in-sample in any meaningful way.
   The calibrated soft rate is cross-fitted: each item's forecast was calibrated by a map fitted on the other four group folds. The ratings of these sets were seen in earlier work (Experiments 28, 46, 48, 55, 56), as the protocol states.
2. **Which metric.** An event rate is a percentage. The human rate is the average over items of the share of that item's ratings in the event. The raw soft rate is the average forecast mass on the event; the hard-label rate is the share of verdicts (argmax labels) in the event; the calibrated soft rate is the average mass after calibration.
   Error = estimator rate minus human rate, in percentage points; zero is perfect, negative means the judge flags the event less often than humans do. "Absolute error" drops the sign; lower is better. For a gate "rate at most tau", an estimator that says pass while the humans say fail is a false pass.
3. **What is subtracted.** Always the estimator's rate minus the human rate on the same set, event and items. H2 compares the absolute error of the calibrated soft rate with that of the raw soft rate in the same cell, and H3 the absolute error of the hard-label rate with that of the calibrated soft rate in the same cell.
4. **Uncertainty.** Percentile 95% intervals from 4,000 group-clustered bootstrap draws (SNLI items that share an image move together), one weight matrix per set shared by all judges and estimators. A significant flip needs the gate to flip in at least 97.5% of the draws. The intervals do not include the variability of the fitted calibration map.
5. **How the judge was chosen.** Not chosen: the five judges are fixed. The calibration is the Experiment 46 map (multinomial logit on [log forecast, 1], ridge 1e-3 toward the identity, individual-rating cross-entropy, L-BFGS-B from the identity), the answer of that optimizer on four folds at a time.
   Because its intercepts are unpenalized, it matches the training label shares, so the calibrated rates agree with the human rate by construction; the comparator in `posthoc_base_rate_rates.csv` (the human base rate of the other folds, no judge) shows the same agreement and was added after the protocol was frozen.

Decisions are in `results/hypotheses.json`: H1 supported (13 of 25), H2 supported (25 of 25), H3 supported (25 of 25); the gate-flip counts are descriptive. The figure (`results/rates_figure.png`): one panel per set; the dashed line is the human rate with its 95% band;
the dots are the three estimators per judge with 95% intervals; the estimator closest to the dashed line is best. Gate thresholds are the human rate rounded to the nearest 5 points, plus or minus 5 and 10 points, which is an arbitrary grid chosen in the protocol; flip counts scale with it.
