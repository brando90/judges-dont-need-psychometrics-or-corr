# How to read this folder

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/judges-dont-need-psychometrics-or-corr/blob/main/experiments/45_how_llm_judges_are_validated_in_practice_literature_census_start_27Nov2026/HOW_TO_READ_RESULTS.md>

This is a literature census, not a model experiment. It trains and selects no judge and has no train, validation or test split. Its numbers are counts and shares of published sources.

1. **Which data.** 53 published sources coded in `census.csv`, in seven groups chosen on purpose (not at random): graded-score judges and metrics (L1), pairwise-preference judges and reward models (L2), benchmark and leaderboard ranking validation (L3), frontier-lab and industry graders (L4), retrieval relevance and annotation replacement (L5), critiques and distributional papers (L6) and pre-LLM context rows (P). The base-rate denominator is the 40 sources of L1 to L5 that validate an LLM judge against human labels, are from the LLM era and were read in full (26) or in part (14). L6 and P are in no base rate. No ratings were collected or modelled, so there is no held-out set and no prior-exposure question.
2. **Which metric.** Every share is "sources with property X" over "sources in the denominator" (or over the sources that select, tune or train on human data, for the selection shares). It is a share of sources, range 0 to 1; neither higher nor lower is better. The headline statistic of a source is the family of the first agreement number in its abstract, else in its first main results table: correlation (Pearson, Spearman, Kendall), accuracy or agreement, F1, kappa or alpha or ICC, or other. Reference values are the pre-declared thresholds in `PROTOCOL_AND_PREDICTIONS_11-27-2026.md` (0.40 and 0.70 for the pooled share; 0.80 with a Wilson lower bound of 0.65 for the graded-score lineage; 0.15 for selection; 0.10 for distributional scoring).
3. **What is subtracted.** Nothing is subtracted between methods. The only comparisons are shares against pre-declared thresholds, shares against the pre-declared predictions, and, in `compare_with_exp43_output.txt`, whether two codings of the same paper agree on a yes or no field.
4. **Uncertainty.** Wilson 95% intervals on a share of sources, and for the lineage-balanced estimate a stratified percentile bootstrap (seed 0, 2,000 draws). They describe this sample, not the field. Coding was done by one agent from a fixed extraction prompt; a second read of 16 rows agreed on 98 of 105 field-level answers (93.3%), and three census rows were corrected afterwards (see `README.md` and `verification.csv`).
5. **How the judge was chosen.** Not applicable: no judge is fitted here. The census records how the sources chose theirs (`selection_by_corr`, `selection_metric`, `selection_loose`), as reported in their text.
6. **Where each number comes from.** Every count and share in `README.md` and `REPORT_11-27-2026.md` is printed by `python3 tally.py --figure` into `tally_output.txt`, or by `python3 compare_with_exp43.py` into `compare_with_exp43_output.txt`. Both are deterministic and were run twice with identical output.
7. **Figure.** `fig_headline_share_by_lineage.png` shows, by lineage, the share of sources with a correlation headline (dot with Wilson 95% interval) and the share that report any correlation (hollow diamond). The dashed lines are the pre-declared pooled thresholds (0.40 and 0.70). The horizontal axis is the share of the lineage's sources (0 to 1; higher means correlation is more common, not that anything is better); the vertical axis lists the five lineages and the pooled row; the column at right gives the count of correlation headlines over the sources in the lineage.

**TLDR-end:** [how to read the literature census] This folder counts what 40 published judge-validation sources report, with Wilson intervals that describe the sample, and fits and selects no judge of its own.
