# How to read the Experiment 44 results

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/judges-dont-need-psychometrics-or-corr/blob/main/experiments/44_pool_as_construct_consensus_sharpness_binned_jsd_power_start_27Nov2026/HOW_TO_READ_RESULTS.md>

**TLDR:** Nothing in this experiment is trained or fitted. Five frozen judges forecast every item once; every number is a score of those fixed forecasts against human ratings that were collected before the judges were run. There is no train, validation or test split, no held-out raters and no search over judges; "held out" here means only that, in Arm S, the ratings that score a stratum are a different random half from the ratings that define it.

**As of:** 11-27-2026 (Friday, Pacific time).

The five questions the project asks of every reported number, answered for this experiment:

1. **Which data?** Public multi-annotator rating sets: ChaosNLI SNLI (600 items), MNLI-m (600), alphaNLI (400), each with 100 crowd labels per item and no rater identities; DICES-350 (350 items, the same 123 raters on every item) and DICES-990 (400 items, 69 to 76 raters per item), with gender, age group and race for each rater. Item selection is Experiment 39's `prepare_data.py` and is described in `README.md`. All items of a set are used; the numbers are in-sample in the sense that nothing was selected on them, and not out-of-sample in any training sense. Prior exposure: the same judges and items were scored in Experiment 28, and the all-item rankings of the five judges were known before this protocol was frozen (`PROTOCOL.md`, "What the analyst had seen").
2. **Which metric?** Unbiased per-sample risk (mean over an item's ratings of a proper score of the judge's forecast, then mean over items), lower is better. ChaosNLI sets use the multiclass Brier score, the sum over categories of the squared difference between forecast probability and the one-hot rating (range 0 to 2; a uniform forecast scores 0.6667 with three classes and 0.5 with two). DICES uses the ranked probability score (RPS) normalized by the number of categories minus one, the mean squared difference between the cumulative forecast and the cumulative one-hot rating over the first two cut points (range 0 to 1; a uniform forecast scores 0.2778 on a rating of No or Yes and 0.1111 on Unsure). Binned JSD (Arm J) is the Jensen-Shannon divergence in bits between pooled human and mean forecast distributions inside bins of items, weighted by bin size (range 0 to 1, lower is better). TVD (Arm P) is total variation distance between two rater pools' rating distributions for an item, 0 to 1. Minimum detectable difference (Arm M) is in risk units.
3. **What is subtracted?** Always the same metric on the same items. Arm P: risk against rater pool A minus risk against pool B for the same judge (positive = the judge looks worse against A; pool A is the first named group, for example Man in "Man − Woman"). Arm S: risk at temperature 1 minus risk at temperature 0.5 on the same half-B ratings of the same stratum (positive = sharpening helps), then consensus-stratum gain minus contested-stratum gain. Arm J: risk after shuffling forecasts within human bins minus original risk, same items and metric. Arm M: the difference between two judges' per-item scores, same metric.
4. **Uncertainty?** Percentile intervals at 95%. Arm P uses a two-way bootstrap (items and, independently within each pool, raters resampled with replacement), 4,000 draws; the distribution test uses a rater-label permutation null with 500 permutations. Arms S and J use item-cluster bootstrap, 4,000 draws, which ignores rater sampling variance. Arm M variances are plug-in estimates from all items; its subsampling check uses 2,000 draws. Holm-Bonferroni inside the families named in `PROTOCOL.md`.
5. **How were the judges chosen?** They were not: the five judges are the frozen frontier judges of Experiment 28 (gpt-6-astra, claude-opus-5-5, kimi-k3, glm-5.3, deepseek-v4.1-flash), temperature 0, one verbalized-distribution forecast per item, told to forecast a whole crowd. There is no search space, objective, optimizer or budget in this experiment. Invalid forecasts are replaced by the uniform forecast (the `sensitivity_drop_invalid` run drops those items instead).

Reading the figures. Every axis label names its metric; "(lower is better)" is on every score axis; the title of each figure gives the count that it summarizes (computed from the CSVs). Figures 1 and 2 show the five judges as separate marks, so spread between judges is visible. In Figure 3, binned JSD is plotted against the proper-score risk of the same judge on the same items, and the bottom panel shows the shuffle diagnostic. In Figure 4(a) the minimum detectable difference is the median over the 10 judge pairs of a set.

What a hypothesis label means. "Supported" means the registered criterion in `PROTOCOL.md` (as amended in `AMENDMENT_A1.md`) was met; "not supported" means it was not met, which for the pool arm means no detectable effect at these pool sizes, not no effect.
