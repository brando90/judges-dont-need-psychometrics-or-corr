\subsection{Frontier AI Judges: Native Probabilities, Prompt Search, and Harness Search}
Earlier real-rating mixtures over three sampled scalar votes suffered severe cross-entropy penalties ($5.49$~nats vs.\ $1.93$~nats for a constant training-histogram forecast) because empty sample bins triggered probability-floor penalties.
The native-probability pool of 15 configurations of seven OpenAI and Mistral frontier models has five-fold task-family cross-entropy of $1.416$~nats ($1.416$~nats with validation ranked probability score (RPS) shrinkage) and lowers held-out test RPS over Pearson-trained mixtures by $+0.0775$ $[+0.0437, +0.1135]$.
Across 250 grouped task-family splits, fitting a 48-parameter softmax head by cross-entropy achieves lower held-out test RPS than Pearson training by $+0.0250$ $[+0.0238, +0.0262]$, with positive sampled mean differences from 5 training task families (~51.7 human ratings) onward. The displayed 95\% intervals resample overlapping split averages and quantify conditional Monte Carlo variability, not population uncertainty or a rating threshold.
In discrete single-prompt (\texttt{gpt-6.1-sol} proposer; \texttt{gpt-5.4-mini} evaluator) and four-architecture harness ($H_1$--$H_4$) selection on a five-family test split, validation-selected proper-loss prompts improve raw held-out RPS directionally ($+0.0418$ $[-0.0200, +0.1813]$) while two-step obligation auditing ($H_3$) lowers held-out RPS to $0.114$--$0.117$ across prompt tracks (joint discrete contrast $-0.0060$ $[-0.0493, +0.0613]$).
Saved metrics include parsing failures replaced by uniform forecasts (7/30 held-out artifacts for the selected cross-entropy prompt) and harness scaffold fallbacks. Historical and native pools differ in models and prompts, so their comparison does not isolate probability representation. Reference-file byte identity with the original human-delivered inputs remains unverified.
